## High priority

**Galaxy clusters in the LoTSS-DR3: Catalogues and detection pipeline for diffuse radio emission** · C. Stuardi, G. Di Gennaro, A. Botteon et al. · [arXiv:2605.06400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06400v1)
- Why it matters: A direct hit on CNNs applied to survey imaging — Radio U-Net segmentation over a full LOFAR data release, with a science-grade catalogue product rather than a pure benchmark.
- TL;DR: The authors run a Radio U-Net pixel-segmentation model across 20-arcsec LoTSS-DR3 144 MHz mosaics and fuse its output with positions, masses, and redshifts from four X-ray/SZ cluster catalogues to score 3,822 clusters for diffuse radio emission. The pipeline yields both a per-cluster probability and an interpretable morphology map, and a high-confidence cut isolates 357 clusters at 76% network accuracy where the diffuse-source detection fraction grows with mass and redshift as expected.

## Medium priority

**Constraining Galaxy Cluster Triaxiality via Weak Lensing — I. Preparation for the Rubin Data Beyond Leading Order** · Shenming Fu, Radhakrishnan Srinivasan, Tae-hyeon Shin et al. · [arXiv:2605.06587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06587v1)
- Why it matters: Weak-lensing-shape methodology being readied for Rubin LSST (and applicable to Euclid/Roman), pushing cluster ellipticity stacking past leading order.
- TL;DR: Using DES Y3 redMaPPer + metacalibration + DNF photo-z catalogues, they stack weak-lensing shears around well-centered clusters with major-axis alignment from satellite distributions and extend the multipole/monopole expansion to second order in ellipticity, measuring a projected ellipticity of 0.310 with no significant mass or redshift trend.

**What You Don't Know Won't Hurt You: Self-Consistent Hierarchical Inference with Unknown Follow-up Selection Strategies** · Reed Essick, Amanda M. Farah · [arXiv:2605.06636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06636v1)
- Why it matters: Survey-population inference that doesn't require modelling messy follow-up triggers — relevant for rare-object pipelines and LSST-era population studies where follow-up is uncoordinated.
- TL;DR: Within hierarchical Bayesian inference, the intrinsic population can be recovered without explicitly modelling the follow-up selection function, even when follow-up correlates strongly with latent parameters; worked examples show contaminant populations may need to be modelled when initial detection is imperfect.
