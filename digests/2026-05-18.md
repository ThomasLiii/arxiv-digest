## High priority

**Strong Gravitational Lensing with the James Webb Space Telescope** · Adi Zitrin · [arXiv:2605.15189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15189)
- Why it matters: A topical review tying strong lensing — your top-priority interest — to JWST capabilities, useful background for ML-based lens search work.
- TL;DR: This is a review article walking through how strong gravitational lensing has matured into a workhorse tool for mapping dark matter and observing magnified high-redshift sources. It summarises recent strong-lensing science enabled by JWST and lays out near-term prospects for both lens modelling and the study of magnified background populations.

**Denoising Interferometric Observations Using Visibilities-Informed Neural Networks** · Jason P. Terry, Cassandra Hall, Sergei Gleyzer · [arXiv:2605.14971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14971)
- Why it matters: A CNN method applied to survey-style imaging that bakes the instrument PSF directly into the architecture and loss — relevant to your self-calibration and CNN-on-imaging interests, and applicable to SKA-era data volumes.
- TL;DR: The authors train a U-Net (VIREO) for denoising radio interferometric images by feeding the dirty-beam PSF in as both an additional input channel and a term in the loss, demonstrated on synthetic protoplanetary disc observations. The model beats traditional CLEAN-style cleaning and PSF-agnostic baselines on synthetic data, and produces visibly cleaner ALMA archival images while preserving substructure, suggesting the approach generalises across interferometric facilities.

**Study of dark interactions through strong gravitational lenses** · F. Villalobos, J. Magana, T. Verdugo · [arXiv:2605.16155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16155)
- Why it matters: Uses strong lensing as an independent cosmological probe to test dark-sector physics — sits squarely at the strong-lensing/cosmology intersection.
- TL;DR: The authors fit three sign-changeable dark-matter/dark-energy interaction models by combining strong-lensing constraints from a sample of early-type galaxy lenses with cluster-scale lensing in Abell 1689. They recover non-zero interaction parameters that are larger in magnitude than those previously inferred from SNe/CMB/BAO and prefer an earlier transition to acceleration, arguing that strong lensing offers complementary leverage on the expansion history versus standard probes.

## Medium priority

**Near-IR Weak-lensing (NIRWL) Measurements in the CANDELS Fields. II.** · Kyle Finner, Bomee Lee, Ranga-Ram Chary et al. · [arXiv:2605.15264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15264)
- TL;DR: First NIR weak-lensing mass map of the five CANDELS fields, with 12 shear-selected overdensities validated against X-ray detections — explicitly framed as a precursor to Roman HLWAS weak lensing.

**KiDS+VIKING-450 cosmology with Bayesian hierarchical model redshift distributions** · George T. Kyriacou, Arrykrishna Mootoovaloo, Alan F. Heavens et al. · [arXiv:2605.15078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15078)
- TL;DR: Reanalyses KV450 cosmic shear with a Bayesian hierarchical photo-z model and PCA-reduced SED templates, marginalises over the inferred n(z), and finds the resulting S_8 modestly relaxes the tension with Planck.

**Identifying group galaxies merging with massive clusters using machine learning** · Rhys Jordan, Meghan E. Gray, Alfonso Aragón-Salamanca et al. · [arXiv:2605.14930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14930)
- TL;DR: Supervised classifier trained on cosmological-simulation mocks separates cluster/group/field galaxies from spectroscopic phase-space inputs, outperforming Friends-of-Friends and Gaussian-mixture baselines and aimed at upcoming cluster-infall spectroscopic surveys.

**Analytical method for computing the covariance matrix of cosmic shear two-point correlation function** · Kosuke Nagura, Ryo Terasawa, Taisei Terawaki, Masahiro Takada · [arXiv:2605.16186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16186)
- TL;DR: Compares iNKA, f_sky and weighted quartic-counts analytical covariance estimators for real-space cosmic shear under HSC-Y3-like masks; off-diagonal terms in harmonic space matter and the quartic-counts approach is the most faithful to Gaussian sims.

**No Stream Left Unscathed: The imprint of a host galaxy** · Arpit Arora, Peter S. Ferguson, Jacob Nibauer et al. · [arXiv:2605.16200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16200)
- TL;DR: ~15k synthetic stellar streams in FIRE-2 hosts with no subhalo perturbers still develop spurs, kinks, cocoons, and ~10–25% width variations — a baseline that LSST/Euclid/Roman stream surveys will need before attributing features to dark-matter substructure.

**Clusters Hiding Under Millimeter Sources (CHUMS) I: Extreme CHUMS** · Harshda Saxena, Adam B. Mantz, Jack Sayers et al. · [arXiv:2605.15444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15444)
- TL;DR: Three SZ-selected clusters whose central AGN drown out their SZ decrement in ACT are recovered after CARMA-based AGN subtraction, highlighting an under-recognised completeness/bias issue for SZ cluster cosmology.

**Probing the faint-end of simulated galaxy counts at z>3** · Flaminia Fortuni, Emiliano Merlin, Marco Castellano et al. · [arXiv:2605.15893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15893)
- TL;DR: Forward-models TNG100 and EAGLE through a CANDELS-like pipeline; the faint-end deficit persists past detection limits, with simulations underproducing compact bright-cored galaxies and overproducing diffuse low-SB ones at z>3.

**Dipoles for everyone: the pseudo-C_ell approach to directional stacking** · Lea Harscouet, Amy Wayland, David Alonso · [arXiv:2605.15947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15947)
- TL;DR: Recasts oriented-stacking signals (kSZ, moving lens, filament searches) as cross-spectra between the target field and the E/B modes of the orientation spin field, recovering the information losslessly with the speed and visualisation advantages of power spectra.
