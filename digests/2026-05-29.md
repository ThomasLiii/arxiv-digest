## High priority

**Time-Domain Deep Learning for Pairwise Identification of Strongly Lensed Gravitational-Wave Candidates** · Zhang, Zhang, Yang et al. · [arXiv:2605.29510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29510v1)
- Why it matters: Strong-lensing detection with machine learning is my top interest, and this is a clean ML-based detector — a fast deep-learning prescreen for lensed event pairs that sidesteps expensive Bayesian comparisons.
- TL;DR: The authors cast lensed-pair finding as a binary verification problem and train PI-ResNet, a Siamese 1D residual network with squeeze-and-excitation blocks, directly on whitened time-domain strain rather than spectrogram images. Trained on simulated binary-black-hole signals lensed by point-mass and singular-isothermal-sphere lenses injected into LIGO and Einstein Telescope noise, it reaches ~94–96% accuracy under ET design noise and ~78–84% under simulated LIGO noise. They flag the noise-domain gap as the main obstacle to deployment on real detector data.

## Medium priority

**Augmented Correlation Functions for Spectroscopic Galaxy Surveys** · Bianchi · [arXiv:2605.30305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30305v1)
- Why it matters: A new clustering statistic for spectroscopic galaxy surveys, squarely in the large-scale-structure / clustering interest area.
- TL;DR: A flexible "augmented" two-point statistic adds latent dimensions from transformations of the galaxy field (here the pairwise gradient of the inverse-Laplacian density) and, in Quijote-based Fisher forecasts at z=1, tightens constraints on all cosmological parameters versus the standard correlation function.

**Efficient computation of the galaxy angular bispectrum in redshift space** · Gao, Vlah, Challinor · [arXiv:2605.30246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30246v1)
- Why it matters: A practical, validated tool for higher-order LSS clustering analyses relevant to upcoming galaxy surveys.
- TL;DR: By writing the tree-level unequal-time angular bispectrum as sums of products of angular power spectra, the authors enable a flat-sky approximation that matches full-sky integration to sub-percent accuracy and ship a public Python implementation.
