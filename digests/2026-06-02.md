## High priority (1)

**Signatures of Accreting Black Holes in Line Intensity Mapping** · Ogata, Moriwaki, Lidz et al. · [arXiv:2606.01603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01603)
- Why it matters: Directly assesses what SPHEREx (and a CDIM-like successor) can recover from H α and He II line-intensity mapping once accreting-BH emission is included — concrete forecast for a high-priority mission.
- TL;DR: Using IllustrisTNG, the authors build mock H α and He II intensity maps that include both star formation and BH accretion and find that BHs contribute roughly 40–60% of the mean H α intensity (60–80% for He II) near cosmic noon, dominating the shot-noise power on small scales. They predict SPHEREx can probe the BH-influenced bright tail of the H α voxel intensity distribution at z ≲ 4, while He II's BH-dominated regime is in reach of a future CDIM-like survey. The takeaway is that BH emission has to be folded into LIM forward models, not bolted on as a correction.

## Medium priority (4)

**AstroSkyFlow: an astronomical sky image flow simulator for time domain survey validation and machine learning** · Li, Rui, Feng et al. · [arXiv:2606.02140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02140)
- Why it matters: Provides labeled, high-fidelity simulated survey imagery built specifically to train and stress-test ML pipelines for time-domain optical surveys.
- TL;DR: AstroSkyFlow is a modular simulator that walks the full chain from celestial sources through atmosphere to sensor and produces multi-epoch image stacks with realistic noise; the authors report better PSF and noise fidelity than SkyMaker and demonstrate clean injection-recovery of transit and asteroid-trail signals.

**CSST large-scale structure analysis pipeline: IV. Cosmic Voids Identified from Galaxy Group Samples as Probes of the Large-scale Structure** · Song, Yang, Gong et al. · [arXiv:2606.01907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01907)
- Why it matters: LSS methodology for the CSST survey — sits squarely on the cosmology/large-scale-structure mid-priority axis.
- TL;DR: Using CSST mock catalogs, group-defined voids reproduce halo-void statistics down to ~40% spectroscopic completeness (and even at 30% once a redshift-error term is included), making group-voids a viable LSS probe.

**Accurate inner stellar density slopes from projected surface densities in galaxies** · Sanchez Almeida · [arXiv:2606.01956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01956)
- Why it matters: A purely photometric route to constraining inner DM profiles in dwarfs — relevant to dark-matter cosmology and useful where deprojection ambiguity has historically blocked progress.
- TL;DR: A closed-form expression recovers the central 3D density slope directly from radial derivatives of the surface density, sidestepping deprojection; applied to six ultra-faint dwarfs, all show stellar cores.

**Mergers Matter: Gravothermal Collapse in Dwarf Halos with Self-Interacting Dark Matter** · Silverman, Hussein, Arora et al. · [arXiv:2606.02566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02566)
- Why it matters: Cosmological simulation work that introduces a new mechanism (merger-driven heat transport) for SIDM core evolution, with direct implications for the dwarf-galaxy diversity problem.
- TL;DR: In six zoom-in dwarf halos with σ/m = 70 cm² g⁻¹, only the quietest merger histories show gravothermal core collapse; sustained mergers prevent collapse and can drive halos to densities well below the standard fluid-model prediction.
