## Medium priority

**Supermagnified Stars in Lensing Clusters and Small-Scale Structure in the Dark Matter** · Gabriel Torralba, Jordi Miralda-Escudé · [arXiv:2607.14034v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14034v1)
- Why it matters: Uses strong-lensing microcaustic crossings of highly magnified stars as a small-scale dark-matter probe, directly relevant to strong-lensing science and DM substructure detection.
- TL;DR: The authors argue that intracluster stars corrugate the large-scale caustics of galaxy-cluster lenses into micro-caustics, producing frequent microlensing events on stars magnified by factors of ~1000 near the caustic. The rate and lightcurve shapes of these events are shown to be sensitive to sub-galactic surface-density fluctuations such as dark-matter minihalos, and modeled fits to the Kelly et al. (2018) event are used to argue that Habitable Worlds Observatory photometry could distinguish DM small-scale structure from ordinary intracluster microlensing.

**A Universal Relation Between Primordial Density-Potential Cross-correlation Coefficient and Spin Factor Distribution** · Jun-Sung Moon, Jounghun Lee, Juhan Kim · [arXiv:2607.13971v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13971v1)
- Why it matters: Ties large-scale-structure statistics to an observable galaxy-morphology signal, offering a possible route to constrain early-universe physics from LSS observations without cosmological degeneracies.
- TL;DR: Building on tidal-torque theory, the authors derive an analytic mapping from the initial density–potential cross-correlation coefficient q to the mean and variance of the halo spin factor τ, and confirm the mapping holds across smoothing scales in ΛCDM and wCDM Multiverse simulations. Because prior work has shown the τ distribution can be reconstructed from observed galaxy sizes, this relation would let q — and by extension features of the primordial universe — be read off from LSS-scale galaxy statistics.
