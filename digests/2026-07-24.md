## Medium priority

**Atmosphere mitigation in CMB observations using multi-frequency time-domain component separation** · Julien Tang, Shamik Ghosh, Jacques Delabrouille, et al. · [arXiv:2607.21432v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21432v1)
- Why it matters: Survey self-calibration analogue for CMB — reserves part of the multi-frequency focal plane as atmosphere monitors and does time-domain component separation, replacing a lossy low-frequency filter and reporting orders-of-magnitude gains over classical filter-bin mapmaking on the atmospheric power at 30 ≤ ℓ ≤ 300.
- TL;DR: The authors dedicate detectors on the focal plane to sampling atmospheric emission and derive time-domain templates that can be subtracted from the science channels, rather than filtering it out in Fourier space. They define a multipole-dependent decontamination factor that scales inversely with survey time and show, in simulation, that at large angular scales this pipeline suppresses atmospheric contamination by roughly a factor of 4000 relative to a standard filter-bin baseline.

**Web-Halo Model Peak-Background Split (WHM-PBS): halo bias as a distribution, not a number** · Samuel Brieden, Alexander Tipp · [arXiv:2607.21334v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21334v1)
- Why it matters: Turns large-scale halo bias into a full distribution inherited from cosmic-web environment (sheet → filament → halo), useful as a physically motivated prior for LSS analyses, a stochasticity prediction that maps onto EFT shot-noise parameters, and an assembly-bias framework — all analytic, no assembly tuning.
- TL;DR: Combining Shen-et-al. moving barriers for ellipsoidal collapse with a peak-background split, they derive b(M_h) as a strongly skewed distribution set by the halo's host filament and sheet. They recover tight b₂(b₁) and b₃(b₁) relations with scattered tidal bias, reproduce N-body super-to-sub-Poisson shot noise once halo exclusion is included, and explain the observed bias–concentration inversion near M_h ≈ 1.7×10¹³ h⁻¹ M_☉ without any parameter fit to assembly bias.

**Fast Inference on Astronomical Time Series with Trans-Dimensional Flow Matching Posterior Estimation** · Nina van der Meulen, Tin Hadži Veljković, Daniela Huppenkothen, et al. · [arXiv:2607.21134v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21134v1)
- Why it matters: Transformer-based simulation-based inference for pulse decomposition in fast-transient time series (GRBs, magnetar bursts, FRBs) — an amortized ML alternative to MCMC/nested sampling that also handles trans-dimensional models and observational biases like dead time, relevant to rare-object discovery pipelines on time-domain surveys.
- TL;DR: The paper introduces t-FMPE, a transformer that performs amortized flow-matching posterior estimation over both the number and parameters of pulses in a light curve. On simulated and real FRB and X-ray magnetar burst data, posteriors match MCMC references (verified with classifier two-sample tests) while sampling ~100 posterior points per second in an 80-dimensional parameter space, orders of magnitude faster than traditional samplers.

**Hubble tension: the shape wall** · Miguel A. Sabogal, Luis A. Escamilla, Davide Pedrotti, et al. · [arXiv:2607.21244v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21244v1)
- Why it matters: Nonparametric Gaussian-process reconstruction of E(z) from PantheonPlus and DESI DR2 BAO adds a shape-based limit on late-time Hubble-tension solutions, complementing the well-known BAO normalization wall — an observational constraint on modified late-time expansion histories.

**Accurate Extragalactic Magnetic Fields from Faraday Rotation with Optimal Dispersion Measure Estimators** · Hilay Shah, Mark Krumholz, Naomi McClure-Griffiths, et al. · [arXiv:2607.20985v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20985v1)
- Why it matters: Survey-preparation methodology for SKA-era radio polarimetry — MHD-simulation-calibrated DM estimators of the form DM ∝ EMᵅ N_HIᵝ, with environment-dependent exponents and best-practice recommendations that recover extragalactic magnetic fields to a few tenths of a dex.
