## Medium priority (≤8)

**Characterising the epoch of reionisation using the cross-correlation of the kSZ effect and CMB lensing** · Niall MacCrann, Christopher Cain, Aleksandra Kusiak et al. · [arXiv:2608.04868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04868v1)
- Why it matters: A new CMB-lensing × secondary-anisotropy cross-correlation estimator with concrete SO/CMB-HD forecasts and a foreground-mitigation treatment, directly relevant to weak-lensing / LSS analyses.
- TL;DR: Using AMBER reionization simulations, the authors show that correlating the squared-kSZ field with CMB lensing tracks the reionization midpoint and duration. Forecasts give S/N ≈ 2–3 for a Simons Observatory-like setup at 5 μK-arcmin over 40% of the sky and S/N ≈ 50 for CMB-HD, and the paper works through low-z extragalactic foreground contamination and lensing-induced biases in the kSZ² estimator.

**Above-ground Biomass Estimation with Geospatial Foundation Models** · Ghjulia Sialellia, Linus Scheibenreif, Jan Dirk Wegner et al. · [arXiv:2608.04792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04792v1)
- Why it matters: A carefully controlled benchmark of "frozen-encoder" foundation models vs. distributed embedding products on a regression task from survey imagery — the same deployment question that comes up for AION-1-style models on astronomical surveys.
- TL;DR: The authors benchmark 11 geospatial foundation models on the global AGBD biomass dataset in two flavors: models shipped as weights (evaluated as frozen encoders via PANGAEA) and models shipped as pre-computed embedding products (AlphaEarth Foundations, TESSERA). Frozen encoders underperform a fully supervised SOTA, whereas an MLP on AEF embeddings beats that SOTA and generalizes better in space and time, suggesting embedding-product deployment is currently the stronger route for regression on satellite imagery.
