## Medium priority (≤8)

**JWST MIRI Medium Resolution Spectrometer Point Fixed Pattern Corrections: Cleaner and Higher Signal-to-Noise Spectra of Point Sources** · Gordon & Law · [arXiv:2608.13464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13464v1)
- Why it matters: Sits near my self-calibration interest — builds an empirical, dither-based correction on top of the pipeline to remove fixed-pattern residuals in a major space-mission instrument, and ships a reusable package.
- TL;DR: The authors derive Point Fixed Pattern Corrections for the JWST MIRI MRS from four-point dithered observations of flux-calibration stars and asteroids, remove both narrow and broad instrument artifacts left by the standard pipeline, and demonstrate signal-to-noise gains of several times on high-S/N point-source spectra. A Python package (MRS-PFPC) is released so other observers can apply the corrections to their own MIRI MRS data.

**SNIFFLES I: Intended Emission, Unwanted Emission, and Unintended Radiation from Low-Earth Orbiting Satellites Impacting Radio Astronomy from 1–26 GHz** · Indermuehle & Lourenco · [arXiv:2608.12999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12999v1)
- Why it matters: Quantifies a rapidly-growing survey-imaging systematic for wide-field radio astronomy from operational NGSO constellations — a data-quality problem any current-generation radio survey pipeline will need to handle.
- TL;DR: Using ~376 hours on the Mopra 22 m dish (with ATCA follow-up), the authors tracked 4629 passes of Starlink, OneWeb, Amazon Leo, and Guowang satellites and cataloged 2345 detections at 300+ unique frequencies across L/S/C/X/K bands, including emission inside protected bands (OH at 1613 MHz; 2690.76 and 2700 MHz). Recorded platform-electronics leakage and out-of-band harmonics reach flux densities up to eleven orders of magnitude brighter than typical astronomical sources — saturating radio-astronomy receivers.

**Widefield Arecibo Virgo Extragalactic Survey II: Characterizing the HI properties and environment of the WAVES South region** · Partík, Taylor & Minchin · [arXiv:2608.13411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13411v1)
- Why it matters: A large-area HI survey product tied to environmental studies in a cluster — directly relevant to the survey-data / rare-object side of my interests, including "optically dark" clouds as a discovery target.
- TL;DR: Cataloging the WAVES South 21 cm data with both visual and automated source extraction yields 56 HI detections (median rms 0.8 mJy) — including two gas clouds (one optically dark) and the ALFALFA Virgo 7 complex — and a lower detection fraction than the adjoining VC1 region. Deep stacking of up to 157 spectra reveals no additional signal to 0.080 mJy rms, consistent with WAVES South being a more dynamically evolved cluster environment, with residual HI in a few early-type dwarfs supporting environmental transformation from dIrr to dE.

**Cosmography with DESI-DR1 Cosmic Chronometers: Direct H(z) measurements from Luminous Red Galaxy ages** · Álvarez, Cueli, Haridasu, Moresco et al. · [arXiv:2608.13178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13178v1)
- Why it matters: A model-independent expansion-history measurement built on a headline LSS spectroscopic dataset — the sort of DESI-based cosmology result my medium-priority list covers.
- TL;DR: Cosmic chronometers are constructed by spectroscopically cleaning DESI-DR1 LRGs, stacking them to high S/N, and fitting Lick indices to stellar-population models to get ages. Both a pivotal-redshift cosmographic fit and a direct differential estimate yield H(z) constraints over 0.3 < z < 1.2, with the reddest CC envelope giving H(z ≈ 0.61) = 88.5 (+6.7/−12.6) stat ± 8.1 syst km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, and a full covariance released alongside.

**Forecast for the detectability of patchy hydrogen reionization in WEAVE-QSO measurements of the Lyman-α forest power spectrum at z ≥ 4** · Ma, Bolton, Iršič, Gaikwad et al. · [arXiv:2608.13153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13153v1)
- Why it matters: LSS forecasting tied to an upcoming spectroscopic survey, using realistic pipeline systematics — fits my cosmology/LSS interest.
- TL;DR: Using the Sherwood-relics simulations and a mock WEAVE-QSO configuration in four bins from z = 4.0–4.6, the authors forecast the 1D Lyα forest power spectrum with a covariance that folds in sample size, resolution, noise, continuum placement, metal contamination, and damping-wing effects, and infer that WEAVE-QSO should detect the large-scale relic imprint of patchy hydrogen reionization at ~4.5σ.

**The Most Probable Outer Density Profile from Excursion Set Theory** · Florio & Pavlidou · [arXiv:2608.13347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13347v1)
- Why it matters: Excursion-set methodology behind turnaround-radius measurements as an LSS-based dark energy probe — near my cosmology / LSS interest.
- TL;DR: The authors relax the simplifying assumptions behind an earlier analytic prediction for the outer density profile of clusters, re-derive the mode of the profile from a double-distribution formulation, and validate it against numerical realizations. Comparison to N-body profiles shows the analytic profile diverges from simulations because of the different (Markovian vs. correlated) window functions, arguing that the analytic profile should be used only as a simulation-fitted effective description.

**Automatic detection of fast oscillations of dark matter scalar field and updated cosmological constraints on QCDM** · Aboubrahim & Nath · [arXiv:2608.13346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13346v1)
- Why it matters: A generalizable numerical fix for a common Boltzmann-code bottleneck in scalar-field dark-matter models, with updated cosmological constraints tying it back to observations.
- TL;DR: The authors implement an averaging scheme in CLASS that automatically detects the onset of fast Klein-Gordon oscillations of a scalar-field dark matter component, so scans over parameter space no longer need hand-tuned switch-over redshifts. It works for both non-interacting and interacting DM scenarios and is used to update the cosmological constraints on the QCDM model with the latest public data.

**Optical concept model of the future cosmology project BISOU** · Loquet Le Gall, Maffei, Guiot, O'Sullivan et al. · [arXiv:2608.13257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13257v1)
- Why it matters: Instrument concept for a next-generation CMB-spectral-distortion mission — a cosmology-mission methods paper, adjacent to my Euclid/SPHEREx interest even though the target signal is different.
- TL;DR: The authors present ray-tracing plus Gaussian-beam and physical-optics analyses for the BISOU balloon-borne pathfinder, a differential polarizing Fourier Transform Spectrometer fed by a sky-facing telescope and internal calibration source. The design uses two bolometric focal planes with multimode feed horns, split into low (90–300 GHz) and high (0.3–1.5 THz) bands, ahead of a full space mission for CMB spectral distortions.
