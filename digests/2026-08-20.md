## Medium priority (≤8)
**Automatically distinguishing Rubin transients from AGN using variability metrics** · Magill et al. · [arXiv:2608.18823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18823v1)
- Why it matters: A practical, ML-free purity/completeness lever for LSST alert triage that directly addresses rare-transient discovery contamination — exactly the alert-stream tooling I care about.
- TL;DR: Testing simple photometric variability cuts on ZTF data and the MALLORN LSST simulation, the authors find that a two-dimensional cut on (detection flux)/(pre-detection std) versus (detection flux)/(pre-detection mean) most effectively separates SNe/TDEs from AGN variability, with both quantities available directly in LSST alert packets so the cut can run inline on the broker stream.

**Dipolar power asymmetry in wide-angle correlations of galaxy density, velocity and ellipticity** · Mikura et al. · [arXiv:2608.19039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19039v1)
- Why it matters: Establishes when the plane-parallel approximation breaks for weak-lensing/clustering statistics — a prerequisite for using future wide-area surveys to test the CMB dipolar-asymmetry anomaly.
- TL;DR: The authors derive a full-sky formalism for two-point correlations of galaxy density, velocity, and ellipticity under a position-dependent dipolar modulation of the primordial power spectrum, and show numerically that wide-angle corrections become non-negligible for angular separations larger than roughly 30°, forcing analyses of the anomaly with next-generation LSS surveys to move beyond plane-parallel expressions.

**Towards end-to-end Bayesian forward models in global 21-cm cosmology: surrogate modelling and marginalisation of beam uncertainty** · Tutt et al. · [arXiv:2608.18962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18962v1)
- Why it matters: A differentiable surrogate + analytic marginalisation recipe for propagating a slow physical instrument model into a cosmological posterior — the same shape of problem that shows up whenever you fold PSF/beam uncertainty into a survey-scale inference.
- TL;DR: The authors build a two-stage decomposition of simulated antenna directivity patterns into a compact, linear surrogate whose continuous nuisance parameters can be analytically marginalised inside a differentiable Bayesian pipeline, recovering true beams and signals at the instrumental-noise level while showing that ~100 electromagnetic simulations already suffice to control chromatic-beam bias in global 21-cm inference.

**Inferring the Dark from the Observable: Estimating Halo Masses Using Galaxy Properties** · Chen & Ahad · [arXiv:2608.19154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19154v1)
- Why it matters: A first-cut ML mapping from survey observables to host halo mass is the kind of population-level regressor that could be dropped downstream of LSST/Euclid catalogues and used as an anchor for weak-lensing and clustering analyses.
- TL;DR: Applying random forests, OLS, and symbolic regression to central- and satellite-galaxy observables, the study finds that gas mass, stellar mass, and r-band magnitude carry the most halo-mass information, and that adding colour and magnitude beats standard stellar-to-halo mass relations — evidence that halo mass has a multi-dimensional dependence on directly observable properties.
