## High priority (≤5)

**Strong Lensing Cosmology with Population-level Calibrated Neural Ratio Estimation** · Sreevani Jarugula et al. · [arXiv:2608.23534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23534v1)
- Why it matters: Directly hits the high-priority intersection of ML and strong-lensing cosmology, targeting the ~10^5-lens regime Euclid and LSST will deliver.
- TL;DR: The authors train a Neural Ratio Estimator that ingests galaxy-galaxy lensing images together with lens/source spectroscopy and returns per-system likelihood ratios that combine into population posteriors on the dark-energy equation of state w and Ω_m. They add a post-hoc coverage calibration step to fix the overconfidence typical of neural density estimators, and show that with 100 simulated ΛCDM lenses their calibrated model recovers w to ~23% and Ω_m to ~3% fractional uncertainty.

## Medium priority (≤8)

**Observational Evidence for the Kinematic Memory of Cosmic Filaments from Satellite Orbital Orientations** · Peng Wang et al. · [arXiv:2608.22726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22726v1)
- Why it matters: An LSS-cosmic-web observational test of anisotropic accretion into haloes, useful context for interpreting alignment signals in weak-lensing and clustering pipelines.
- TL;DR: Cross-matching SDSS DR12 satellite systems with the Bisous filament catalogue, the authors find a 12.8σ preference for satellite orbital planes to lie parallel to the nearest filament axis, with the alignment strengthening near the spine and for massive hosts and flipping to perpendicular at intermediate radii — consistent with a two-phase filament-fed accretion picture.
