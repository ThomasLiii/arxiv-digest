## High priority (≤5)

**Reducing False Positives in Strong-Lens Searches with Generalized-Mean Consensus of Machine-Learning Ensembles in the Kilo-Degree Survey** · Ziqi Li, Rui Li, Xu Huang et al. · [arXiv:2609.24891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24891)
- Why it matters: Directly on your top interest — strong-lens detection with ML — and tackles the exact operational bottleneck (purity/false-positive rate) that limits Euclid/LSST-era lens searches.
- TL;DR: The authors combine several ML lens classifiers on KiDS by taking a generalized-mean of their scores instead of a plain vote, tuning the mean's exponent to trade recall against purity. On held-out KiDS data the ensemble consensus keeps most true lenses while sharply cutting the contaminant load that dominates human-inspection queues, and they show it is competitive with, and complementary to, single strong classifiers.

## Medium priority (≤8)

**Galaxy evolution in the post-merger regime. V — Atomic gas evolution traced by ALFALFA stacks** · Sara L. Ellison, Manasvee Saraf, Leonardo Ferreira et al. · [arXiv:2609.24925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24925)
- The MUMMI machine-vision pipeline is extended to DECaLS imaging to identify mergers and estimate time-since-coalescence, then combined with ALFALFA HI stacks to trace atomic-gas evolution across the merger sequence — a concrete example of CNN vision applied to a wide-field survey feeding downstream science.

**Merlin: Fast and flexible 3x2pt cosmology with simulation-based inference** · Alexandra Wernersson, Guillermo Franco-Abellán, Guadalupe Cañas-Herrera · [arXiv:2609.24946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24946)
- An SBI pipeline (marginal neural ratio estimation over cosmic shear, clustering, and their cross-correlation) built for Stage-IV weak-lensing analyses like Euclid, avoiding Gaussian-likelihood assumptions in 3x2pt inference.

**Two-dimensional weak lensing shear for cluster mass, concentration, ellipticity and miscentering estimation** · Calum Murray, Martin Kilbinger, Constantin Payerne · [arXiv:2609.24948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24948)
- Forward-models the full 2D reduced-shear map around clusters rather than azimuthally averaging to 1D, jointly recovering mass, concentration, ellipticity, and miscentering with reduced degeneracies for cluster weak-lensing pipelines.

**Fast and accurate astronomical source deblending with Density-Peak Clustering** · M. D. Lepinzan, F. Tomba, E. Romelli et al. · [arXiv:2609.24318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24318)
- Redesigns the Advanced Density-Peak clustering algorithm for source deblending at survey scale, targeting the blending regime where Rubin/Euclid depths dominate the error budget for photometry and morphology.

**Scale-Vector Alignment: A Scale-Aware Framework for Spatially Resolved Morphological Similarity in Astronomical Images** · Mengke Zhao, Guang-Xing Li, Keping Qiu et al. · [arXiv:2609.24304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24304)
- Uses Constrained Diffusion Decomposition to break astronomical images into scale components and defines a per-pixel, per-scale similarity between different tracer maps — a tool for cross-tracer / multi-band morphology comparison in survey imaging.

**EPOCHS-DR2 I: Expanded data release and $6.5<z<16.5$ photometrically selected galaxies across ~700 arcmin² of JWST/NIRCam deep fields** · Duncan Austin, Christopher J. Conselice, Thomas Harvey et al. · [arXiv:2609.24987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24987)
- Public catalog of 2,452 high-z candidates from the galfind photometric pipeline across the main JWST/NIRCam blank-field surveys, with a photo-z vs. spec-z comparison on 293 confirmations — useful benchmark for photo-z methods on JWST imaging.

**Sound-Horizon-Independent Test of the Cosmic Distance Duality Relation Using Artificial Neural Networks and Gaussian Processes** · Bo-Hao Jiang, Guo-Jian Wang, Tao Yang · [arXiv:2609.24203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24203)
- ANNs and Gaussian processes are used to non-parametrically reconstruct $D_L$ and $D_A$ over $0<z<2.5$ and test the distance-duality relation without assuming a sound horizon — a small example of ML-based reconstruction in observational cosmology.

**Natural coordinates for constrained correlation functions: partial autocorrelations and the geometry of positive power spectra** · Thomas Erben · [arXiv:2609.24500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24500)
- Reparameterizes two-point correlation coefficients using partial autocorrelations so that non-negativity of the underlying power spectrum becomes an unconstrained box — potentially useful for priors and compression in cosmic-shear / LSS likelihoods.
