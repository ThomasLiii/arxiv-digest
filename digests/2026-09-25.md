## High priority (≤5)

**The shell around VY Canis Majoris in SPHEREx is likely an artifact** · Viraj Karambelkar, Yashvi Sharma, Kishalay De · [arXiv:2609.28612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28612)
- Why it matters: Direct SPHEREx methods work — a public rebuttal of a recently claimed astrophysical structure that turns out to be an instrumental effect.
- TL;DR: A recently reported large infrared shell around the hypergiant VY Canis Majoris in SPHEREx imagery is likely a detector artifact rather than a real feature. The authors find matching shell-like structures at consistent positions around other bright stars in SPHEREx Band 1, undermining the astrophysical interpretation.

**CERIDWEN: Fast and Flexible GPU-Accelerated Stellar Population Inference** · Amanda Stoffers, Sandro Tacchella, Benjamin D. Johnson · [arXiv:2609.30145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30145)
- Why it matters: A methods paper explicitly aimed at the Euclid / Rubin / Roman scale-up in galaxy samples, tackling the SED-fitting bottleneck that will otherwise gate wide-survey stellar-population science.
- TL;DR: CERIDWEN is a GPU-native, JAX-based, end-to-end differentiable SED-fitting pipeline that runs full Bayesian nested sampling with gradients in roughly four minutes per fit — about 130× faster than a CPU Prospector run — and reproduces Prospector posteriors on real JWST data. It supports non-parametric star-formation histories with ~120 age bins, time-dependent chemical enrichment, and joint α/Fe–Fe/H sampling, making richer stellar-population models tractable at survey volume.

## Medium priority (≤8)

**Wave-Optics Imprints of Warm Dark Matter Subhalos with Prompt Cusps on Strongly Lensed Gravitational Waves** · Bhashin A. Thakore, Shin'ichiro Ando · [arXiv:2609.28626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28626)
- TL;DR: A strong-lensing wave-optics framework extended to warm dark matter shows that percent-level amplitude modulations in LISA-band signals from strongly lensed massive-black-hole binaries can probe WDM particle mass only in the ~20–40 keV window, and cannot resolve the inner prompt-cusp profile.

**PSFSim: PhySics First Simulations for the Point Spread Function of the Roman Space Telescope** · Nihar Dalal, Chris Hirata, Charuhas Shiveshwarkar et al. (Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team) · [arXiv:2609.28821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28821)
- TL;DR: A raytrace-based, detector-aware PSF simulator for the Roman WFI that supports optical/detector perturbation studies relevant to commissioning tasks such as optical-ghost characterization, aberration inference from diffraction spikes, and mirror-polarization effects.

**Spatial segregation as the origin of anisotropic satellite quenching in haloes and beyond** · Zhuoming Zhang, Weiguang Cui, Yun Chen et al. · [arXiv:2609.29739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29739)
- TL;DR: Using SIMBA, TNG100, EAGLE, and a feedback-free SIMBA run, the authors argue that the observed alignment of quenched satellites with central major axes is set by spatial segregation of quenched and unquenched populations rather than AGN feedback, and extends outside halos through filamentary environment anisotropy.

**Simultaneous PSF Matching via Dual Kernel Optimization** · Carter Lee Rhea, Pieter Van Dokkum, Roberto Abraham et al. · [arXiv:2609.29860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29860)
- TL;DR: Dual Kernel Optimization jointly solves for a pair of convolution kernels — one per image — so that two mismatched PSFs are homogenized at minimum combined kernel size, using stochastic-gradient Langevin dynamics to navigate the degenerate solution space.

**Differential Double Blind Fourier Holography (diff-DBFH)** · Oren Pedatzur, David T. Chuss · [arXiv:2609.30183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30183)
- TL;DR: A two-image variant of Double Blind Fourier Holography introduces a small pupil-plane amplitude perturbation to sharpen focal-plane wavefront estimation on segmented-aperture space telescopes, benchmarked against differential OTF on a Habitable Worlds Observatory-inspired geometry in simulation and on an optical bench.

**Statistics of large-scale structure from the transition probability of Lagrangian trajectories** · Lawrence Dam · [arXiv:2609.30251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30251)
- TL;DR: The two-point function of gravitational clustering is recast in configuration space as a Lagrangian transition probability that broadens diffusively with time, giving a natural interpretation of the effective large-separation suppression usually described as infrared resummation, e.g. around the BAO scale.
