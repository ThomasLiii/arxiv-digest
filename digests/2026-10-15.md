## High priority (≤5)

**Strong gravitational lensing with Euclid** · Sonnenfeld · [arXiv:2610.19835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19835v1)
- Why it matters: A direct intersection of two top interests — Euclid mission science and strong lensing — forecasting how the imminent 30× jump in known lens systems will reshape the field.
- TL;DR: Euclid is expected to add roughly 10⁵ new strong lenses to a known population of a few thousand, with the first quick data release already yielding hundreds of systems ranging from isolated ellipticals to edge-on disks and galaxy clusters. The paper surveys what this growing haul enables, from double-source-plane lenses (four already found) to prospective detections of dwarf-satellite deflectors and systems with many images, foregrounding the discovery space Euclid is about to open.

**Cosmology with strong gravitational lenses** · Treu · [arXiv:2610.19598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19598v1)
- Why it matters: Review of strong-lensing cosmology framing exactly the Euclid/Rubin/Roman/SKA-era discovery pipeline — relevant both to the strong-lensing-detection interest and to downstream science motivation for ML lens finders.
- TL;DR: The review summarises how time-delay and multiplane lenses constrain H₀ and the dark-energy equation of state, while image distortions probe dark-matter subhalo abundance and internal structure. Recent highlights include a 3%-precision H₀ measurement under flat ΛCDM and a tentative dark-matter self-interaction detection, with forthcoming wide-field optical and radio surveys poised to grow lens samples by orders of magnitude and make strong lensing a central cosmological probe.

**RPCA: a Resolved PSF reconstruction method for undersampled images** · Nie, Shan, Li et al. · [arXiv:2610.19909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19909v1)
- Why it matters: PSF modelling is a core weak-lensing/photo-z systematic for Stage-IV survey imaging, and this method is targeted at CSST — adjacent methodology to anything that will matter for Euclid and Roman.
- TL;DR: RPCA uses an Expectation-Maximisation PCA that explicitly accounts for pixel integration to recover high-resolution PSFs directly from undersampled star stamps. On simulated CSST data it drives the ΔR²/R² bias down by roughly an order of magnitude relative to MCCD, RCA and PSFEx and holds up under realistic SNRs, cosmic-ray hits and bad pixels, positioning it as a candidate PSF pipeline for CSST and similar space-based surveys.

**Low-cost neural summaries for cosmological inference: an ensemble of convolutional neural networks trained on approximate simulations** · Sáez-Casares, Bianchi, Calabrese et al. · [arXiv:2610.19891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19891v1)
- Why it matters: A concrete recipe for training CNN summary statistics on cheap approximate simulations and transferring them to higher-fidelity data — a transfer-learning pattern directly transferable to foundation-model-style survey ML.
- TL;DR: The authors compress particle-mesh matter density fields with an ensemble of CNN architectures trained on fast low-resolution simulations, then build a summary statistic from a greedy posterior-volume-selected subset of networks and emulate theory with a Gaussian process. Applied to simulations ~1000× more expensive than the training set and to independent N-body halo catalogues at multiple redshifts and densities, the ensemble tightens Ωₘ–σ₈ constraints beyond the binned power spectrum, while any single network often underperforms the power spectrum.

**Microcaustics: Fast GPU-Accelerated Dynamical Microlensing of Time-Dependent Extended Sources** · Fagin, Stone, Chan et al. · [arXiv:2610.19703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19703v1)
- Why it matters: Microlensing is both a probe in its own right and a key systematic in strong-lens time-delay H₀ cosmography; tooling that makes fully dynamical microlensing tractable enables simulation-based inference pipelines across the strong-lensing stack.
- TL;DR: Microcaustics is a PyTorch package combining local-exact / far-field deflection, source-directed lens-plane scouting with temporal reuse, and biquadratic inverse polygon mapping, fused with Triton rasterisation on GPU. It produces a 10-year 147-frame multiband light curve for a quad-lensed quasar in ~0.7 s with sub-mmag RMSE, roughly 780× faster than a recent CUDA IPM implementation, and ships GPU-resident GR and reverberating quasar source models plus support for evolving sources such as supernovae.

## Medium priority (≤8)

**A heterogeneous set of unusual quasars selected from astrometry and broad-band photometry** · Cheng, Valeckas, Christensen et al. · [arXiv:2610.19838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19838v1)
- Why it matters: Rare-object discovery using Gaia astrometry and SPHEREx redshifts — overlapping both the SPHEREx and anomaly/rare-object interests.
- TL;DR: Combining zero-parallax/proper-motion Gaia pre-selection with wide-field photometric colour-colour diagrams and SPHEREx-anchored redshifts identifies 21 unusual quasars (0.14 < z < 3.73) from 24 spectroscopic follow-ups, heavily populated by broad-absorption-line systems and including a low-redshift Little-Red-Dot analogue.

**Identifying Planetary Nebulae Candidates from IPHAS and VPHAS+ Surveys via YOLO Deep Learning Models** · Sérandour, Méalier, Colicchio et al. · [arXiv:2610.19760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19760v1)
- Why it matters: A CNN detector running on narrowband+broadband survey imaging across thousands of square degrees — the "CNNs applied to survey imaging" pattern in rare-object mode.
- TL;DR: A YOLOv12m detector trained on ~2600 HASH-labelled PNe in r/i/Hα HiPS tiles achieves 98.7% precision and 97.4% recall at IoU ≥1%, deploys over the full ~3755 deg² IPHAS/VPHAS+ coverage, matches or beats a recent Swin Transformer baseline, but still struggles to separate PNe from H II regions in dense Galactic fields.

**Joint Line Intensity Recovery from Multi-Tracer Cross-Correlations** · Sierra, Karkare · [arXiv:2610.20467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20467v1)
- Why it matters: Multi-tracer LIM × LSST photometric galaxies — relevant to large-scale-structure analysis and photo-z-driven cross-correlation workflows.
- TL;DR: A joint auto/cross power-spectrum likelihood for an SPT-SLIM-like 125–175 GHz LIM survey plus an LSST Y1-like photo-z catalogue breaks line-blending degeneracies, recovering CO(2–1) through CO(6–5) and [CI](1–0) over 0.3 ≲ z ≲ 4 and tightening line constraints by factors of ~3–4 where galaxies overlap and 20–50% beyond, where the external tracer only indirectly constrains low-z interlopers.

**Binary-lens amplification of starlight: from magnification flares to quasi-periodic tracking of orbital evolution** · Wang, Kocsis, Zumalacárregui · [arXiv:2610.20557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20557v1)
- Why it matters: Strong-lensing phenomenology from supermassive black-hole binaries — a new transient class for time-domain lens search pipelines.
- TL;DR: Generalising earlier BLAST work, the authors catalogue the magnification signatures a sub-parsec SMBHB imprints on background starlight — single or double-peaked caustic-crossing flares in the high-proper-motion limit, and quasi-periodic lensed-starlight tracking the binary orbit in the low-proper-motion limit — with magnifications up to ~10⁶ and template-ready amplitude/period/Fourier predictions.

**Testing Departures from the ΛCDM model in f(T) Gravity with DESI DR2** · Zheng, Wang, Zhang · [arXiv:2610.20002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20002v1)
- Why it matters: Follow-up on recent DESI-driven hints of departures from ΛCDM — directly in the LSS/cosmology track.
- TL;DR: Fitting three representative f(T) models (power-law, exponential, hyperbolic-tangent) to DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + Pantheon+ SNe shows parameter shifts whose statistical weight depends sensitively on the data combination, cautioning against reading dynamical-dark-energy signals from any single dataset mix.

**Galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity has a limited impact on reionization and the 21-cm signal at fixed mean emissivity** · Zhu, Qin · [arXiv:2610.20184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20184v1)
- Why it matters: Clarifies which astrophysical scatter actually matters for reionization/21-cm forecasts — context for upcoming LSS and intensity-mapping cross-analyses.
- TL;DR: Using the Meraxes semi-analytic model, the authors show that most apparent effects of galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity on reionization timing and the 21-cm signal are driven by shifts in mean emissivity rather than redistribution at fixed emissivity, with the latter leaving only modest, scale- and redshift-limited residuals once the mean is recalibrated.
