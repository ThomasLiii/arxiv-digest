## High priority (≤5)

**Evaluating Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection for Identifying Unusual Galaxy Morphologies in CEERS and NGDEEP** · Surakan, Kartaltepe, Pattnaik et al. · [arXiv:2610.29868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29868v1)
- Why it matters: Direct hit on multiple high-priority buckets at once — a CLIP-based foundation model applied to deep JWST survey imaging to flag rare/unusual galaxy morphologies without labels, exactly the anomaly-detection and foundation-model-for-imaging combination you've been tracking (and the paper explicitly points at Roman as the next target).
- TL;DR: The authors run AdaCLIP, a zero-shot anomaly detector built on CLIP and fine-tuned on non-astronomical anomaly datasets, over 38,853 galaxies in CEERS and NGDEEP and compare its anomaly scores and 768-dim embeddings against parametric (GALFIT) and nonparametric (statmorph) morphology measurements. Embeddings track morphological and observational structure; anomaly scores correlate with asymmetry, concentration, M20, and size, though less so with Sérsic index or Gini. The result is a label-free, survey-agnostic path to rare-object discovery that should scale to Roman-era datasets.

**Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Data processing for intracluster light measurements** · Euclid Collaboration, Bamford, Gao et al. · [arXiv:2610.29682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29682v1)
- Why it matters: The methods backbone for the Euclid ICL series — exactly the kind of large-scale background modeling and low-surface-brightness post-processing that overlaps with self-calibration and mosaicking concerns, and it validates what Euclid imaging can actually deliver at the faint end.
- TL;DR: The paper documents a post-processing pipeline that suppresses background variations without resorting to small-scale subtraction, preserving diffuse faint signal. Simulated-cluster injection into real Euclid frames shows reliable surface-brightness measurements down to ~27.5 mag/arcsec² in Y/J/H and ~29 in I, with ~10% precision in surface brightness, ~10° in position angle, and 0.1 in ellipticity out to z~0.8. The authors argue Euclid can now routinely resolve ICL in individual clusters, enabling the companion science papers.

**Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). A systematic study of the intracluster light fraction at 0.2 < z < 1.5** · Euclid Collaboration, Gully, Hatch et al. · [arXiv:2610.29684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29684v1)
- Why it matters: A flagship Euclid Q1 result demonstrating that the Wide Survey can detect ICL in individual massive clusters out to z~1.5, pinning down what Euclid imaging can and cannot do at low surface brightness.
- TL;DR: Combining SPT (SZ-selected) and Euclid-selected (optical) clusters with Hydrangea simulations, the team measures BCG+ICL at 50–200 kpc for ~300 clusters. ICL is detected in 70–80% of massive clusters out to z~1.5, contributes ~40% of BCG+ICL light and ~20% of total cluster stellar light, and these fractions stay roughly constant with redshift at fixed mass. The partitioning between BCG, ICL, and satellites looks mostly established by z~1.5, consistent with co-assembly via accretion and tidal stripping of ~10¹⁰ M☉ galaxies.

**Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). The shape of cluster haloes revealed by intracluster light** · Euclid Collaboration, Kolcu, Hatch et al. · [arXiv:2610.29683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29683v1)
- Why it matters: Uses Euclid Q1 imaging to recover halo shapes from diffuse light and finds consistency with strong+weak lensing-based halo ellipticities — directly relevant to using Euclid imaging as a complement to lensing-based halo characterization.
- TL;DR: For 205 eROSITA/DES clusters at 0.1 ≤ z ≤ 0.8, ICL shape parameters agree across Euclid's five bands. The ellipticity distribution peaks near e ≈ 0.5, matching lensing-inferred halo shapes. Cores are rounder (e ~ 0.2) and the light elongates outward to e ~ 0.5 by ~0.1 R_200c. ICL position angles align closely with the BCG (87% within 30°) but less with satellites, and ICL yields usable PAs for roughly twice as many clusters as satellite-based measurements, suggesting ICL is a more robust tracer of large-scale cluster geometry.

**Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). The metallicity of the intracluster light** · Euclid Collaboration, Golden-Marx, Hatch et al. · [arXiv:2610.29686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29686v1)
- Why it matters: First statistical ICL metallicity measurement at large radii enabled by Euclid NIR imaging — a worked example of what Euclid's filter set unlocks beyond photometry.
- TL;DR: Using stacked YE−HE, JE−HE, and IE−HE colour profiles for 168 redMaPPer clusters at 0.1<z<0.75 within Euclid Q1, the team measures ICL colour out to ~300 kpc and finds a blueward gradient from BCG to ICL. SPS modeling shows NIR colours track metallicity rather than age at these ranges; the BCG is super-solar ([Z/Z☉] > 0.1) while the ICL is metal-poor ([Z/Z☉] ≈ −0.5), supporting a formation picture in which the BCG grows by massive-galaxy mergers while the ICL is built up from tidal stripping of lower-metallicity stars.

## Medium priority (≤8)

**Data-driven characterisation of variable source light curves with multimodal representation learning** · Madarász, Marton, Audard · [arXiv:2610.29412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29412v1)
- Why it matters: Label-free representation learning on ZTF/LSST-style survey time-series using a two-view Transformer + CNN architecture — close analogue of the imaging-domain foundation-model work, with an eye on cross-survey transfer.

**Optimal galaxy magnification: generalized fundamental-plane residuals and number counts are all you need** · Xu, Sheth · [arXiv:2610.30133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30133v1)
- Why it matters: Clean theoretical result that weak-lensing magnification Fisher information compresses into GFP residuals plus number counts under Poisson statistics, with practical implications for pipelines beyond cosmic shear.

**Constraining Dynamical Dark Energy with Tomographic Galaxy and CMB Weak Lensing Through Higher-Order Statistics** · Chen, Hill, Haiman · [arXiv:2610.29937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29937v1)
- Why it matters: Non-Gaussian summaries (Minkowski functionals, peaks/minima, wavelet scattering transform) on Rubin×SO-like simulations tighten w0/wa by ~25–28% over the power spectrum alone, pointing at the practical payoff of higher-order statistics for upcoming cosmic shear.

**HETDEX [OII] galaxies at z ≤ 0.48: Growth of structures from power spectrum multipoles** · Moon, Komatsu, Ciardullo et al. · [arXiv:2610.29591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29591v1)
- Why it matters: Establishes HETDEX [OII] clustering as a competitive low-z growth-of-structure probe, with the growth index γ consistent with GR at the ~0.05 level.

**νGundam: a unified framework for point-redshift and PDF-aware spatial correlation functions** · Donoso, Lopez Cortez, Sureshkumar et al. · [arXiv:2610.29116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29116v1)
- Why it matters: Practical, benchmarked tooling for propagating full photometric-redshift PDFs through pair-count estimators, validated against DESI and CosmoDC2 — directly relevant to photo-z-aware clustering analyses.
