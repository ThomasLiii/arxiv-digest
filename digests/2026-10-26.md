## High priority

### [Complete Multi-Band Structural Characterization for 10 Million Galaxies in the Hyper Suprime-Cam Wide Survey using Deep Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32913)

Milind Sarkar, Aritra Ghosh, Andrew Connolly, C. Megan Urry et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32913)

**Why:** CNNs on survey imaging: Bayesian GaMPEN gives calibrated multi-band bulge/disk posteriors for 10M HSC galaxies, aimed next at Euclid and Rubin.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Galaxy structure is wavelength-dependent, as different photometric bands trace distinct stellar populations and structural components. We present per-galaxy Bayesian posterior distributions of the bulge-to-total light ratio ($L_B/L_T$), effective radius ($R_e$), and flux ($F$) in the $g$, $r$, and $i$ bands for nearly $10$ million galaxies across $\sim$1400\,deg$^2$ of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP) Wide survey at $z \leq 0.75$ and $m < 23$. To our knowledge, this is the largest multi-band catalog of bulge--disk structural parameters with per-galaxy posteriors. $\sim$3.9 million of the above galaxies have full multi-band coverage. Measurements are generated using the Galaxy Morphology Posterior Estimation Network (\gampen{}), a Bayesian deep-learning framework. We demonstrate that we can adapt \gampen{} from previously-trained single-band models to the new bands by using transfer learning on only $\sim$0.5\% of our dataset. Despite this extremely small training set, \gampen{} successfully reproduces light-profile fitting measurements and its output posteriors are extremely well-calibrated with $\lesssim6\%$ mean deviation. This approach offers a scalable pathway to structural parameter measurements with robust uncertainties for Rubin, Euclid, and Roman data, which we will pursue in forthcoming work. While structural measurements remain robust across most bands and redshift ranges, performance degrades significantly for the $g$-band at $z \gtrsim 0.5$, where it increasingly probes rest-frame ultraviolet emission; these measurements are therefore excluded from the public release. This public catalog will enable large-scale statistical studies of morphological color gradients, wavelength-dependent galaxy structure, and galaxy evolution.

</details>

## Medium priority

### [AMICO galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000: Cosmological constraints from the two-point correlation function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31767)

Massimiliano Romanello, Giorgio Francesco Lesci, Federico Marulli, Lauro Moscardini et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31767)

**Why:** Cluster clustering cosmology: two-point correlation multipoles of 9049 KiDS-1000 AMICO clusters give improved Omega_m and S8 constraints.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In this paper we study the clustering properties of an optically identified photometric galaxy cluster catalogue from KiDS-1000. The sample was identified by the AMICO algorithm and consists of 9049 clusters with $S/N\geq3.5$, in the redshift range $0.1\leq z<0.8$, spread over an effective area of 838.78 deg$^2$. Clusters are selected according to their observed intrinsic richness, with thresholds of $\lambda^*{obs}\geq 20,25,30$ within $0.10\leq z<0.30$, $0.30\leq z<0.45$ and $0.45\leq z<0.80$, respectively. We measure the multipoles of the two-point correlation function over different ranges of scales from 5 $h^{-1}$ Mpc to 150 $h^{-1}$ Mpc. We model the clustering signal in the context of the $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, accounting for the main observational effects, such RSDs and photo-$z$ uncertainties. The effective bias of the cluster sample is estimated via a mass-richness scaling relation. For the MCMC analysis, we adopt Gaussian priors on the parameters of the mass-richness scaling relation, and broad, uniform priors to derive cosmological constraints on the cold matter density parameter, the power spectrum amplitude and the structure growth parameter, $S_8\equiv\sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega{m}/0.3}$. Specifically, we find $\Omega_{m}=0.24^{+0.02}{-0.02}$ and $\sigma_8=0.86^{+0.13}{-0.10}$ representing an improvement in precision over both the 2D and 3D AMICO clustering analyses performed on the previous KiDS data release. Moreover, we obtain $S_8=0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ in $1\sigma$ agreement with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts constraints estimated from the same catalogue, as well as with some results from external early- and late-Universe data sets. Our work strengthens the potential of cluster clustering as a complementary and competitive probe, to be included and further developed with the final KiDS-Legacy data release, and in other future Stage-IV photometric redshift surveys.

</details>
