## High priority

### [Polar-structure galaxies beyond the local Universe: first results from Euclid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34049)

Jacob A. Guerrette, Aleksandr V. Mosenkov, Seneca K. H. Bahr, Isaac H. Jensen et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34049)

**Why:** Euclid Q1 VIS search finds ~100 high-redshift polar-structure galaxy candidates; Euclid science and rare-object discovery in survey imaging.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Galaxies hosting strongly misaligned stellar and gas structures offer a direct window into external accretion and the buildup of angular momentum through multiple spin components. Such polar-structure systems are intrinsically rare and, until now, have been identified almost entirely at low redshift. The Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1), with its uniform, space-based VIS imaging, provides an unprecedented opportunity to locate these objects over wide areas and at much greater distances. We present the first visual search for polar-structure galaxy (PSG) candidates in Euclid VIS data, spanning approximately 10% of the Q1 survey footprint. This search yields the largest high-redshift PSG candidate sample assembled to date, including roughly 100 high-confidence systems with over 50 at $z \gtrsim 0.3$, along with additional populations of face-on rings and polar tidal structures (PTS). Robust candidates are detected out to redshifts of $z\sim1$, demonstrating that polar structures can be identified well beyond the local Universe. Although the present sample is not yet sufficient for quantitative occurrence rate or evolutionary measurements, it enables initial classification and basic structural characterization of PSGs at intermediate redshift. These results lay the groundwork for future statistical studies with forthcoming Euclid data releases and illustrate Euclid's power for rare-object science across cosmic time.

</details>

## Medium priority

### [Photometric Redshift Estimation with Hybrid Quantum Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35196)

Giuseppe Sarracino, Vincenzo Fabrizio Cardone, Irene Graziotti, Crescenzo Tortora et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35196)

**Why:** Hybrid quantum neural network for KiDS+VIKING photometric redshifts, compared against a classical network.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A Hybrid Quantum Neural Network (HQNN) is applied to recover the photometric redshift of a galaxy dataset built using data from two astronomical surveys, the Kilo-Degree Survey (KIDS) and the VISTA Kilo-Degree Infrared Galaxy (VIKING), which provide data with coverage from the optical to the near-infrared electromagnetic spectrum. The results are then compared with those obtained by a classical neural network. We see that HQNN consistently recovers the reference photometric redshift with a high statistical fidelity. Furthermore, both the $R^2$ and the MSE found by the HQNN are consistently better than those found by the classical counterpart (albeit consistent in the error range), proving the worth of further investigating these novel technologies for this particular astrophysical application.

</details>
