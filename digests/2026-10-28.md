### [The Great Chat in the Sky: Aligning Exotic Astronomical Data to Language with AstroBridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36375)

Ibrahim Habib, Kevin Li, Trung Nguyen, Gabriel A. Patron et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36375)

**Why:** Multimodal astronomy models: AstroBridge aligns frozen image, spectrum and light-curve encoders to an LLM using literature-derived captions.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Astronomical observations include images, spectra, and light curves, while the scientific literature describes these objects in natural language. We present AstroBridge, a framework that connects these observations to an LLM through a literature-assisted caption generation pipeline. We used catalog metadata to match observations with scientific texts that describe the same objects. A vision-language model generates descriptive captions from representations of observations, matched passages, and available metadata, creating observation-caption pairs without manual captioning. We train AstroBridge to predict captions directly from raw observations. Modality-specific frozen encoders extract representations that a shared, trainable fusion network aligns with the embedding space of an LLM. In caption-based evaluation of spectra, AstroBridge achieves 91.5% accuracy in distinguishing quasars from galaxies, compared to 69.0% from the base model prior to fine-tuning. Limitations remain in galaxy morphology, classification of minority supernova classes, and the factual quality of generated captions. We believe that AstroBridge's framework is a step towards the goal of aligning the totality of astronomy's diverse, exotic data through the lingua franca of text.

</details>

### [The SIRMOS mission concept: a space-based near-IR spectroscopic survey of 100 million galaxies in the Euclid Wide Survey footprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35945)

Gregory D. Wirth (BAE Systems -- Space & Mission Systems, Broomfield, CO, USA) et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35945)

**Why:** Euclid: SIRMOS mission concept for slit spectra of 100M galaxies, targeted from Euclid Wide imaging, for LSS cosmology.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

SIRMOS is a mission concept for a future NASA Astrophysics Explorer opportunity that aims to revolutionize our understanding of the universe by mapping the large-scale structure of galaxies in unprecedented breadth and detail to explore cosmic origins and evolution. Specifically, the primary science goals of SIRMOS are to discover how cosmic inflation seeded large-scale structure, reveal the nature of the accelerating expansion of the universe, and measure the sum of neutrino masses to exceptionally high precision. Its secondary science goals include determining how environment shapes galaxy evolution at cosmic noon and beyond, and probing the Milky Way's dust-enshrouded regions, reaching the far side of our Galaxy. To accomplish its transformative science goals, SIRMOS executes a 3-year cosmology survey over the Euclid footprint of ~14,000 deg$^2$, obtaining slit spectra for ~100 million galaxies at 1<z<4. SIRMOS is the first space mission designed specifically to leverage Euclid's wide near-infrared (IR) imaging survey for target selection and employs a digital micro-mirror device (DMD) to acquire up to 4,000 simultaneous spectra over a wide 0.9 deg$^2$ field of view. The instrument's excellent spectral resolution of $R=1300$ and continuous wavelength coverage from 1.25--2.5 $\mu$m capture the redshifts of distant galaxies with no gaps, reducing systematic biases to achieve ground-breaking science.

</details>

### [A High Precision Metacalibration Response Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35998)

Erin S. Sheldon · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35998)

**Why:** Weak lensing shear measurement: Metacalibration noise correction reaching unbiased response with only ~2% extra noise instead of sqrt(2).

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In Metacalibration the shear response of a weak lensing measurement is estimated by applying an artificial shear to the image, but the shear operation imprints a spin-2 modulation on the image noise power, which biases the measured response. The standard fix adds a sheared noise field that symmetrizes the anisotropy, at the cost of doubling the noise variance ($\sqrt2$ in the noise). The fusion correction presented in this note reaches the same unbiased response while adding only the minimum power needed. It measures the noise power transfer by passing an impulse through the Metacal process, forms the common-level azimuthal deficit that equalizes the spin-2 ($m{=}2$) and spin-6 ($m{=}6$) modulations across the metacal types, and adds just that deficit by filtering the rotated noise field. The fusion correction raises the per-pixel noise by only $\sim2\%$ for a round PSF (versus $\sqrt2$ for the standard correction), rising toward the $\sqrt2$ level only for highly elliptical PSFs.

</details>

### [Field-level modeling of halo and galaxy alignments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36027)

Nickolas Kokron, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36027)

**Why:** Weak lensing systematics: field-level EFT model of intrinsic alignments, the main contaminant of cosmic shear, beating NLA/TATT on simulations.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We develop an effective field theory (EFT) of intrinsic alignments at the level of spin-2 field fluctuations of large-scale structure tracers, such as the shapes of galaxies and dark matter halos. Our perturbative model includes the non-linear treatment of the Zel'dovich displacement, and contains all necessary shape operators through the third order. We apply our field-level model to the full 3D distribution of galaxy shapes, whose sky projections are a known contaminant of cosmic shear measurements. Our field-level EFT model achieves a better description of the helicity fields of dark matter halo shapes than the standard non-linear alignment (NLA) and tidal torquing + tidal alignment (TATT) models in the literature, as evidenced by improved correlations with the simulations' initial conditions. We perform precision measurements of field-level bias parameters by means of a generalized least-squares problem, for which we derive an optimal estimator and analytic covariance matrices. We measure the full set of linear, quadratic, and cubic shape bias coefficients as a function of halo mass in the $\texttt{Quijote}$ N-body simulations. We then study the intrinsic alignments of galaxies in the $\texttt{FLAMINGO}$ hydrodynamical simulation, focusing on observationally relevant samples: spirals, ellipticals, luminous red galaxies (LRG) and emission-line galaxies (ELG). We detect linear and nonlinear alignment coefficients at high significance for LRG, spiral and elliptical galaxies. We also find evidence for non-zero density-weighted tidal bias coefficient of the simulated ELG. This work supports the notion that EFT-based models of alignments will be paramount in optimally extracting constraints from future shape correlations, and lays the foundation for the development of simulation-based priors for future galaxy shape samples.

</details>

### [Two-dimensional galaxy cluster shapes using lensing data: a simulated analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36039)

C. Giocoli, M. Meneghetti, G. Despali, L. Moscardini et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36039)

**Why:** Weak/strong lensing of clusters: tests how well 2D weak-lensing modelling recovers cluster morphology and compares it to strong-lensing critical lines.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Context. Cluster morphology reflects assembly history and dynamical state. High-resolution weak lensing can reveal departures from spherical symmetry, but its ability to recover two-dimensional shapes remains less explored. Aims. We assess how reliably weak lensing recovers projected cluster morphology and its dependence on orientation and dynamical state. We also compare large-scale weak-lensing morphology with that traced by strong lensing near the cluster centre. Methods. We study three clusters at $z=0.22$ from The Three Hundred Project, representing relaxed, mildly disturbed and unrelaxed systems. For each, we consider three random projections and three along the principal axes of the mass tensor. For each projection, we generate 128 weak-lensing catalogues with a source density of 30 arcmin$^{-2}$ and intrinsic ellipticity dispersion $\sigma_{\epsilon}=0.3$. We constrain mass and concentration from one-dimensional NFW and BMO fits, then recover ellipticity, position angle and centre using a two-dimensional elliptical NFW model. We also measure morphology from true convergence maps, Kaiser \& Squires reconstructions and strong-lensing tangential critical lines. Results. Recovered parameters depend on orientation and dynamical state. The relaxed cluster has similar inner and large-scale shapes, whereas disturbed systems appear more elliptical centrally. Two-dimensional weak-lensing modelling recovers the projected mass orientation despite source sampling and shape noise. Kaiser \& Squires reconstructions recover the position angle well but favour rounder mass distributions. Centroid offsets are larger in unrelaxed systems. Conclusions. Combining weak- and strong-lensing morphology probes cluster structure across physical scales and offers a promising way to investigate morphology and dynamical state.

</details>

### [Getting Ready For Analysing Rubin Data : CLMM v2, The Updated LSST-DESC Cluster Weak Lensing Mass Modeling Library For Cosmology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37455)

Michel Aguena, C\'eline Combet, Shenming Fu, Constantin Payerne et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37455)

**Why:** Weak lensing tooling: CLMM v2, the LSST-DESC cluster mass modelling library, with stacking and WL multipoles ready for Rubin.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Dark Energy Science Collaboration has prepared a multitude of pipelines to process the upcoming Vera C. Rubin Observatory data and perform different scientific analyses. Here, we present the methodology to constrain the mass information of galaxy clusters from weak lensing, which is encompassed by the v2.0 release of the Cluster Lensing Mass Modeling (CLMM) code. This Python library provides the ingredients for both the modeling and data reduction of lensing observables, and the improvements in the new version were customized to handle the first WL analyses of LSST clusters, including, e.g., stacking functionalities and WL multipoles. This new version of CLMM has allowed various cluster analyses using LSST-DESC simulated data, precursor survey and Rubin commissioning and survey validation data, and is ready to be used for the first cluster cosmology analyses. CLMM is an open-source software package collaboratively written in Python, providing multiple example notebooks, including a set that can reproduce all figures in this work, and extensive documentation, publicly available at https://lsstdesc.org/CLMM/.

</details>

### [The First Deep Learning all-sky List of Fermi-LAT Gamma-ray Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37287)

Saptashwa Bhattacharyya, Giacomo Principe, Dmitry Malyshev, Roberto Ruiz de Austri et al. · `astro-ph.HE` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37287)

**Why:** CNNs on survey imaging: U-Net segmentation plus DNNs build 1FDL, the first deep-learning all-sky Fermi-LAT source list.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Aims: We develop a deep-learning-based pipeline to construct a list of {\gamma}-ray sources and to quantitatively assess the impact of background uncertainties on source detection and characterization. This work extends and enhances the existing AutoSource-ID algorithm, previously adopted on optical observations, for applications on {\gamma}-ray data. Methods: Source detection was performed through image segmentation using a U-Net architecture, while dedicated deep neural networks (DNNs) were employed for source localization, flux estimation, and the classification of detected sources as either genuine or spurious. The training data were generated from Monte Carlo simulations based on 10 years of Fermi-LAT observations. Results: We present the first Fermi-LAT {\gamma}-ray all-sky source list derived using a deep-learning-based method (1FDL), containing more than 3800 sources with estimated positions, fluxes, and probability scores that quantify the likelihood of each detection being genuine or spurious. We further evaluated the robustness of the pipeline against variations in the interstellar emission model (IEM) and compared the resulting source list with the 4FGL-DR2 catalog. Conclusion: We find that the our deep-learning-based approach achieves performance comparable to, or better than, the likelihood-based methods employed in the standard Fermi-LAT catalog pipeline when tested against changes in the IEM. The 1FDL source list is broadly consistent with the Fermi-LAT 4FGL-DR2 catalog obtained over the same 10-year observational period, including a few new sources confirmed in the recent Fermi-LAT 16-year source list (FL16Y). These results demonstrate the potential of DNN methods for the detection and characterization of {\gamma}-ray sources and provide a foundation for their application in future high-energy astrophysical analyses.

</details>
