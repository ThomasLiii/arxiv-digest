### [Identifying Scientifically Interesting Galaxies with Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37747)

Yixuan Cheng, Nolan Koblischke, Jo Bovy · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37747)

**Why:** Foundation models and anomaly detection: off-the-shelf vision-language models rank HST galaxy images by scientific interest, recovering lenses zero-shot.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Anomaly searches in large astronomical image datasets aim to identify rare objects for follow-up investigation. Yet the objects that are most statistically unusual are not necessarily those of greatest scientific relevance. We investigate whether pretrained vision-language models can directly assess scientific interest from natural-language instructions, enabling galaxy images to be ranked without labeled examples. We evaluate models on a 20,000-image Hubble Space Telescope sample that includes 167 reference anomalies previously identified through machine learning and expert inspection. By scoring images for scientific interest, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite places 98.2% of the reference anomalies within the highest-ranked ~2,000 images, at a present-day inference cost of approximately $5.05 per 10,000 images. We recover known gravitational lenses, jellyfish-galaxy candidates, and galaxies with visible jets, and release 102 high-scoring candidates for further investigation. These results show that an off-the-shelf vision-language model, without any training on the target data, offers a practical way to rank large image datasets by scientific relevance rather than rarity. The project code is available at https://github.com/TheCheng2005/Galaxy-Anomaly-Search/

### [Extracting non-Gaussian information of line-intensity maps with weighted skew spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40063)

Eleonora Vanzan, Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah, Ely D. Kovetz · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40063)

**Why:** SPHEREx methods: optimally weighted skew spectra compress H-alpha intensity-map bispectrum, forecasting bias-ratio constraints for SPHEREx-like surveys.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The bispectrum of line-intensity maps carries cosmological and astrophysical information beyond the power spectrum, but its large data vector hampers the analysis of survey data. Skew spectra address this by compressing the bispectrum into a handful of power-spectrum-like observables, cross-spectra of the observed field with filtered squares of it. We present the first application of optimally weighted skew spectra to line-intensity mapping of galaxy-hosted emission, where all clustering and shot-noise terms are luminosity weighted and the mean intensity is unknown. We perform idealized real-space Fisher forecasts for a SPHEREx-like survey of H$\alpha$ emission at $0.2 \lesssim z \lesssim 4.6$, in its deep fields and in a noisier all-sky configuration. First, the compression carries over to intensity maps: on perturbative scales and for a fixed redshift evolution of the emitters, four skew spectra, one of them matched to the shot noise, recover the bispectrum constraints on biases, shot noise and cosmological parameters to within a few percent. Second, the joint analysis of the power spectrum and the skew spectra measures the ratios of the second-order biases to the linear bias in each redshift bin. Within a bin the biases are degenerate with the unknown mean intensity, but their ratios are not, so their redshift evolution is measured without a model for the emitters. For emission that depends on halo mass alone, these ratios are determined by luminosity-weighted averages of the halo biases, so their evolution can test the halo-emission connection, including dependence on assembly history or environment. The bias ratios are not accessible to the tree-level real-space power spectrum alone. At $z \lesssim 3$ an all-sky SPHEREx-like configuration can detect departures of order $0.1$ in the bias ratios at $3\sigma$, while deeper fields perform better in the highest-redshift bins.

</details>

### [Tabular Foundation Models Dramatically Outperform Bespoke Photometric Redshift Estimators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39332)

Biprateep Dey, Joshua S. Speagle · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39332)

**Why:** Photometric redshifts: tabular foundation models with in-context learning beat bespoke photo-z estimators, especially with biased training data.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Estimating galaxy redshifts (a proxy for distance) from imaging data alone, also known as photometric redshifts (photo-$z$'s), is essential for studies of extragalactic astrophysics and cosmology. However, data-driven estimators rely on spectroscopic training datasets, which are scarce and systematically biased toward bright and easily observed galaxies. In this work, we show that tabular foundation models (TFMs), whose synthetic pretraining amounts to a learned prior over regression problems, can outperform bespoke estimators using only in-context learning. Evaluating on a widely used benchmark dataset, we find that TFMs predicting full probability distributions in a single forward pass systematically outperform bespoke estimators across both representative and biased training data regimes, matching or exceeding baselines trained on over $40{,}000$ galaxies with just $1{,}000$ training context examples. We also show that the TFM advantage is largest when little to no calibration data are available, and that it persists even when the bespoke baselines are supplemented with up to $10{,}000$ representative calibration spectra. This shows that TFMs offer a sample-efficient framework for upcoming imaging-only astronomical surveys, where observational selection bias can be mitigated simply by including a small representative calibration sample in context.

</details>

### [Low-variance renoising for weak gravitational lensing shear estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39554)

Xiangchong Li · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39554)

**Why:** Weak lensing shear estimation: new spectral renoising cuts added noise in Metacalibration/AnaCal, raising effective galaxy density ~20%.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The "renoising" noise-bias correction used by Metacalibration and AnaCal adds to every galaxy image an independent noise field with the statistics of the original noise rotated by 90 degrees. The rotated field cancels, to first order in shear, the anisotropy that the shear-response operation induces in the original noise, at the cost of doubling the noise variance. We derive the general condition that an added noise field must satisfy for this cancellation: the distribution of the total noise -- the original noise transformed with the pixel shear-response operator, plus the added noise carrying an assigned shear dependence that we are free to choose -- must be independent of shear to first order. Under this condition the shear response of the renoised ellipticity is estimated from the known realization of the added noise alone. The rotated noise field, which we call rotation renoising, is one solution of the condition and yields the analytical response estimator of AnaCal. We then construct a second solution, spectral renoising, which adds only a small fraction of the original noise variance: for homogeneous Gaussian noise the added field is an independent, scaled-down copy of the image noise, and its assigned shear response is the same field passed through a fixed filter set by the noise power spectrum deconvolved by the point spread function. Image simulations show that spectral renoising keeps the multiplicative shear bias at the $10^{-3}$ level and raises the effective galaxy number density by about 20 per cent at the faint end.

</details>

### [UNIONS: Red and blue alignments of DESI DR1 galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40411)

R. Paviot, C. Murray, M. Kilbinger, C. Daley et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40411)

**Why:** Weak lensing systematics: UNIONS-DESI intrinsic alignment measurements by colour and mass, including a 3.3-sigma blue-galaxy detection.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are a dominant source of systematics in weak-lensing cosmological analyses, and direct measurements on spectroscopic samples are the primary source of priors on their amplitude. We measure the IA of galaxies from the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) as a function of colour, stellar mass, and halo mass, by cross-correlating the DESI spectroscopic catalogues with the weak-lensing shape catalogue of the Ultraviolet Near-Infrared Optical Northern Survey (UNIONS) over up to 2400 deg$^2$. We fit the three-dimensional galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-shape multipoles of the DESI BGS, LRG, and ELG samples, split by colour and stellar mass, with the non-linear linear alignment (NLA) and the tidal alignment and tidal torquing (TATT) models. Halo masses are measured for the BGS red sample from galaxy-galaxy lensing and extended to the LRGs through the resulting stellar-to-halo mass relation. Red galaxies are strongly and radially aligned, but their alignment amplitude is not a single power law of halo mass between $6\times10^{11}$ and $3\times10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$: it is nearly flat at low mass and rises steeply above a break at $1.3\times10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$. Massive blue BGS galaxies instead show a tangential alignment, detected at $3.3\sigma$, to our knowledge the most significant detection of a non-zero alignment of blue galaxies to date, while the low-mass blue BGS galaxies and the ELGs are consistent with zero. At our current precision, the data do not favour TATT over NLA.

### [The Carousel Lens III: Joint lens convergence and source kinematic modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37971)

William Sheu, Xiaosheng Huang, Tania M. Barone, Fuyan Bian et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37971)

**Why:** Strong lensing modeling: joint lens convergence and source kinematics fit to MUSE datacube of the Carousel Lens.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce a new, independent modeling component to the Carousel Lens series by exploiting the spatially-resolved kinematics contained within the lensed arcs, which so far has remained rather underexplored in strong-lensing analyses. We present an end-to-end forward-modeling framework that reconstructs an integral-field spectroscopic datacube from a parameterization of the lens convergence profile together with the line-of-sight velocity, velocity dispersion, and surface brightness profiles of the unlensed source. The kinematic structure of the source acts as an additional set of ray-traceable markers distributed across every lensed image, inaccessible from imaging data alone. Furthermore, by generating an [OII] emission-line datacube, we isolate a single source redshift and remove lens and line-of-sight light contamination. We apply this framework to deep VLT/MUSE observations of the Carousel Lens, modeling the [OII] emission of the quadruply-imaged star-forming galaxy at $z_{\rm s} = 1.432$ that anchors the system's cosmography constraints. We measure an Einstein radius $\theta_{\rm E} = 12.816''^{+0.005}_{-0.007}$ and a logarithmic slope $\gamma = 1.412^{+0.002}_{-0.002}$, tightening the statistical uncertainties on both by roughly a factor of $2-2.5$ relative to the corresponding flat emission-line map model. We find that the source galaxy exhibits a strong rotation, lies confidently along the star-forming main sequence, and has an inferred central black hole mass of $\log M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot} = 7.89 \pm 0.51$. These results establish datacube-level forward modeling as a practical and independent route to traditional image-based lens models in the current systematics-limited regime, and one that extends naturally to this system's other lensed sources and to other lens systems.

### [GRAVITY-RESOLVE: A comprehensive image reconstruction tool for Galactic Center observations with GRAVITY](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38589)

**Why:** Image mosaicking: GRAVITY-RESOLVE jointly reconstructs nine separate VLTI pointings into one NIR mosaic, 10x faster and deeper.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We aim to improve and extend the imaging capabilities of the GRAVITY beam combiner instrument of ESOs VLTI with respect to reconstruction software in the Galactic Center. We revise and develop the image reconstruction code GRAVITY-RESOLVE $\text{G}^{\text{R}}$, which has been specifically developed to reconstruct stars in the Galactic Center with GRAVITY. The main objectives are to increase the limiting magnitude in GRAVITY images, reduce its computational runtime, and allow for a joint reconstruction of multiple, spatially separate datasets/pointings on sky. ### [Disentangling constraints on $S_8$ from the high and low redshift CMB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37752)

Joann Jones, Ualype de Andrade Uch\^oa, Scott Dodelson · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37752)

**Why:** Weak lensing/clustering S8 tension: separates primordial CMB from CMB lensing, finding primordial S8 agrees with DES.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The clustering of matter in the universe is quantified by $\sigma_8$, the RMS fluctuations today in linear theory in spheres of radius $8h^{-1}$ Mpc, where $h$ parametrizes the Hubble constant. Surveys of gravitational lensing and galaxy clustering best constrain $S_8\equiv (\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5} \sigma_8$, where $\Omega_m$ is the energy density of matter in units of the critical density. Comparing this late time measurement to the value of $S_8$ inferred from the cosmic microwave background (CMB), one can test the $\Lambda$CDM prediction for the growth of clustering from the primordial universe to now. Several analyses report hints that CMB measurements of $S_8$ are larger than those from galaxy surveys. However, CMB measurements have information from both high and low redshift due to lensing, and these tests traditionally conflate the two. Here, we present a technique to extract constraints on the cosmological parameters from each redshift component separately, enabling a high-z vs. low-z clustering comparison that was not previously possible. We marginalize over lensing information using a principal component analysis, and show that the primordial CMB from combined Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 prefers $S_8= 0.795\pm0.022$, in agreement with the recent determination from the Dark Energy Survey (DES) of $S_8=0.789\pm0.012$. The clustering inferred from CMB lensing is larger. However, both the level of excess lensing and increase in agreement with DES depends on the prior on the sum of neutrino masses, $\sum m_\nu$. We find that removing lensing brings the CMB towards agreement with DES in any case.

### [Efficient GLS map-making and assessment of $1/f$ noise impact for future CMB satellite missions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39581)

Marta Monelli, Guillaume Patanchon, Avinash Anand · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39581)

**Why:** Map-making methods: STITCH inpainting removes chunk-edge artifacts in circulant GLS map-making for CMB satellites.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Time-correlated $1/f$ noise is a major source of large-scale contamination in measurements of the polarized cosmic microwave background (CMB) that can limit sensitivity to primordial $B$ modes. Generalized least-squares (GLS) map-making is optimal for stationary Gaussian noise when its covariance is known, but practical implementations rely on approximations to make the computation tractable. In particular, approximating the noise covariance as circulant enables efficient map-making using fast Fourier transform, but introduces artificial edge effects when applied to finite data chunks. We introduce STITCH (STatistical Inpainting for Timeline CHunks), a preprocessing method that mitigates these effects by extending time-ordered data chunks with synthetic samples that make the extended timelines statistically consistent with the assumed circulant noise model. They can then be processed with standard circulant GLS solvers, preserving their computational efficiency while reducing residuals associated with chunk boundaries. Simulations show that STITCH substantially suppresses these edge effects, bringing the resulting noise power spectra close to those obtained when the circulant approximation holds. We demonstrate negligible impact of our procedure on the recovered signal, such as $E\to B$ leakage, even at large scales. Finally, we investigate how temporal $1/f$ noise projects onto angular scales for representative LiteBIRD-, \emph{Planck}-, and PICO-like scanning strategies. Using noise simulations spanning a range of temporal noise parameters, we fit an effective model to the resulting angular noise spectra. These results provide an empirical characterization of the temporal-to-angular noise mapping for the configurations studied and a basis for further investigations of correlated noise in future CMB polarization missions.

</details>

### [Field-level inference under pressure: the information content of the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40617)

Carmen Embil Villagra, Adrian E. Bayer, William R. Coulton, Alina Sabyr et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40617)

**Why:** CNN field-level inference on simulated tSZ maps compared with Minkowski functionals in an SBI framework.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich (tSZ) field is highly non-Gaussian and contains cosmological information that cannot be fully captured by its angular power spectrum. In this work, we investigate the information content of this field using a convolutional neural network (CNN), and compare to simple and interpretable summary statistics, in a simulation-based inference (SBI) framework. We perform the first field-level analysis of the tSZ field, using a suite of $10{,}000$ simulated Compton-$y$ maps, where we jointly vary the cosmological parameters $\Omega_c$ and $\sigma_8$ (the density fraction for cold dark matter and the matter clustering amplitude, respectively) and two parameters describing the amplitude and shape of the electron pressure profile, $P_0$ and $\beta$, that aim to capture astrophysical uncertainties. We consider both noiseless simulations and simulations including realistic Simons Observatory (SO) noise after component separation. We use SBI to compare the constraints obtained from the CNN with those from the three Minkowski functionals (MFs), individually and in combination, as well as the angular power spectrum. This is the first application of SBI to the MFs of the tSZ field. We find that the CNN provides tighter constraints than conventional summary statistics in the noiseless case. However, at SO-like noise levels, the combination of MFs and the power spectrum recovers constraints comparable to those obtained with the CNN. Our results highlight the potential of these interpretable summary statistics, as well as field-level inference, for extracting information from the tSZ field, motivating efforts toward their application to real data.

### [Celia: Catalog Embeddings Learned from cosmologIcal simulAtions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40218)

Xiaowen Zhang, Matthew Ho, Anshul Kumar, Tiziana Di Matteo et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40218)

**Why:** Representation learning: self-supervised foundation model embedding simulated galaxy catalogs, linearly recovering cosmology and transferring to unseen codes.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Cosmological hydrodynamical simulations connect observed galaxies to cosmology and the physics of galaxy formation. Each simulation is expensive, and different codes model galaxy formation in different ways. We present Celia (Catalog Embeddings Learned from cosmologIcal simulAtions), a self-supervised foundation model that maps catalogs of subhalos and their galaxy properties to an embedding vector for the purposes of compression and organization of astrophysical and large-scale structure information. Celia is pretrained without parameter labels on the IllustrisTNG, SIMBA and Astrid suites of CAMELS and their N-body counterparts, by reconstructing masked subhalo properties and predicting the representations of a teacher network. Simple linear models fitted to the embeddings recover $\Omega_{\mathrm m}$ with $R^2=0.999$, and $\sigma_8$ and both supernova-feedback amplitudes with $R^2\geq0.97$, in all three suites. The embeddings translate well to out-of-distribution simulations such as Swift-EAGLE, a code that Celia never saw. A linear model fitted on the three training codes predicts its $\Omega_{\mathrm m}$ with a correlation coefficient $r=0.985$, and linear models fitted on 20 labeled simulations reach $r\geq0.94$ for $\Omega_{\mathrm m}$, $\sigma_8$ and a supernova-feedback amplitude. We decompose the variation among the simulations of each suite into principal components of the embedding, and simple power laws of the simulation parameters describe the leading components. Celia gives simulated galaxy catalogs a shared coordinate system for simulation-based inference, emulation, the comparison of galaxy-formation models, the detection of physical degeneracies and the discovery of new astrophysical probes.

### [The Redshift-Space Cluster-Galaxy Cross-Correlation as a Probe of Gravity on Cosmic Scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37782)

Nicole Marcelina Gountanis, David H. Weinberg, Andr\'es N. Salcedo, Claire Lamman · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37782)

**Why:** Large-scale structure: forecast of cluster-galaxy redshift-space cross-correlation in DESI BGS as a gravity test.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Testing General Relativity (GR) on cosmological scales requires probes that are directly sensitive to gravitational dynamics. We present a Fisher matrix forecast of how well the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) can constrain departures from GR through the cluster infall velocity field, using the redshift-space cluster-galaxy cross-correlation function $\xi^{\rm s}_{\rm cg}(r_p, r_\pi)$ as the primary observable. We construct mock catalogs from the AbacusSummit N-body simulations, using a Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model for galaxies and a log-normal mass-observable relation for cluster selection. We parameterize departures from GR with a single multiplicative factor $F_{\rm G}$ that rescales halo infall velocities relative to the GR prediction. Marginalizing over the HOD, mass-observable, and cosmological parameters, we find that DESI BGS can constrain $F_{\rm G}$ to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) = 0.033$ at $z \sim 0.3$. This precision is sufficient to distinguish representative $f(R)$ and Galileon gravity models from GR at $\gtrsim 4\sigma$. The strongest limitation is a degeneracy between $F_{\rm G}$ and the scatter in the cluster mass-observable relation, while the constraint shows no strong degeneracy with any individual HOD parameter. Perfect knowledge of the galaxy-halo connection would improve the constraint to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) \approx 0.015$, and including the galaxy autocorrelation function in the data vector could recover part of this improvement. The same data vector yields tight constraints on the cosmological parameters, $\sigma(\Omega_m) = 0.010$ and $\sigma(\sigma_8) = 0.010$, only moderately correlated with $F_{\rm G}$. These results demonstrate the potential of DESI BGS cluster-galaxy correlations to test gravity on quasi-linear scales, complementary to existing modified gravity probes and to weak-lensing cluster mass profiles.

### [The scale of homogeneity in DESI Data Release 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38083)

Cullan Howlett, L. Giani, Elizabeth G. O'Brien, A. Whitford et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38083)

**Why:** Large-scale structure: precise measurement of the cosmic homogeneity scale from DESI DR1 galaxies and quasars.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present measurements of the scale of cosmic homogeneity using the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), the largest publicly available spectroscopic extragalactic survey to date. Using a counts-in-spheres estimator applied to nearly 5 million galaxies and quasars across six independent redshift bins spanning $0.1 < z < 2.1$, we measure the correlation dimension $D_2(r)$ and define the homogeneity scale $R_H$ as the point at which the galaxy distribution reaches within 1\% of the homogeneous asymptotic value. We find that all tracers clearly transition to homogeneity, with the galaxy homogeneity scale ranging from $93.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}$ to $142.6^{+1.4}_{-1.4}$ $h^{-1}$Mpc depending on the tracer redshift, galaxy bias, and modelling used to extract the scale. Using a cosmology-dependent model, we reconstruct the underlying matter homogeneity scale, finding it decreases monotonically with redshift in excellent agreement with $\Lambda$CDM predictions. We identify a systematic offset of up to $\sim 10\%$ between measurements in the North and South Galactic Caps, largely due to fiber assignment effects reproduced in our simulations. Accounting for this, and with a complementary demonstration using toy models that our methodology correctly identifies inhomogeneous universes in all but rather finely-tuned observer configurations, this hence provides strong evidence in favour of the cosmological principle across our full redshift range. Our matter homogeneity scale measurements are consistent with, and substantially more precise than, previous constraints from WiggleZ and SDSS, demonstrating the power of DESI data for precision tests of the cosmological principle.

</details>
