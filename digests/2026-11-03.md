### [Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Mass-to-light ratio of elliptical galaxies: constraints from strong lensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00224)

Euclid Collaboration, A. Sonnenfeld (Department of Astronomy, School of Physics and Astronomy, Shanghai Jiao Tong University et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00224)

**Why:** Euclid Q1 strong lensing science: 95 lenses with reproducible selection function constrain elliptical-galaxy mass-to-light ratios, correcting for selection effects.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Mass is among the most fundamental properties describing a galaxy. The relation between the luminosity and the total mass of a galaxy contains information on the efficiency of the star formation process and on the distribution of dark matter. We used strong gravitational lensing data from the Euclid Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) to constrain the total mass enclosed within an aperture of $5\,h^{-1}$kpc, $M_5$, of elliptical galaxies. Our analysis is based on a sample of 95 lenses, which we built by ensuring that their selection function is reproducible. This enabled us to correct our inference for selection effects. The average $\log_{10}{(M_5\,h\,M_\odot^{-1})}$ for a galaxy with an $r$-band luminosity of $10^{10.7}L_{\odot}\,h^{-2}$ at $z=0.5$ is $11.30\pm0.02$. The mass $M_5$ scales with luminosity to the power $\beta_\mathrm{L}=0.71\pm0.07$, and decreases with increasing redshift at fixed luminosity. Assuming a scenario in which galaxies grow by mergers and stellar populations evolve passively, these correlations imply that $M_5$ increases with time for individual galaxies. These measurements provide a useful benchmark for testing models of galaxy structure and evolution that make predictions on the distribution of total mass on the scales of $5\,h^{-1}$kpc.

</details>

### [Beyond Point Photo-$z$ Estimates: Normalizing Flows for Tomographic $N(z)$ Reconstruction with DESI DR1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00121)

Zhuo Chen, Antoine Rocher, Kwan Chuen Chan, Jean-Paul Kneib · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00121)

**Why:** Photometric redshifts: normalizing-flow PDFs for tomographic N(z) with DESI DR1 and Legacy Surveys photometry, beating random-forest baselines.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Accurate calibration of tomographic redshift distributions, $N(z)$ is essential for cosmological analysis of weak lensing data. We investigate conditional normalizing flows (NFs) and compare their performance with random forest (RF) and quantile random forest (QRF) models. We consider DESI galaxy and quasar targets from DESI DR1 spectroscopic data and with the photometry from Legacy Surveys DR10. We train on 1.1 million sources and test on 2.0 million sources in a non-overlapping sky region ($0<z_{\rm spec}<4.5$). For point estimation, the NF achieves the lowest scatter and outlier fraction at $0<z_{\rm spec}<1.6$ and outperforms both baselines across all three metrics at $z_{\rm spec}>1.6$. By introducing distribution-level metrics like Wasserstein distance and JS divergence, we find that stacking NF posterior PDFs provides more accurate tomographic $N(z)$ reconstruction than QRF, particularly at high redshifts. For $z_{\rm median}>2.5$, the NF achieves a smaller absolute mean-redshift bias ($0.060\pm0.031$ versus $0.318$) and higher probability overlap ($0.905\pm0.008$ versus $0.787$) than QRF. We apply redshift-dependent post-hoc PDF calibration derived from a held-out validation sample. After calibration, the absolute mean-redshift biases in four bins spanning $0.2<z_{\rm median}<1.0$ range from $0.0016$ to $0.0019$, with central values below referring benchmark $0.002(1+z)$. In the highest-redshift bin, calibration further reduces the bias to $0.016\pm0.011$. Our results demonstrate the potential of NF PDFs for tomographic $N(z)$ reconstruction while highlighting challenges in transferring models across galaxy populations.

</details>

### [Recovering Missing Degrees of Freedom in Simulation-Trained Field-Level Generative Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02627)

Masato Shirasaki, Shiro Ikeda · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02627)

**Why:** Weak lensing field-level inference: physics-informed latent corrections recover variance lost by simulation-trained normalizing-flow generative models.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Simulation-trained generative models are emerging as a powerful framework for field-level inference in cosmology, particularly in strongly nonlinear regimes where analytical approaches become intractable. However, the finite size of training datasets often leads to variance shrinkage, indicating that part of the realization-level variability of the underlying physical process is not captured by the learned generative dynamics. In this Letter, we interpret variance shrinkage as a diagnostic of missing effective degrees of freedom and introduce physics-informed latent modifications to the generative dynamics of a continuous normalizing flow. We show that these latent corrections recover a substantial fraction of the missing variability without requiring additional training simulations. Using weak-lensing field-level inference as a representative cosmological application, we further demonstrate through posterior analysis that the learned latent directions are actively exploited during inference and behave as effective nuisance parameters that absorb part of the mismatch between the training and target distributions. As a result, the model exhibits improved robustness to out-of-distribution shifts in field-level inference. Our work establishes variance shrinkage as a practical diagnostic for identifying missing degrees of freedom in simulation-trained generative models and provides a framework for recovering them through physics-informed modifications of generative dynamics.

</details>

### [Characterisation Methodology for Persistence and Anti-persistence in IR Detectors for the Euclid and SVOM Space Missions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02860)

Naomie De Araujo, Aur\'elia Secroun, Marie Tourneur Silvain, \'Eric Kajfasz et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02860)

**Why:** Euclid methods: characterisation methodology for persistence and anti-persistence in Euclid H2RG infrared detectors, toward in-flight correction.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

H2RG and ALFA are the infrared detectors used in the Euclid and SVOM space missions. These types of detectors may be affected by persistence and anti-persistence, phenomena that distort pixel responses after exposure to light due to simultaneous trapping and release of charges within the pixel. These phenomena have shown to cause biases in flux measurements and potentially false detections, among other effects. This article presents a universal methodology for characterising persistence and anti-persistence using differential ramps, Savitzky--Golay filtering, and non-linearity correction. When applied to ground characterisation data, this approach reveals spatial inhomogeneities, environmental dependencies (in particular fluence, flux, temperature and wavelength), and unexpected spectral effects in ALFA. These preliminary results validate the method and pave the way for in-flight correction models capable of meeting the missions' accuracy requirements.

</details>

### [Statistical Modeling of Foreground Galactic Cirrus for Diffuse Halo Light Measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03172)

Qing Liu, Henk Hoekstra, Ama\"el Ellien · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03172)

**Why:** Euclid imaging methods: statistical Galactic cirrus foreground reconstruction tested on Euclid VIS Abell 2390 for low-surface-brightness photometry.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Galactic cirrus, the diffuse Milky Way starlight scattered by interstellar dust, has become a major source of systematic uncertainty in measurements of extragalactic diffuse emission at ultra-low surface brightness levels. Proper modeling and correction of this foreground is crucial to unlock the full potential of next-generation deep imaging surveys for studies of faint diffuse emission such as galactic stellar halos, ultra-diffuse galaxies, and intracluster light (ICL). We develop a statistical approach to reconstruct the 2D distribution of the Galactic cirrus while preserving the underlying diffuse extragalactic emission from stellar halos. We combine the broad dust morphology traced by far-infrared emission with multiscale structural information extracted from the optical images. The method synthesizes plausible cirrus structures through iteratively refining the foreground, the smooth diffuse light model, and the residual sky. The performance is assessed through injection--recovery experiments using Euclid VIS observations of the Abell 2390 cluster, which show substantial improvements in recovered stellar halo photometry, although residual biases depend on the local foreground structure and sky estimation. We apply the method to the central Abell 2390 field to correct for a prominent cirrus complex near its brightest cluster galaxy (BCG), revealing a smoother BCG+ICL distribution with a steeper outer light profile and reduced contamination in the integrated light. Our results demonstrate the potential of statistical modeling of Galactic cirrus to improve diffuse light photometry in deep wide-field imaging surveys.

</details>

### [Multiprobe strategies for Kinematic Lensing with the Roman Space Telescope High Latitude Wide Area Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03497)

Yu-Hsiu Huang, Jiachuan Xu, Tim Eifler, Elisabeth Krause et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03497)

**Why:** Weak lensing: forecasts for Roman kinematic lensing and a joint KL-clustering-GGL multiprobe analysis, doubling dark energy FoM.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In its latest design, the High Latitude Wide Area Survey (HLWAS) Medium Tier comprises overlapping photometric and spectroscopic data across 2,300 square degrees. This dataset enables Kinematic Lensing (KL), a Weak Lensing (WL) technique that exploits galaxy kinematics to control for shape noise, the dominant statistical limitation of traditional WL. We present several simulated analyses quantifying the expected cosmological science return of a Roman KL analysis with the latest design of the HLWAS: We explore KL prospects with early Roman data from the Deep Tier fields and from the early Medium Tier fields; we demonstrate the impact of different roll-angle choices on the KL performance; we benchmark KL against traditional WL; we introduce a KL-based multiprobe analysis that jointly models KL, galaxy clustering, and galaxy-galaxy lensing. In these analyses, we consider systematics, including photometric uncertainties, shear calibration bias, galaxy bias, and baryonic feedback. Our results show that KL alone achieves a dark energy figure of merit (FoM) of 22.54, which is more than a factor of two improvement over a corresponding WL analysis. The KL multiprobe approach improves the full-survey FoM to 34.58, even assuming conservative scale cuts in the clustering and galaxy-galaxy lensing. For the HLWAS roll-angle strategy, we find that the current plan of 4 passes with rotations of approximately 0, 10, 170, 180 degrees are near optimal for KL.

### [Baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignments in profile: a frequentist view of DES Y3 cosmic shear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03537)

Anoma Ganguly, Elisabeth Krause, Tanvi Karwal, Vivian Miranda · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03537)

**Why:** Weak lensing: profile likelihoods for DES Y3 cosmic shear expose S8-baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignment degeneracies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Current cosmic shear surveys primarily constrain $S_8$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, while most other model parameters remain dominated by priors. Consequently, marginal posteriors are vulnerable to prior-volume effects, particularly through degeneracies among $S_8$, baryonic feedback, and intrinsic alignment (IA) model parameters. To isolate data-driven constraints from prior volume effects, we compare profile likelihoods with Bayesian posteriors for DES Y3 cosmic shear under the DES Y3+KiDS-1000 Hybrid model. For cosmic shear alone, the profile global best fit and the marginal posterior mean agree in $S_8$, but the profile likelihood reveals a strong degeneracy between feedback strength and $S_8$, leaving the upper $1\sigma$ limit on $S_8$ determined by the assumed feedback range. External CMB information on parameters other than $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ and $S_8$ breaks this degeneracy, suppressing prior-volume effects and reconciling the profiles with the posteriors. With CMB data included, small-scale cosmic shear data prefers moderate baryonic feedback strengths consistent with hydrodynamical simulations calibrated to observed gas fractions. Furthermore, additional unconstrained parameters in the tidal alignment and tidal torquing (TATT) model result in weaker constraints, shifting and broadening the $S_8$ profile relative to the nonlinear linear alignment (NLA) model. Adding DES Y3 shear ratios mitigates this degeneracy, bringing profile likelihood and marginal posteriors into close agreement.

</details>

### [Shear Mapping in Python (SMPy): Modular, Extensible, and Accessible Dark Matter Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00225)

Georgios N. Vassilakis, Jacqueline E. McCleary, Maya Amit, Sayan Saha · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00225)

**Why:** Weak lensing mass mapping: open-source SMPy package with Kaiser-Squires, aperture mass and first public full KS+ implementation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Understanding the Universe's large-scale distribution of dark matter is a central objective in the era of precision cosmology. A key technique for the study of dark matter is weak gravitational lensing: a phenomenon where light from distant galaxies is sheared as it passes through the gravitational field of a massive object, like a galaxy cluster. This shear, which manifests as a slight (weak) distortion of shapes over thousands of galaxies, allows astrophysicists to infer the distribution of total matter, including both luminous and dark matter. Obtaining a mass distribution from a catalog of galaxy shears involves reconstructing the convergence ($\kappa$), the projected surface mass density divided by the critical surface density for the lens-source system. The Shear Mapping in Python (SMPy) package provides a standardized, well-documented, and open-source solution for creating convergence maps from weak lensing galaxy shear measurements. SMPy implements three mapping methods: the classic Kaiser-Squires inversion, aperture mass mapping, and, to our knowledge, the first publicly available implementation of the full KS+ algorithm, which corrects for missing data, field borders, and reduced shear. SMPy was initially developed to support the Superpressure Balloon-borne Imaging Telescope (SuperBIT) and has since evolved into a general-purpose tool suitable for analyzing the weak lensing data from cosmological surveys.

</details>

### [Anomaly-ranked confirmation slows new-class learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03250)

Argyro Sasli, Michael Coughlin · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03250)

**Why:** Anomaly detection: shows confirming top-ranked anomalies teaches new classes less efficiently than random selection, across five benchmarks.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Anomaly detectors rank unfamiliar examples for expert confirmation, and the confirmed examples teach a classifier to recognise a new class. Across five benchmarks from physics, medicine and computer vision, the members flagged by the detector are those the classifier learns first. In three systems, confirming the highest-ranked members of a new class gives lower recognition than confirming the same number of random members. Handing subsequent selection to the promoted classifier recovers part of this loss in two systems. A paired intervention further shows that replacing nearby known classes with distant ones sharply reduces the confirmations needed for recognition. These results distinguish finding a new class from selecting examples that teach its extent: within the tested classes, concentrating confirmations at the top of the anomaly ranking makes learning less efficient.

</details>

### [What can strong lensing recover in edge-on disc galaxies? A mock study based on TNG50 simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03084)

Zheng Fang, Xiaoyue Cao, Ran Li, Weipeng Lin · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03084)

**Why:** Strong lensing: TNG50 mock study of what PyAutoLens imaging fits recover for edge-on disc lenses, mass slope and decomposition.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Strong gravitational lensing tightly constrains the projected mass within the Einstein aperture, but what else imaging alone can recover for edge-on disc lenses remains untested against known truth. We construct HST/ACS-like mock observations of 35 edge-on TNG50 disc galaxies selected to resemble lenses from the Sloan WFC Edge-on Late-type Lens Survey (SWELLS), and fit them with a five-stage Source-Light-and-Mass workflow in PyAutoLens; the final model represents the lens mass distribution by scaling the Multi-Gaussian Expansion (MGE) foreground-light template with a global mass-to-light ratio and adding a spherical NFW halo. Decomposed fits succeed for 21 of the 35 systems; in the other 14, coherent MGE lens-light residuals prevent reliable modelling. For the successful subset, the Einstein radius and the total aperture mass are recovered with normalised median absolute deviation (NMAD) scatters of 0.3% and 0.7%. The global projected mass-weighted slope has an NMAD scatter of 10.3%, smaller than that of the mass-sheet-invariant reduced deflection curvature $\xi$ (11.4%) and well below that of the local slope (40.7%), while the stellar and dark-matter aperture masses scatter by 15.1% and 108.8%. A 35-system true-light diagnostic using the exact input light gives component-mass scatters of 0.8% and 6.3%. Under these controlled assumptions, imaging constrains Einstein-scale total mass and global radial structure more reliably than local radial structure or the stellar-dark-matter decomposition.

</details>

### [A multiprobe $\Lambda$CDM consistency analysis with gravitational-wave standard sirens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00415)

T. M. C. Abbott (DES Collaboration), M. Aguena (DES Collaboration), F. Andrade-Oliveira (DES Collaboration), S. Avila (DES Collaboration) et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00415)

**Why:** Weak lensing and clustering: DES Y6 3x2pt combined with GW standard sirens to assess S8 and H0 tensions.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-messenger cosmology offers an independent path to stress-test the $\Lambda$CDM model as gravitational wave (GW) catalogs grow in size. We present the first multiprobe assessment of $\Lambda$CDM tensions incorporating GW standard sirens: LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA Collaboration (LVK) fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog (GWTC-5.0) standard sirens are combined with Dark Energy Survey (DES) Year 6 weak lensing and galaxy clustering (3$\times$2pt), baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), and type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), together with Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) BAO, and confronted with primary cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements from Planck, the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT), and the South Pole Telescope (SPT). All probes are analyzed under homogeneous priors and combined via normalizing flows, and we quantify the $S_8$ discrepancy and $H_0$ tension with respect to CMB. Adding standard sirens to the 3$\times$2pt analysis improves the constraints in the $\Omega_m$-$S_8$ plane by $\sim$30%, while constraining $H_0$ to 6.2%. We present cosmological constraints from the low-redshift probes including standard sirens, yielding $S_8 = 0.793 \pm 0.010$, $\Omega_m = 0.308^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$, and $H_0 = 69.4^{+4.1}_{-4.4}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$. At current GW sensitivity, however, both the $S_8$ discrepancy and the $H_0$ tension remain essentially unchanged. Our analysis nonetheless delivers the first assessment of $\Lambda$CDM tensions computed with standard sirens included among the probes, and establishes them as a mature cosmological probe -- ready to be combined on equal footing with established datasets in forthcoming surveys such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), Euclid, and Roman.

</details>
