### [COSMIO: A Benchmark for Cross-Survey Modality Imputation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04139)

Dichang Zhang, Yixuan Shao, Jiaqi Deng, Jiali Cui et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04139)

**Why:** Cross-survey (LSST/Euclid/Roman) imputation benchmark using galaxy-galaxy strong lensing as the rare-event, downstream lens detection task; hits strong-lensing ML and Euclid.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large-scale astronomical surveys define a heterogeneous, partially observed multi-survey learning setting: each object is observed by only a subset of surveys, while survey observations differ in resolutions, wavelength coverage, noise characteristics, and footprints. This setting gives rise to a natural machine learning problem: given observations of an object in some surveys, how to infer its appearance in other surveys. We formalize this as the problem of cross-survey modality imputation and introduce COSMIO, a multi-survey benchmark of aligned observations obtained by forward-rendered shared astrophysical scenes across LSST, Euclid, and Roman under survey-specific instrument characteristics. The benchmark enables systematic evaluation of imputation across source-target survey combinations and missing-survey patterns. We evaluate a broad range of machine learning methods for imputation, which reveal a clear domain gap: methods developed for existing imputation settings transfer poorly to the survey setting. We identify rare-event imputation as a central challenge. This regime arises in the early stages of new surveys, when rare but scientifically valuable phenomena may be available only from prior surveys. We use strong gravitational lensing, where light from a background galaxy is gravitationally deflected by a foreground galaxy, as a representative rare-event setting. We evaluate model performance on zero-shot imputation of gravitational lenses and on downstream scientific tasks using the imputed lens observations. On our constructed evaluation set of galaxy-galaxy strong lenses, performance degrades under rare-event distribution shift, and this degradation further propagates to the downstream lens-detection task. Code: https://github.com/fw-ic/COSMIO.

</details>

### [3D dark matter map of the JWST COSMOS-Web field using diffusion-based generative priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03786)

Diana Scognamiglio, Brandon Zhao, Katherine L. Bouman, Olivier Dor\'e et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03786)

**Why:** Weak-lensing 3D dark-matter mapping of JWST COSMOS-Web using a diffusion-based generative prior, combining imaging with ML, matching your weak-lensing and representation-learning interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Mapping the three-dimensional distribution of dark matter is key to tracing the growth of cosmic structure. Two-dimensional weak lensing mass maps recover its projected distribution, but they blend structures at different distances along the line of sight. We reconstruct the three-dimensional matter distribution in the 0.54 deg$^2$ COSMOS-Web field using deep James Webb Space Telescope imaging, with an effective source density of 121 galaxies arcmin$^{-2}$ We combine a physical weak lensing forward model with a diffusion-based Bayesian generative prior learned from cosmological simulations, yielding a posterior distribution of the matter field across 20 lens planes to $z \simeq 2$, doubling the redshift reach of previous Hubble Space Telescope-based 3D reconstructions and approaching cosmic noon, an epoch of rapid galaxy growth. The reconstruction separates structures that overlap in projection and recovers non-Gaussian matter structure across a range of spatial scales, including over-densities associated with independently identified galaxy clusters that were not detected in earlier weak lensing maps. Posterior sampling quantifies the range of three-dimensional matter distributions allowed by the data, enabling a probabilistic visualization of the cosmic web while propagating uncertainties in its inferred structure. Null and simulation-based tests show that the recovered structures are supported by the lensing signal and are consistent with expectations from the fiducial $\Lambda$CDM cosmology. These results extend three-dimensional weak lensing mapping into the high-redshift, non-Gaussian regime and establish a route towards probabilistic mapping of the evolving cosmic web with current wide-field lensing surveys.

</details>

### [SBi3PCF: cosmology with Dark Energy Survey Year 3 cosmic shear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05367)

David Gebauer, Anik Halder, Zhengyangguang Gong, Cora Uhlemann · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05367)

**Why:** First simulation-based inference of a 3-point cosmic shear statistic on DES Y3, squarely in your weak-lensing cosmology interest.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present the first cosmic shear data analysis of the integrated 3-point correlation function (i3PCF), a higher-order weak lensing statistic, and the first simulation-based inference (SBI) analysis of a three-point cosmic shear correlation function on survey data. Using SBi3PCF, our previously developed SBI framework for the i3PCF, we jointly constrain the $w$CDM parameter space with the cosmic shear 2-point correlation functions $\xi_\pm$ (2PCFs) and the i3PCFs $\zeta_\pm$ from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3) weak lensing shape catalogue, from which we detect $\zeta_\pm$ with a signal-to-noise ratio of 12.8. Our forward model is built on the CosmoGridV1 N-body simulations and includes the survey mask, shape noise, intrinsic alignment, baryonic feedback, photometric redshift uncertainty and shear calibration bias. The analysis was carried out blind, with all choices frozen, and only unblinded once coverage tests, tests of the accuracy of the learned likelihoods, and internal consistency tests had been passed. For the joint $\xi_\pm$ + $\zeta_\pm$ analysis we obtain $\Omega_m = 0.272^{+0.038}_{-0.033}$, $S_8 = 0.784^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$ and $w_0 = -1.04^{+0.25}_{-0.19}$. Adding $\zeta_\pm$ improves the figure of merit in the $\Omega_m$-$S_8$-$w_0$ parameter space by a factor of $1.59$ over $\xi_\pm$ alone, as predicted by simulated i3PCF analyses. This gain comes from third-order information on angular scales $\geq 15'$ and aperture radii $\geq 50'$, a regime where baryonic effects are subdominant. The constraints are consistent with the other DES Y3 analyses of the same catalogue and with independent lensing analyses, and differ by at most $2.4\sigma$ from CMB and BAO data (Planck 2018 + BAO, in $\Omega_m$-$S_8$-$w_0$). This shows that $\zeta_\pm$ can be analysed on cosmological survey data with a fully simulation-based approach, an important step towards Stage-IV surveys.

</details>

### [Stress-Testing Small-Scale Cosmological Inference With Empirical Galaxy--Halo Connection Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04419)

Hanyue Wang, Kate Storey-Fisher, Philip Mansfield, Andrew Hearin et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04419)

**Why:** Small-scale galaxy clustering cosmology (S8 from SHAM vs HOD), relevant to your large-scale-structure/clustering interest.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Small-scale galaxy clustering contains substantial cosmological information, but extracting it reliably requires robust modeling of the galaxy--halo connection. In this work, we compare two widely adopted families of empirical models, subhalo abundance matching (SHAM) and the halo occupation distribution (HOD), testing their predictions for galaxy clustering and their recovery of the input cosmology. Using Aemulus-$\beta$, a new multi-cosmology simulation suite with substructure merger trees, we fit multiple SHAM and HOD models to the small-scale clustering of a stellar-mass-selected mock galaxy catalog generated with the more physical semi-empirical model UniverseMachine. We then use cosmological evidence modeling to infer the structure growth parameter $S_8$. Our results show that the SHAM model, regardless of the treatment of numerically disrupted subhalos, is able to offer tight, unbiased constraints on $S_8$ with ~2-3% precision, using simulations of relatively small volumes that span a limited number of cosmologies. In contrast, HOD models, even when accounting for assembly bias, yield weaker and potentially biased cosmological constraints that are sensitive to implementation choices: the inferred $S_8$ degrades in precision to ~6-11% and can be offset by ~1$\sigma$. This suggests that the excessive parametric flexibility and model misspecification of most commonly adopted HOD models can degrade and bias the resulting cosmological constraints. We further evaluate each model's performance in predicting higher-order statistics not used in the fit and find that SHAM generally reproduces them more accurately than the tested HOD models. These results highlight the potential of leveraging predictive subhalo-level information from simulations to enable robust cosmological inference on small scales.

</details>

### [Reconstructing the Large-Scale Velocity Field from DESI DR1 with Bayesian Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04830)

Raeed Mundow, Adi Nusser · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04830)

**Why:** Bayesian reconstruction of the large-scale velocity/density field from DESI DR1, relevant to your large-scale-structure interest.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Survey boundaries and nonlinear dynamics complicate reconstruction of large-scale matter-density and peculiar-velocity fields from galaxy redshift surveys. We develop and compare two reconstruction methods within a common Bayesian framework, based on linear perturbation theory and the Zel'dovich approximation, and apply them to the DESI Data Release 1 Bright Galaxy Survey. We infer the initial density field using a Gaussian prior and likelihood, with the Zel'dovich model displacing particles from a uniform Lagrangian grid. Both yield maximum a posteriori (MAP) fields; only the linear posterior is sampled. Our main findings are: \textit{(i)} Both reconstructions yield similar large-scale density structures and coherent velocity patterns in the survey interior, beyond roughly \(50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\) from the footprint boundaries. \textit{(ii)} Comparison with Cosmicflows-4 groups, predominantly from the SDSS Fundamental Plane sample in the DESI overlap, shows close agreement between mean observed radial velocities and MAP predictions when binned by the latter, despite substantial individual scatter. \textit{(iii)} Both yield similar reflex-dipole profiles, defined as mean shell velocities relative to those at randomly sampled centres, out to \(\sim225\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). The profiles depend weakly on the prior normalization \(\sigma_8\), but lie below the fiducial linear \(\Lambda\)CDM reference at \(r<50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). Sparse sampling limits the largest radii. \textit{(iv)} In an exploratory BAO test, shifting galaxies and randoms to a target redshift \(z=8\) using reconstructed displacements sharpens the acoustic feature in both cases, with a slightly more pronounced peak for Zel'dovich. Survey geometry and galaxy bias remain the principal limitations.

</details>

### [Mapping large-scale structure without galaxy formation - combining fast radio bursts with CMB lensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05351)

James M. Sullivan, Kiyoshi Masui, Ryan Raikman, Shion Andrew et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05351)

**Why:** Combines FRBs with CMB lensing to constrain sigma8/Omega_m, a large-scale-structure probe relevant to your cosmology interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are an emerging precision probe of cosmological fluctuations. The amount and quality of observed FRB data will grow enormously over the next decade. FRB dispersion data directly probe the free electron distribution up to intergalactic scales. Precision models of the free electron distribution are necessary to exploit this exciting new probe of large-scale structure. We show that the free electron distribution almost exactly follows the total matter distribution. We confirm this fact by estimating the electron field-level bias parameters for the first time from the FLAMINGO simulations. With simulation-based knowledge of a handful of electron free parameters, the electron distribution largely acts as a second realization of the matter distribution. Working on large scales, we can leverage this fact to combine FRB correlations with those of Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) weak lensing to constrain cosmological parameters. Assuming access to localized FRBs with redshifts, we perform cosmological forecasts using these two large-scale matter probes, which have no strong dependence on baryonic feedback models nor on parameters that encode galaxy formation. We show that the parameters $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ are constrained at a level similar to or better than that obtained by combining FRB dispersion with weak lensing of galaxy shapes under conservative assumptions. These results motivate the use of FRB data as a complementary probe of cosmic structure and will be especially compelling as redshifts become available for localized FRB host galaxies.

</details>

### [Spin Spherical Fourier Neural Operators: Efficient Equivariant Learning for Vector and Spin Fields on the Sphere](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04389)

Kevin Mulder, Matthew A. Price, Eirini Leftaki, Jason D. McEwen · `cs.CV` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04389)

**Why:** Rotationally-equivariant neural operators for spin fields on the sphere, demonstrated on weak-lensing shear and CMB polarization; ML directly applicable to lensing imaging.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Many fields of physical interest on the sphere, such as wind, the polarisation of the cosmic microwave background and the shear of weak gravitational lensing, are spin fields, whose values change phase when the local frame rotates. We extend spherical Fourier neural operators (SFNOs) to fields of any spin, through lifted spherical convolutions that are exactly rotationally equivariant. Lifting spin fields to Mackey functions on the rotation group provides the conceptual framework, while computation is performed exactly and efficiently through their harmonic representation and by appealing to sampling theorems on the sphere and rotation group. We construct spin SFNOs (S$^2$FNOs) from these building blocks. We demonstrate empirically that rotational equivariance holds to machine precision for these layers. We achieve state-of-the-art performance on existing benchmarks, and introduce new benchmarks on which spin-aware models outperform their scalar counterparts.

</details>
