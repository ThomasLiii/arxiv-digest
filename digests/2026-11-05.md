### [Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Refining the strong lens sample with Space Warps crowd-sourced science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06667)

Euclid Collaboration, P. Holloway (Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, Portsmouth PO1 3FX et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06667)

**Why:** Euclid Q1 strong lenses: calibrated Space Warps volunteer grading reaches 79% completeness for A+B lenses, projecting ~12,500 lenses in DR1.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We demonstrate the efficacy of crowd-sourced refinement of strong gravitational lens candidate samples by citizen scientists. Binary (lens/non-lens) classification by citizen scientists has been conducted previously, and here we extend such categorisation into `definite', `probable', `possible', and `non-lenses'. This grading is a required stage ahead of any strong lens sample publication and is usually carried out by (small) teams of researchers. We compare the responses of the volunteers to those of strong lens researchers and discuss the optimal strategy for lens classification and grading in future strong lens searches. Volunteers are found to be able to correctly identify systems of each grade, and their responses can be calibrated to be comparable to those of researchers. Grade posteriors, defined as the probability that a strong lens system classified by the volunteers is a certain grade, converge most rapidly for non-lens systems. We find that one researcher classification is equivalent to only 1.9 volunteer classifications when grading such systems. There is significant disagreement in the grading of intermediate-grade lens candidates amongst both volunteers and researchers. Moving towards calibrated lens probabilities for future lens searches would help to account for such subjectivity. If the Q1 lens search was rerun with volunteers performing lens grading (and corresponding calibration), a completeness of 79% for Grades A+B (corresponding to definite + probable lenses respectively) could be achieved with 41% purity prior to researcher inspection - this would correspond to an expected 12500 true lenses (i.e. excluding contaminants) in Euclid DR1.

</details>

### [HLA-Net: A Physics-Guided Skeleton-Based Framework for Automatic Gravitational Arc Detection in Astronomical Images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05438)

Don S · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05438)

**Why:** Strong lensing detection: an annotation-free, physics-guided skeleton and circle-fitting pipeline for automatically finding gravitational arcs in cluster images.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Gravitational arcs produced by strong gravitational lensing provide a unique approach for investigating dark matter distribution, galaxy cluster mass reconstruction and the structure of the Universe. Automated detection remains challenging because gravitational arcs are rare, often faint and morphologically similar to elongated galaxies.In this paper, we propose \textbf{HLA-Net (Hybrid Lens Arc Network)}, an interpretable multi-stage framework for automatic gravitational arc detection that explicitly exploits both the geometric morphology and physical characteristics of gravitational lensing without requiring manually annotated training datasets.The proposed framework was evaluated on astronomical FITS images containing complex galaxy cluster structures. Starting from 169 initial arc candidates extracted from ordered skeleton representations, the multi-stage verification pipeline reduced the search space to 83 geometrically consistent candidates, while robust circle fitting successfully modelled 113 candidate arc structures. The results demonstrate that HLA-Net effectively suppresses false-positive detections while preserving physically reasonable lens candidates through an interpretable and modular verification strategy.

### [Euclid: Survey mask effects on clustering of galaxy clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06304)

A. Fumagalli, R. Ingrao, M. Costanzi, T. Castro et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06304)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 cluster clustering: shows survey mask and inhomogeneous coverage barely bias the correlation function but inflate covariance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Wide-area cluster catalogues from Stage-IV surveys will substantially improve the precision of cluster clustering measurements, making the control of observational systematic effects essential for cosmological inference. In preparation for the first Euclid data release (DR1), we assess the impact of the survey mask and non-homogeneous sky coverage on the two-point correlation function of galaxy clusters and its covariance. Using DR1-like lightcone mocks, we confirm that the irregular footprint does not introduce appreciable changes in the two-point correlation function when the same mask is applied to the random catalogue. We also find that spatial inhomogeneities, implemented as variations of the cluster selection function in different patches of the sky, do not significantly bias the clustering amplitude, up to levels of variation well above those expected for DR1. When the degree of inhomogeneity is increased to the regime where the effect becomes significant, we show that its amplitude is related to the maximum variation of the selection function among different regions, rather than to the number of such regions. We further find that irregular mask geometry and non-uniform sky coverage both increase the clustering covariance, introducing small, nearly scale-independent offsets that can be captured by nuisance parameters in the covariance model. Our results consolidate the treatment of mask systematic errors for DR1 and inform the methodological refinements required for future \Euclid cluster cosmology.

</details>

### [3D dark matter map of the JWST COSMOS-Web field using diffusion-based generative priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03786)

Diana Scognamiglio, Brandon Zhao, Katherine L. Bouman, Olivier Dor\'e et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03786)

**Why:** Weak lensing plus ML: 3D dark matter map of COSMOS-Web from JWST shear using a diffusion generative prior, reaching z~2.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Mapping the three-dimensional distribution of dark matter is key to tracing the growth of cosmic structure. Two-dimensional weak lensing mass maps recover its projected distribution, but they blend structures at different distances along the line of sight. We reconstruct the three-dimensional matter distribution in the 0.54 deg$^2$ COSMOS-Web field using deep James Webb Space Telescope imaging, with an effective source density of 121 galaxies arcmin$^{-2}$ We combine a physical weak lensing forward model with a diffusion-based Bayesian generative prior learned from cosmological simulations, yielding a posterior distribution of the matter field across 20 lens planes to $z \simeq 2$, doubling the redshift reach of previous Hubble Space Telescope-based 3D reconstructions and approaching cosmic noon, an epoch of rapid galaxy growth. The reconstruction separates structures that overlap in projection and recovers non-Gaussian matter structure across a range of spatial scales, including over-densities associated with independently identified galaxy clusters that were not detected in earlier weak lensing maps. Posterior sampling quantifies the range of three-dimensional matter distributions allowed by the data, enabling a probabilistic visualization of the cosmic web while propagating uncertainties in its inferred structure. Null and simulation-based tests show that the recovered structures are supported by the lensing signal and are consistent with expectations from the fiducial $\Lambda$CDM cosmology. These results extend three-dimensional weak lensing mapping into the high-redshift, non-Gaussian regime and establish a route towards probabilistic mapping of the evolving cosmic web with current wide-field lensing surveys.

</details>

### [The PAU Survey: Self-supervised image denoising for flux-preserving photometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05447)

J. Chen, M. Eriksen, D. Navarro-Giron\'es, P. Renard et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05447)

**Why:** Survey imaging ML: flux-preserving self-supervised Noise2Void denoising with LoRA fine-tuning on PAUS images, relevant to photometry and photo-z.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Context. Deep photometric surveys increasingly rely on low signal-to-noise imaging, where noise affects source detection, flux measurements, photometric redshifts, and sample selection. Self-supervised denoising methods are attractive because they do not require ground-truth images, but applying them to quantitative photometry requires controlling the flux biases they may introduce. Aims. We evaluate and improve Noise2Void (N2V) for denoising real astronomical images, focusing on preserving fluxes, reducing photometric scatter, and retaining the main source features across a wide magnitude range. Methods. We use real observations from the Physics of the Accelerating Universe Survey (PAUS) together with PAUS-like simulated images. We show that standard N2V systematically underestimates galaxy fluxes, especially for faint galaxies. To mitigate this bias, we add a bounded-output layer that limits large negative outputs, and fine-tune the model with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and two flux-aware loss terms that preserve the flux scale and improve repeated-exposure flux consistency. Results. For PAUS galaxies with 22 <= i_AB < 23 in the NB645 band, the standard N2V flux-ratio distribution peaks far below unity, while the bounded-output model restores it close to the original PAUS flux scale. The flux-aware LoRA fine-tuning reduces the width of the repeated-exposure flux-difference distribution to sigma_68 = 0.529 in NB645, an effective 47% reduction in random flux scatter relative to the original PAUS uncertainty scale, while keeping the median flux ratio close to unity. Conclusions. Denoising methods for astronomical surveys must be evaluated with photometric diagnostics, not image-quality metrics alone. With flux-aware modifications, self-supervised denoising can be adapted to low-SNR survey images while preserving the flux information needed for quantitative analysis.

</details>

### [Spectroscopy-informed Deep Learning Searches for Photometric Little Red Dots in JWST Surveys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06026)

Chenxuan Zhang (HUST), Huanian Zhang (HUST), Qingwen Wu (HUST) · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06026)

**Why:** Rare object discovery: cross-modal model transfers NIRSpec spectra into NIRCam image representations to find hundreds of new Little Red Dot candidates.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Little Red Dots (LRDs) are a population of compact, red sources discovered at high redshift with JWST that exhibit distinctive V-shaped spectral energy distributions (SEDs). Existing LRD samples are typically identified using a variety of color-based selection criteria, which can lead to differences in the populations recovered by different studies. We develop a cross-modal machine-learning framework that combines matched NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy to transfer spectroscopic information into imaging-based representations, enabling LRD identification from JWST/NIRCam imaging alone. Applied to spectroscopically observed high-redshift galaxies previously classified as non-LRDs, our model identifies 35 new LRD candidates. The majority satisfy at least one independent LRD color selection criterion, and among the 13 candidates with medium- or high-resolution NIRSpec grating spectra, 10 exhibit broad H$\alpha$ emission - a hallmark of LRDs. We further apply the classifier to public NIRCam imaging from the Dawn JWST Archive and two independent JWST surveys, yielding 357 and 23 additional LRD candidates, respectively. The candidates recovered by our method span a broader range of observed SED properties than those in existing samples, suggesting that current color-selected samples may not fully capture the diversity of LRD-like systems present in JWST surveys. Our findings highlight the value of spectroscopy-informed photometric classification as a powerful strategy for systematically mining large-area JWST imaging datasets and provide a practical framework for future studies of this emerging population.

</details>

### [Euclid: Forecasting the number counts of bright line emitters in the Euclid Wide Survey and Euclid Deep fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05484)

Euclid Collaboration, P. Cassata (Dipartimento di Fisica e Astronomia "G. Galilei", Universit\`a di Padova, Via Marzolo 8 et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05484)

**Why:** Euclid mission forecasts of H-alpha, [OII] and [OIII] emitter counts via pipeline-processed mocks, setting LSS/BAO sample sizes.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Euclid mission will revolutionize our understanding of the visible Universe by collecting images and spectra of millions of galaxies across cosmic time and environments. Here, we forecast the number counts of galaxies emitting [OII], H\b{eta}, [OIII], H{\alpha} and Pa\b{eta} lines in the Euclid Wide Survey (EWS) and Deep Survey (EDS). We compare predictions from different models, including semi-analytic models and empirical scaling relations. We then simulate spectra for galaxies in the MAMBO lightcone using FastSpec, designed to rapidly generate one-dimensional spectra as observed by Euclid. We process these simulated spectra with the official Euclid spectroscopic pipeline to recover redshifts and line fluxes and derive expected counts. Predictions for H{\alpha}+[NII] emitters are broadly consistent across mocks and with existing observations. In the EWS, relative RMS scatter among mock predictions for integrated counts is approximately 19% for H{\alpha}+[NII], 44% for [OII] and 41% for [OIII], reflecting greater model dependence for oxygen-line forecasts. After accounting for flux losses and redshift misidentification, corrected flux measurements recover most intrinsic line counts, especially in the EDS. From the MAMBO-based FastSpec simulations, we predict that Euclid will detect about 42 million H{\alpha} emitters in the EWS and 1.7 million in the EDS. The same simulations predict approximately 0.5 and 0.8 million [OII] and [OIII] emitters, respectively, in the EDS, and about 1.3 and 7.5 million in the EWS. These non-H{\alpha} forecasts remain subject to substantial model-dependent uncertainties, as shown by scatter among the different mocks. These samples will be instrumental in pushing large-scale structure and baryon acoustic oscillation studies beyond redshift z~2, extending Euclid cosmological and galaxy evolution studies beyond what is achievable with H{\alpha} alone.

</details>

### [Pushing the frontiers of low-frequency radio astronomy: An ultra-deep sub-arcsecond LOFAR image of ELAIS-N1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06152)

J. M. G. H. J. de Jong, L. K. Morabito, R. J. van Weeren, J. B. R. Oonk et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06152)

**Why:** Calibration and wide-field imaging: ultra-deep sub-arcsecond LOFAR ELAIS-N1 mosaic using new long-baseline calibration, showing large-area imaging is feasible.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present ultra-deep sub-arcsecond radio images of the ELAIS-N1 field covering 2.5x2.5 deg^2, produced from 200 hours of LOw Frequency ARray (LOFAR) observations in the 115-168 MHz band. The radio continuum images are obtained using recent advances in LOFAR long-baseline calibration and imaging. We achieve a central point-source sensitivity of 5.3 $\mu$Jy/beam at an angular resolution of 0.33"x0.39" and an area-weighted sensitivity of 7.4 $\mu$Jy/beam at 0.36"x0.45", making these the deepest radio images below 1.28 GHz to date and among the deepest at any radio frequency. By combining our data with previously released deep 6" LOFAR images, we compile a catalogue with a total of 62,916 unique radio sources across 2.5x2.5 deg^2. Our calibration strategy, combined with astrometric alignment using 95 Gaia quasars, results in positional offsets of $\Delta$RA = -3 $\pm$ 27 mas and $\Delta$DEC = 2 $\pm$ 17 mas for these same sources. We further demonstrate the potential for deep polarisation studies at sub-arcsecond resolution with instrumental leakages from Stokes I into linear polarisation down to 0.12%, which is about a factor of two better than the typical leakage in the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey at 6" resolution. Our results demonstrate that imaging a large number of observations over a wide field area at sub-arcsecond resolution is now computationally feasible. This establishes a new benchmark for low-frequency radio imaging in terms of combining depth, resolution, and survey area, which lays the foundation for deeper and wider surveys in the LOFAR2.0 and SKA era.

</details>

### [Object-independent evaluation of small-data astronomical image classifiers: architecture, representation, and acquisition shortcuts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06484)

Eraldo Pereira Marinho · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06484)

**Why:** CNN and ViT on survey images: object-independent evaluation shows acquisition and context shortcuts inflate small-data classifier accuracy.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reported performance in small astronomical image datasets can reflect object reuse, field-of-view differences, acquisition settings, and catalogue-derived properties as well as astronomical morphology. We designed \dataset{} to measure these effects in a four-class task comprising galaxies, globular clusters, nebulae, and open clusters. The dataset contains 370 catalogue-supported physical objects, each represented by three Digitised Sky Survey FITS bands, and uses a group-constrained split of 222 training, 75 validation, and 73 test objects. We compared a custom CNN--Transformer trained from scratch with ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 under an adaptive full-field representation, a common angular field, and two central-region-masked controls, using three fixed seeds. In the one-pass object-independent test, mean balanced accuracy on the adaptive view was $0.773\pm0.014$, $0.874\pm0.054$, and $0.866\pm0.023$ for the custom model, ResNet-50, and ViT-B/16, respectively. No primary architecture contrast rejected its null after Holm correction; ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 differed by 0.007 balanced-accuracy points (95\% cluster-bootstrap interval $[-0.048,0.062]$). Controls based on acquisition and catalogue metadata reached 0.721--0.735, and central-region-masked adaptive views reached 0.731--0.800. These results demonstrate generalisation to unseen physical objects, but also show that image context, acquisition settings, and catalogue-derived object properties carry substantial class information. In this regime, high accuracy cannot be interpreted as evidence that a classifier learned target morphology, and the comparison does not isolate architecture from pretraining.

</details>

### [Euclid. Predicting scaling relations for emission-line galaxies using simulated data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05482)

Euclid Collaboration, C. Mancini (INAF-IASF Milano, Via Alfonso Corti 12, 20133 Milano et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05482)

**Why:** Euclid spectroscopic science forecasts: recovering main sequence, dust and metallicity relations from simulated Euclid emission-line data.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Euclid spectroscopic surveys will substantially increase the number of observed emission-line galaxies. We assess the ability of the Euclid Wide Survey (EWS) and Euclid Deep Survey (EDS), at the final DR3 depth, to recover the star-forming main sequence (MS), dust-mass relation (DMR), and mass-metallicity relation (MZR) from emission-line measurements. We use MAMBO (mocks with abundance matching in Bologna) spectroscopic simulations processed with the official Euclid pipelines. The MS is traced over $0\leq z\leq 4$ using Pa$\beta$, H$\alpha$, and [OII] as SFR tracers. The shallow EWS flux limit biases the MS towards higher normalisation and a flatter slope by selecting the brightest, most strongly star-forming galaxies. The deeper EDS enables almost complete sampling with Pa$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ at log$_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)\geq10.3$ over $0\leq z\leq 1.88$, with mild biases at both redshift limits. The [OII]-based MS is increasingly biased towards higher SFRs at the highest redshifts. The DMR is traced through the Balmer decrement over $1.45\leq z\leq 1.88$ and $0.9\leq z\leq 1.88$ in the EWS and EDS, respectively. The derived colour excess agree well with the input values, although detecting the faint H$\beta$ line restricts the sample to less obscured galaxies at fixed $M_*$, particularly in the EWS; the DMR is better sampled in the EDS. Finally, a golden sample detected in multiple optical rest-frame emission lines at $1.49\leq z\leq 1.88$ recovers gas-phase metallicities with typical accuracy of 0.1 dex at log$_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)\geq10$, while lower-mass galaxies show biases up to 0.15 dex. Relative to H-band limited sample, this sample preferentially traces the upper MS and lower MZR. Despite the simplified simulations lacking effects such as mutual spectral contamination and detector persistence, these forecasts provide a benchmark for future Euclid spectroscopic analyses.

</details>
