### [Confirming HSC strong lens candidates with DESI Spectroscopy. II. Analysis of 80 galaxy-scale systems with lens redshifts above 0.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07524)

Shen Li, Yiping Shu, Lin Nie · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07524)

**Why:** Strong lensing: lens modeling confirms 71 secure HSC galaxy-scale lenses at z>0.8, nearly tripling the known high-z lens sample.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The total mass distribution of galaxies provides a sensitive probe of the fundamental physical processes governing galaxy evolution, including gas inflow/star formation, AGN/SN feedback, galaxy merging, and the nature of dark matter. State-of-the-art cosmological simulations have therefore made extensive predictions for the evolution of the total-mass density slope. Interestingly, their predictions diverge significantly at high redshift, primarily due to differences in how these key physical mechanisms are implemented. However, measurements of the total-mass structure evolution have been largely confined to low and intermediate redshifts, leaving the regime where model predictions differ most significantly (z>0.8) largely unexplored. In the first paper of this series, we reported the discovery of 84 candidate strong-lens systems with lens redshifts above 0.8. In this work, we perform lens modeling of the 80 galaxy-scale systems to assess their lensing nature. Of these, 71 systems (approximately 90%) exhibit clear lensing features that are well reproduced by our lens models and are therefore classified as secure strong lenses. Another eight systems are classified as probable (5) or possible (3) strong lenses, based on the prominence of their lensing features and the quality of the lens modeling. One system lacking reliable lensing features in current imaging data is classified as a non-lens. Our analysis nearly triples the number of known galaxy-scale strong lenses at z_lens > 0.8, making statistical studies of the total mass structure of high-redshift galaxies possible for the first time. This expanded sample provides an unprecedented opportunity to test the predictions of cosmological simulations and discriminate between competing physical prescriptions.

</details>

### [GradFlex: a Gradient-Based Method for Weak Lensing Flexion Measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07824)

Jiarui Sun, Jun Zhang, Toshifumi Futamase · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07824)

**Why:** Weak lensing methods: GradFlex measures flexion from surface-brightness gradients without assuming PSF or galaxy morphology models.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Flexion, the second-order gravitational lensing distortion, probes small-scale mass distribution and complement the cosmic shear measurement. However, its detection is highly challenging due to the faint signal and the contamination arising from the intrinsic galaxy morphology. In this Letter, we present GradFlex, a new method that extracts flexion from the gradient field of galaxy surface brightness distributions without assuming a specific point-spread function model or galaxy morphology. Using surface-brightness conservation, we relate the pre- and post-lensing gradient fields and derive flexion estimators from ensemble-averaged statistics of the corresponding spins. Tests on numerical simulations across diverse galaxy morphologies and multiple noise realizations demonstrate its reliability under realistic observing conditions. Combined with shear measurements, it could tighten constraints on cluster density profiles and enable more detailed studies of substructure than shear alone.

</details>

### [Detection of Differential Chromatic Refraction with LSST Camera data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07161)

Matthew J. Temple, Phoebe N. Lister · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07161)

**Why:** Photometric redshifts: first detection of differential chromatic refraction in LSST commissioning data, used to help break photo-z degeneracies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Earth's atmosphere acts like a prism, deflecting blue light towards the zenith more than red light. Airmass-dependent changes in astrometry can therefore be used to infer additional information about the wavelength distribution of light within a photometric passband. The Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will observe each point in the southern sky several hundred times, with observations spread across a range of airmasses. Using commissioning data from the LSST camera released in LSST Data Preview 2, we report the detection of this differential chromatic refraction (DCR) effect in observations of galaxies and quasars in the COSMOS field. We show how information from DCR can complement the six-band colour information from LSST and help break degeneracies when selecting extragalactic samples and estimating their photometric redshifts. DCR will be a valuable resource for scientific studies and care should be taken not to remove useful DCR information from future LSST data releases.

</details>

### [Is Morphology All You Need? Quantifying Imprints of Past Star Formation in the Present-Day Morphologies of Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07373)

Juan Pablo Alfonzo, Kartheik G. Iyer, Masayuki Akiyama, Camilla Pacifici et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07373)

**Why:** ML on survey imaging and photo-z: Katachi-PRIME uses gri image morphology priors with implicit likelihood inference to sharpen redshift and SED properties.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The next generation of galaxy surveys will bring a plethora of new observations, a vast majority of which will be observed with a few photometric broadbands, limiting the information we can infer from traditional SED fitting methods. To maximize the scientific value of these surveys, techniques that can go beyond traditional summary statistics in extracting information from galaxy observations are needed. Moving beyond integrated photometry, we introduce the Katachi-PRIME method to do spectral energy distribution (SED) based inference that jointly incorporates morphological and SED information. Katachi-PRIME uses implicit likelihood inference (ILI) to incorporate realistic morphological priors from gri broadband images, to make predictions of: stellar mass (M$_*$), specific star formation rate (sSFR), redshift (z), age (t$_{50}$), dust attenuation (Av), and gas-phase metallicity (12+log(O/H)) of the galaxy. We find that this improves constraints compared to traditional 5-band photometry. We then use the learned morphological priors to examine the extent to which morphology encodes information about past star formation, finding a stark dichotomy in how morphology encodes star formation history. While the stable morphologies of main-sequence and quiescent galaxies can predict past star formation out to $\sim 4-7$ Gyr and $\sim3-5$ Gyr, respectively, starburst and green-valley populations above and below the main sequence tend to experience structural disruptions that obscure all but the most recent star formation activity. Methods like Katachi-PRIME maximize the scientific value of future telescopes by providing broad-baseline SED-level property estimates by incorporating morphological information.

</details>

### [Probing the Seed Effect of Supermassive Primordial Black Holes with Weak Gravitational Lensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07574)

Lei Zu, Kazunori Kohri · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07574)

**Why:** Weak lensing: DES Y3 cosmic shear constrains supermassive primordial black hole seeds; LSST forecast gives ~100x improvement.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We investigate the cosmological implications of supermassive primordial black holes (PBHs) as a component of dark matter (DM), focusing on their seed effect on large-scale structure formation. In contrast to the Poisson effect associated with the discrete distribution of PBHs, the seed effect arises because PBHs act as early gravitational seeds, accreting surrounding DM and developing dense DM spikes to form dressed PBHs. These structures enhance the matter power spectrum, particularly on small scales. We model their contribution within the halo-model framework under two prescriptions for DM accretion: an optimistic scenario (Case 1) and a conservative scenario (Case 2), which predict different modifications to the 1-halo term. We constrain the PBH abundance using weak-lensing measurements from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3), performing a Markov Chain Monte Carlo analysis to derive upper limits on the PBH fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$, as a function of the PBH mass $M_{\rm PBH}$. We find that weak lensing places competitive constraints on supermassive PBHs, with the upper limits on $f_{\rm PBH}$ becoming progressively stronger toward higher PBH masses. Case 1, which predicts more efficient DM accretion and hence a larger enhancement of the matter power spectrum, yields stronger constraints than Case 2; in particular, it excludes $f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{-4}$ for $M_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{10} M_\odot$. We further forecast the sensitivity of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) using mock weak-lensing data and find that the constraints could improve by approximately two orders of magnitude. Our results establish weak gravitational lensing as a sensitive probe of the seed effect of supermassive PBHs and demonstrate its potential to complement other cosmological and astrophysical searches.

</details>
