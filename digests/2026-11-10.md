### [Forward modeling linear variable filter data: High resolution PAH and Paschen-$\alpha$ maps from SPHEREx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12846)

Andrew K. Saydjari, Connor Hainje, Benjamin Dodge, Philipp Frank et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12846)

**Why:** SPHEREx methods: scalable forward modeling of linear-variable-filter data yields stripe-free all-sky emission-line maps with GP infilling.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission is a powerful tool for the study of the interstellar medium (ISM) in both Galactic and extragalactic contexts. It can reveal ionization state, radiation field, metallicity, composition, and ISM structure as sculpted by processes like turbulence and stellar feedback. Here we use NASA's SPHEREx to produce clean, high-resolution PAH maps at spectral resolution R $\sim$ 35 (compared to WISE at R $\sim$ 2). Prior treatments of the SPHEREx linear variable filter data seek to convert them to more conventional band-averaged maps that can leave striping artifacts associated with spatially variable wavelength sampling, especially at emission lines. In contrast, we deliver a scalable forward-modeling method capable of generating high-quality all-sky SPHEREx emission-line maps at 8.5 arcsec FWHM angular resolution (on 6.4 arcsec pixels), while naturally producing maps without visible striping. We reformat the data into a pixel-level database, mask stellar contamination, and infill missing pixels with a conditional, data-driven, anisotropic Gaussian process. We apply our method to produce 3.3 $\mu$m and 3.4 $\mu$m PAH and Paschen-$\alpha$ emission maps of a set of fields spanning a range of Galactic and extragalactic conditions including the Rosette nebula, the Corona Australis molecular cloud, M33, and M31. We demonstrate the quality and synergies of our SPHEREx 3.3 $\mu$m PAH maps by comparisons with other available gas and dust tracer maps and achieve sensitivities up to $\sigma(I_{3.3}) = 2.7 \times 10^{-8}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ sr$^{-1}$. We estimate that a two-year extension of the SPHEREx survey, with modest modifications to the scanning strategy, would match or exceed the PAH sensitivity of WISE over the whole sky.

</details>

### [A DOLPHOT/Euclid Study of the Resolved, Faint Outer Milky Way Globular Cluster AM-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10127)

Daniel R. Weisz, Alessandro Savino, Andrew E. Dolphin, Meredith J. Durbin et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10127)

**Why:** Euclid methods: new DOLPHOT Euclid module with empirical VIS/NISP PSFs and calibration from Q1 imaging.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present a resolved star study of AM-1, a distant ($D \simeq 120$ kpc), faint ($M_V \sim -5.1$) Milky Way (MW) globular cluster, based on Euclid Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) imaging. To perform photometry of the Euclid VIS and NISP imaging, we develop a Euclid-specific module for the crowded field stellar photometry package DOLPHOT. We construct and present empirical point spread function (PSF) models for VIS and NISP, along with a photometric calibration, entirely from the public Q1 images. Our photometry reaches $5\sigma$ point source depths of $I_E, Y_E, J_E, H_E = 27.1, 25.3, 25.5, 25.3$, $\sim$1-1.5 mag deeper than the public Q1 catalog, as expected for dedicated resolved star PSF photometry. We resolve the cluster to $\sim$1 mag below its main sequence turnoff in all bands. The Euclid photometry agrees with color-magnitude diagram (CMD) modeling of archival HST/WFPC2 imaging and with the 2MASS-anchored VISTA Hemisphere Survey to $\lesssim0.02$ mag in $I_E$, $J_E$, and $H_E$, with a $\sim$0.07 mag offset in $Y_E$. We find that AM-1 hosts an old ($\sim$10.6 Gyr), intermediate-metallicity ([Fe/H] $\sim -1$) population, with a present-day stellar mass of $\sim 2\times10^4\,M_\odot$ and $M/L_V \simeq 2.2$, and has a half-light radius of $\sim$12-16 pc with a round, low-concentration profile. These affirm earlier studies placing AM-1 among the most extended, diffuse outer-halo clusters, which dynamical work identifies as accreted. We describe and publicly release DOLPHOT v2.1, the Euclid module, and its empirical PSFs, which together enable Euclid resolved star studies ranging from faint MW systems such as AM-1 to more luminous galaxies throughout the Local Volume. We anticipate further refinements, including spatially varying PSFs, with Euclid Data Release 1.

</details>

### [FAIR Universe Weak Lensing ML Uncertainty Challenge: Dataset and Cosmological Inference Competition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10228)

Biwei Dai, Po-Wen Chang, Erwan Allys, Wahid Bhimji et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10228)

**Why:** Weak lensing ML benchmark; winning solutions include Vision Transformers for cosmological inference under systematics.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Weak gravitational lensing is a powerful cosmological probe that enables direct mapping of the matter distribution and precise constraints on the fundamental parameters governing structure formation in the universe. In recent years, machine learning has shown strong potential for extracting cosmological information from weak-lensing data beyond traditional summary statistics. However, progress has been limited by the lack of standardized benchmarks that capture the complexity of the dataset, simulator imperfections, and various systematic biases encountered in realistic analyses. To address this gap, we present the FAIR Universe Weak Lensing Machine Learning Uncertainty Challenge, one of the NeurIPS 2025 competitions, as a benchmark dataset and evaluation study. Our dataset reflects realistic weak-lensing observations, incorporating multiple systematic biases, and thereby providing a controlled testbed for robust cosmological inference under uncertainties. We summarize the main methodological advances developed by the winners of our challenge, including data augmentation with conditional optimal transport, an AI-agent-driven approach for parameter inference, ensemble learning, Vision Transformer-based architectures, and physics-informed feature integration. By unifying the benchmark dataset and the winning solutions, this work provides a community resource for developing and evaluating machine learning methods for weak lensing cosmology and, more broadly, for scientific inference under systematic uncertainties.

</details>

### [Optimal summary statistics to constrain w0waCDM with the DESI BGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10084)

Simon Bouchard · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10084)

**Why:** Large-scale structure clustering: simulation-based non-Gaussian summary statistics improve DESI BGS w0wa constraints.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) is the first stage-4 spectroscopic instrument installed on the 4-m Mayall Telescope at Kitt Peak National Observatory in Arizona. It contains 5000 robotically positioned fibers, allowing the measurement of as many galaxy spectra in one observation (~20 minutes). The data acquisition (2021--2028) aims at constraining the cosmological parameters, and especially the parameters describing the evolution of the dark energy with time. However, standard clustering analysis based on 2-point statistics only capture the Gaussian information of the matter density field, while the analytical models used to match these statistical probe are limited to quasi-linear scales, as they are based on perturbation theory. Therefore, the non-Gaussian and non-linear information of the density field is not exploited. To go beyond these limitations, several alternative clustering methods have been developed that rely on a simulation-based pipeline. In this presentation, we focus on the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey, a magnitude-limited sample of bright galaxies in the redshift range of 0.1 < z < 0.4. We present how accessing the non-linear and non-Gaussian information of the density field allows around 40% improvement on the constraints of some $\Lambda$CDM parameters. We are also extending the method to constrain w0waCDM in order to improve DESI's sensibility at low redshift where the Universe is dominated by dark energy.

</details>

### [Detecting Baryon Acoustic Oscillations from Galaxy Clusters: Distance Constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11587)

Xiaoyun Shao, Paula S. Ferreira, Carlos A. P. Bengaly, Rodrigo S. Gon\c{c}alves et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11587)

**Why:** Clustering: BAO detected in 170k Legacy Survey galaxy clusters, giving distance and dark energy constraints.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present a measurement of the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) feature using the largest galaxy cluster sample with spectroscopic redshift information to date, comprising 169,679 clusters selected from the WH24 catalogue based on the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. We measure the two-point correlation function in two redshift bins, $0.17\leq z<0.35$ and $0.35\leq z<0.5$, corresponding to effective redshifts of $z_{\rm eff}=0.25$ and $0.43$, with the BAO feature detected at approximately the $4\sigma$ level or above in both bins. By fitting the measured correlation functions, we obtain dilation parameters of $\alpha=0.97\pm0.07$ and $\alpha=1.01\pm0.04$, corresponding to volume-averaged distance measurements of $D_V/r_d=6.70\pm0.48$ and $D_V/r_d=11.25\pm0.47$, respectively. Using the Planck 2018 sound horizon calibration, we obtain $H_0=66.6 \pm 4.9 \,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ within the flat $\Lambda$CDM framework. We further combine our cluster BAO measurements with cosmic microwave background and Pantheon+ Type Ia supernova observations to constrain dynamical dark energy within the Chevallier--Polarski--Linder parametrization. The resulting constraints are consistent with $\Lambda$CDM, which lies within the $68\%$ joint credible region in the $w_0$--$w_a$ plane. Our results demonstrate the potential of galaxy clusters as complementary tracers for BAO measurements and cosmological constraints.

</details>
