### [First constraints on axion-like particles with SPHEREx data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13090)

Connor Hainje, Elena Pinetti, David W. Hogg · `hep-ph` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13090)

**Why:** SPHEREx science: first axion-like particle dark matter search with 16.5 months of SPHEREx spectral data, competitive limits.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present the first search for axion-like particle dark matter using SPHEREx data. We perform a conservative analysis on the first 16.5 months of SPHEREx data and derive new limits on the mass range $0.496 < m_a < 3.32\,\mathrm{eV}$ that are competitive with the literature. Our tightest limit on the coupling relative to those previously published is $g_{a\gamma\gamma} < 2.10 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$ at $m_a = 0.606\,\mathrm{eV}$, with a median limit of $g_{a\gamma\gamma} < 4.16 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$. Cast as model-independent limits on the lifetime of the dark matter particle, the median limit is $\tau > 6.5 \times 10^{25}\,\mathrm{s}$. We are also able to detect several expected emission features at high significance, such as atmospheric He I and the 3.3 $\mu$m PAH feature. We calculate the Cram\'er-Rao bound and compare to sensitivity forecasts, finding that our limits could be improved by more than an order of magnitude with solely methodological improvements.

</details>

### [The PAU Survey: Dissecting strong-lensing systems with narrow-band photometric redshifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14229)

Shuaibo Geng, Pablo Renard, Qiuhan He, David Navarro-Giron\'es et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14229)

**Why:** Strong lensing + photo-z: deblended narrow-band PAUS redshifts for lens and source components, aimed at Euclid lens candidate vetting.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Wide-field imaging surveys such as Euclid will identify large samples of strong-lens candidates, whose scientific use requires redshifts for both deflectors and lensed sources. Source redshifts are challenging because faint arcs are blended with foreground light. We test lens-model-informed photometry on eight known strong lenses, using broad-band models to define component regions, point-reflection subtraction to reduce contamination, and PAU Survey narrow bands combined with five HSC or CFHT broad bands. BCNz estimates separate lens and source redshift distributions without imposing their expected ordering. All eight lens redshift peaks agree with spectroscopy within $|\delta_z|\leq0.014$, where $\delta_z=(z_b-z_{\rm spec})/(1+z_{\rm spec})$. Four source peaks, all at $z_{\rm s}^{\rm spec}\leq1.23$, agree within $|\delta_z|\leq0.025$. Symmetric subtraction reduces the median source $|\delta_z|$ from 0.336 to 0.071. At $|\delta_z|<0.05$, narrow- plus broad-band photometry yields eight lens and four source matches, versus three and two using EAZY with broad bands alone. These results demonstrate component redshift recovery in blended systems and support redshift-based lens-candidate assessment alongside morphology and colour. Larger narrow-band surveys, including J-PAS, could extend this approach, pending uncertainty calibration and blind candidate tests.

</details>

### [SHINE: Shear inference environment - Generative forward modelling for radio weak gravitational lensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14301)

Ezequiel Centofanti, Benjamin Remy, Emma Ay\c{c}oberry, Fran\c{c}ois Lanusse et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14301)

**Why:** Weak lensing: generative forward-model shear inference for radio interferometers, with autoencoder galaxy priors removing morphology bias.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Next-generation radio interferometers, such as SKA, will observe the radio sky with unprecedented sensitivity and resolution enabling weak gravitational lensing studies using radio data, which not only complement optical observations but also probe higher redshifts. However, traditional shear estimation methods, based on measuring the ellipticity of observed galaxies, are not directly applicable to radio interferometric data. Interferometric observations are acquired in the Fourier domain, where galaxy images are delocalised, making shape measurements non-trivial. Furthermore, deconvolution methods such as CLEAN can introduce systematic errors when converting observations to the image domain, biasing shear estimates. To address these challenges, we propose a cosmic shear inference method based on forward generative modelling, which avoids explicit shape measurements. Our forward model simulates galaxy images, applies the shear transformation and, by including the instrumental response of the radio interferometer, simulates the visibilities of observed galaxies, with the likelihood explicitly defined in Fourier space. Using Monte Carlo sampling algorithms, we estimate the posterior distribution of the cosmic shear conditioned on a set of galaxy observations that share a common shear. We obtain unbiased shear estimates with sub-percent accuracy when applied to parametric galaxy simulations. We compare our method to state-of-the-art shear estimators, demonstrating a reduction in uncertainty of up to 3 times and no apparent multiplicative bias. We then show that applying this parametric model to COSMOS galaxies with realistic morphologies introduces a bias in the shear estimate over 10{\sigma}. Finally, we demonstrate that, by incorporating a generative forward model based on an autoencoder architecture trained on real galaxy images, we can mitigate this bias to below the 2{\sigma} level.

</details>

### [Connecting field-level and n-point function measurements of galaxy bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14456)

Hsiang-Ming Huang, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14456)

**Why:** Large-scale structure: uses renormalization group to connect field-level and n-point galaxy bias measurements across cutoffs.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Precise measurements of galaxy bias parameters are essential for extracting cosmological information from galaxy surveys. Field-level analyses infer bias coefficients at a finite cutoff $\Lambda$, typically related to the lattice resolution, while $n$-point function analyses typically adopt renormalization conditions defined in the low-$k$ limit. In this work, we use the renormalization group (RG) to relate field-level measurements at different cutoffs and connect them to $n$-point function measurements. We generate mock galaxy fields with a Lagrangian perturbation theory forward model and a galaxy bias expansion, and then measure the bias parameters across a range of cutoffs. We show that the RG predictions describe the measured running and, in the limit $\Lambda\to 0$, agree with the (tree-level) $n$-point function measurements of first-order $b_\delta$ and second-order $b_{\delta^2}, b_{{G}_2}$. We also measure the running on halo catalogs based on full $N$-body simulations and show that the RG prediction describes the measurements well within the predicted systematic errors. Our results establish the RG flow as a natural formulation for relating galaxy bias measurements at different cutoffs and connecting the schemes used in field-level and $n$-point function analyses.

</details>

### [DustER: Dust Extinction with Photometric Red Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14280)

Ivan Espinoza-Bustamante, Eli S. Rykoff, Eduardo Rozo, Chun-Hao To et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14280)

**Why:** Survey systematics: dust reddening maps from red-sequence galaxies with photo-z bias correction, validated on clustering.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present DustER, a new method for reconstructing dust reddening maps from photometric galaxy survey data. DustER uses red galaxies as "standard crayons". That is, it relies on the mean color of red-sequence galaxies being independent of their position in the sky. Given the galaxy's redshift, the difference between a galaxy's observed color and its expected color measures the reddening by dust along the galaxy's line of sight. When using photometric galaxy survey data, the method must rely on photometric redshifts, which, combined with selection effects, bias the recovered dust maps. DustER uses a novel approach to characterize and correct for these biases by leveraging the known statistical properties of the distribution of dust around the Milky Way. Testing on simulations, we find that the recovered dust maps are contaminated by large-scale structure. However, when we use the DustER map to deredden and select red-sequence galaxies, we find the resulting two-point function is not contaminated by dust.

</details>
