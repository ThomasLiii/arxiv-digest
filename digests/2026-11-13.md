### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) A: Strong lens catalogue overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15883)

Euclid Collaboration, N. E. P. Lines (Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, Portsmouth PO1 3FX et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15883)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 strong lensing: 15,072 high-confidence lens candidates found with ML ensemble plus citizen science, largest catalogue ever.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present 15 072 high-confidence strong lens candidates in Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1), spanning 2000 deg$^2$. These comprise 6073 grade A ('sure lens') and 8999 grade B ('probable lens') systems, which we publish alongside a further 9010 grade C ('possible lens') candidates. With more high-confidence candidates than all previous catalogues combined, this sample establishes a large, homogeneous data set of galaxy-scale strong lenses with high-resolution imaging, transforming strong lensing from a field of rare discoveries to a robust statistical tool for astrophysics and cosmology. Of the roughly 900 million sources in DR1, we searched the 68 million passing our catalogue selection using a bespoke discovery pipeline that synthesises scores from five machine-learning models with visual classifications from around 7000 citizen scientists and 67 strong lensing experts. Beyond classical Einstein rings, our catalogue includes 84 candidate double-source-plane lenses and over 1000 candidate disc lenses, two exotic classes with few previously known examples, as well as several hundred candidate lensed AGN and quasars. We deliver automated lens models for the combined grade A+B systems, and photometric redshifts and stellar population properties for the deflector galaxies, providing immediate science-ready data products. We find that the majority of the deflector galaxies are massive and quiescent, with stellar masses of $\log_{10}(M_\ast /M_\odot) = 11.39^{+0.29}_{-0.33}$ (Salpeter initial mass function; 16th-84th percentile range). The deflector galaxies span a range of photometric redshifts ($z=0.68 ^{+0.33}_{-0.29}$), extending to higher redshifts than previous ground-based lens samples, and the lenses have a typical Einstein radius of $0.85 ^{+0.44}_{-0.24}$ arcsec. Extrapolating by area, we expect over 100 000 high-confidence strong lens candidates in the Euclid Wide Survey.

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) B: Discovery pipeline overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15886)

Euclid Collaboration, P. Holloway (Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, Portsmouth PO1 3FX et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15886)

**Why:** Strong lens detection with ML in Euclid DR1: multi-stage neural network, citizen-science and expert pipeline with measured completeness.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present the method used to identify the $15\,000$ high-grade galaxy-galaxy strong lens candidates in Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Given that lenses are intrinsically rare systems and the DR1 data set is vast, this lens search was conducted in a number of stages: a catalogue-level cut; a machine-learning and ensemble search; a two-stage citizen-science search, and finally grading of lens candidates by strong lens researchers. In this paper, we describe each search stage and their respective results. This search was enabled by the automated classification of over 68 million objects by neural networks, more than 4.5 million classifications of $300\,000$ objects by citizen-scientist volunteers, and the inspection of over $30\,000$ objects by strong lens researchers. We find this multi-stage approach helps maximise the number of lenses identified, while minimising inspection requirements of both citizen scientists and strong lens researchers. We measure a completeness in our comprehensive search of $0.73_{-0.15}^{+0.08}$ to grade A ('definite') systems and $0.34_{-0.06}^{+0.05}$ to grade A+B ('definite' + 'probable') systems. Our search therefore identifies the majority of the definitive strong lens systems in the DR1 area with the remainder likely to be fainter or more ambiguous cases. We find that researcher grading remains subjective; in this work we implement calibration to account for biases of individual inspectors and highlight the benefit of lens modelling (ECLIPSE-C) in determining the nature of a lens candidate. This paper forms part of the ECLIPSE series, here focussing on the discovery pipeline used to identify lens candidates in Euclid DR1; an overview of the lenses discovered is presented in ECLIPSE-A.

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Colouring Euclid with external ground-based imaging data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16533)

Euclid Collaboration, P. Corcho-Caballero (Kapteyn Astronomical Institute, University of Groningen, PO Box 800 et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16533)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 and self-calibration/mosaicking: calibrated co-addition of ground-based imaging with photometric homogeneity validation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Accurate photometric-redshift estimation and calibration of the weak-lensing point-spread function (PSF) for Euclid require complementary ground-based optical imaging with high photometric precision and spatial homogeneity. The Organisation Unit for External Data (OU-EXT) integrates heterogeneous ground-based surveys into the Euclid Science Ground Segment (SGS), delivering uniformly calibrated, co-added images, and associated PSF models. To minimise systematics in spectral energy distribution (SED) reconstruction and photometric-redshift estimation, these data must achieve relative photometric stability at the $\sim$10 mmag level on Euclid-tile scales. We present the ground-based imaging component of Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1), comprising $\sim2500$ deg$^2$ across the wide, deep, and auxiliary fields. We summarise the OU-EXT production workflow and validation framework, and quantify the achieved data quality of the co-added products in terms of depth, photometric zero-point stability, and spatial colour homogeneity. In the Euclid Wide Survey (EWS), the point-source $10\sigma$ depths in the $griz$ bands reach 24.8/24.3/24.0/23.0 AB mag, with the deep fields extending 1--2 magnitudes deeper. Most EWS DR1 tiles meet the 10 mmag relative photometric requirement, with 1.1% and 1.5% showing offsets exceeding 50 mmag in the northern and southern hemispheres, respectively. Spatial colour variations remain typically below 5--10 mmag across the southern EWS footprint, while larger residual structures of 10--25 mmag appear in some northern regions. These results demonstrate that the EXT production chain provides a largely homogeneous framework for integrating ground-based datasets into Euclid, while identifying calibration and co-addition limitations that have to be addressed in DR2 to meet the mission's most stringent cosmological requirements. ### [A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of an Astronomy Foundation Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15653)

Rachel C. Zhang, Alberto Bietti, Wes Gurnee, Alessandro Favero et al. · `cs.LG` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15653)

**Why:** Multimodal foundation models: first causal mechanistic interpretability study of AION-1, localizing its image-plus-photometry redshift advantage.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Foundation models are increasingly used across the sciences, but whether their internal computations correspond to known scientific operations or proceed through different mechanisms remains unclear. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for assessing reliability, identifying failure modes, and learning new science from the models. We present, to our knowledge, the first causal mechanistic interpretability study of an astronomy foundation model, AION-1, focusing on its multimodal advantage in redshift prediction, where combining imaging with photometry outperforms photometry alone. We find that this advantage is largest for spatially extended and dust-attenuated galaxies, and that most of it is localized to the first of the encoder's twelve blocks. Within that block, how well a property is linearly decodable does not predict whether it can be localized. Spatial extent is causally used, and one attention head dominates linear probing for it, yet ablating that head has negligible effect on redshift prediction: the information is distributed across heads and is decodable again one layer after we erase it. Dust attenuation shows the opposite pattern: it is only weakly decodable anywhere in the encoder, yet a single head is necessary for most of the multimodal advantage, particularly for the most dust-attenuated galaxies, and keeping that head as the only active head in its block preserves nearly all of the advantage. None of the astrophysically motivated operations we tested explains what this head computes. Whether this reflects a limitation of current interpretability methods or a computation that does not correspond to any known astrophysical operation remains open, and resolving it is a prerequisite for using interpretability as a tool for scientific discovery.

</details>

### [CosmoSim: Lens-parameter estimation of the Einstein Cross NGC 6505 using machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14678)

Ben David Normann, Oda Hjemli, Hans Georg Schaathun · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14678)

**Why:** Strong lensing with ML: CNN trained on CosmoSim simulations estimates lens parameters for real Euclid and AGEL lenses.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

CosmoSim is an open-source tool for simulating gravitational lenses and reconstructing lens mass distributions, either by ray tracing a parametric lens model or through a Roulette expansion of the lens potential. It is also used to generate the large, labelled synthetic image sets needed to train machine-learning (ML) parameter estimators. We present the first application of CosmoSim to real data. A convolutional neural network trained only on CosmoSim images estimates Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid lens and source parameters directly from observed images. We test the pipeline on 21 images of 19 strong lenses from Euclid and the AGEL survey, after removing the foreground lens light with a custom decontamination tool built on AstroPy. As a case study we reconstruct the Einstein Cross NGC 6505 both from the lens model published by the discovery team and from parameters predicted by our ML model. Both reconstructions recover the observed four-image structure, and the ML-based reconstruction performs at least as well as the literature-based one: it scores better on masked structural similarity, intersection-over-union and Chamfer distance, with an essentially equal Hausdorff distance. Across the ensemble, the predicted Einstein radii are consistent with catalogued values within the quoted uncertainties (mean relative difference 0.08 for Euclid and 0.19 for AGEL lenses), and the enclosed masses agree to within a median of 0.12 dex. The predicted ellipticities differ systematically from the catalogued values, while the comparison of orientations is inconclusive. These results establish CosmoSim as a tool that can be applied directly to observational data.

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) C: Lens modelling pipeline overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15888)

Euclid Collaboration, J. W. Nightingale, S. H. Vincken, L. R. Ecker et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15888)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 strong lensing: automated PyAutoLens-JAX modelling of 15k candidates with source reconstructions and deblended photometry.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Galaxy-scale strong lenses provide precise measurements of galaxy mass distributions, magnified views of distant galaxies, and a competitive cosmological probe. Historically, such studies have been limited to hundreds of lenses, with automated modelling methods proving difficult to scale. We present automated lens modelling of 15 072 strong-lens candidates from Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1), over an order of magnitude larger than any previously modelled sample. Using PyAutoLens-JAX, we perform three fits with multi-Gaussian expansion (MGE), pixelised, and S\'ersic representations of the source, adopting an isothermal ellipsoid mass model with external shear. These fits provide foreground lens-light subtraction, adaptive Delaunay source reconstructions, and deblended photometry. More than 20 expert inspectors visually assess the resulting models, and each model is assigned the classification on which at least 75% of its inspectors agree. Of the 6073 grade A (secure) lens candidates, inspectors classify 4098 (67.5%) as successfully modelled, 580 (9.6%) as failures, 317 (5.2%) as multi-galaxy lenses, 15 (0.2%) as double-source-plane lenses, and 7 (0.1%) as unsure if lens, while 1032 (17.0%) reach no consensus and 24 (0.4%) have incomplete models. For the 8999 grade B (probable) candidates, the modelling success rate falls to 43.1%, primarily reflecting an increase in cases without consensus (37.6%), rather than a comparable increase in agreed failures (12.9%). Successfully modelled grade A and B systems have a median effective Einstein radius of 0.85 arcsec and a median total $I_E$-band source magnification of 8.1. We publicly release the successful lens models, their inferred properties, and magnified-source reconstructions through the ECLIPSE catalogue, demonstrating automated modelling at Euclid sample sizes and enabling population-level strong-lensing science.

### [Mitigating variance shrinkage in multi-task galaxy property inference from imaging data via statistical manifold alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16598)

Zhijian Luo, Jianzhen Chen · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16598)

**Why:** Vision transformer on HSC survey imaging infers photo-z and stellar properties, with MMD loss fixing variance shrinkage.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Inferring photometric redshifts ($z$) and key stellar population properties ($\log(M_*/\mathrm{M}_\odot)$, $D_n4000$, and $\mathrm{H}\delta_A$) directly from multi-band imaging is increasingly critical for spectroscopically limited large surveys such as LSST and CSST. However, conventional point-wise loss functions (e.g., mean squared error or Huber loss) employed in deep neural networks often induce ``variance shrinkage''---a regression-to-the-mean effect that contracts predicted variances, distorts non-linear physical manifolds (such as bimodal distributions), and truncates heavy-tail populations. Here, we present ViTMultiTask, a vision transformer framework that incorporates a multi-scale maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) loss to enforce higher-order joint distribution alignment in reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). Trained on 83,846 galaxies from Subaru HSC five-band ($g,r,i,z,y$) imaging cross-matched with DESI Data Release 1 Bright Galaxy Survey spectra, the model achieves high point-estimate precision on an independent test set of 5,000 galaxies: $\sigma_{\mathrm{NMAD}} = 0.020$ for $z$, $0.169\,\mathrm{dex}$ for $\log(M_*/\mathrm{M}_\odot)$, $0.073$ for $D_n4000$, and $1.185\,\text{\r{A}}$ for $\mathrm{H}\delta_A$. Ablation experiments demonstrate that incorporating the MMD loss effectively mitigates variance shrinkage and more accurately reproduces the observed spectroscopic parameter distributions (including bimodal structures and heavy tails), while maintaining point-estimate accuracy statistically consistent with the baseline (RMSE changes $\le 3.5\%$) and catastrophic outlier rates fully comparable to the baseline. This kernel-based manifold alignment offers a statistically grounded and practical paradigm for comprehensive galaxy property inference in the deep imaging era.

### [Can we constrain cosmology with compound gravitational lenses?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15714)

Duncan J. Bowden, Tian Li, Aprajita Verma, Thomas E. Collett et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15714)

**Why:** Strong lensing and Euclid: quantifies cosmology biases from double-source-plane lenses in mock Euclid samples.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Compound lensing in double-source-plane lenses (DSPLs) introduces multiple mass-sheet transformations that bias the inferred distance ratios $\beta$ used to constrain cosmology. We present the recovered $\beta$ from lens models of simulated compound lenses and explore the systematic biases that arise from a mass-sheet transformation in the first source plane. We examined simulated observations based on SDSS J0946+1006, in which we varied the matter distribution of source 1. Therefore, we must eliminate systems with unreliable lensing information, and for extreme compound lensing, the model complexity must be sufficient to reproduce the lensing observations. DSPLs remain a promising cosmological probe that could provide independent and complementary constraints to existing probes.

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Persistence in NISP detectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16766)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

### [Simulation-Based Priors and BAO Shifts from the Lyman-$\alpha$ Forest at the Field Level](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17299)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present an analytic forward model for the Lyman-alpha (Ly-alpha) forest flux field based on cubic-order large-scale structure perturbation theory (effective field theory) calculations. We validate our cubic model on two sets of hydrodynamic simulations spanning $2 \leq z \leq 4$: the small-volume CAMELS suite, which spans a wide range of cosmological and astrophysical parameters, and the large-volume, high-resolution Sherwood simulations.
