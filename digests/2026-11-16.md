### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Enhancing deep-learning models: the impact of image processing and training data selection for strong-lens detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17420)

Euclid Collaboration, M. Fogliardi (Dipartimento di Fisica e Scienze della Terra, Universit\`a degli Studi di Ferrara, Via Giuseppe Saragat 1 et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17420)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 strong-lens CNN ablation: training-set design, hard negatives, FITS vs JPEG, VIS vs RGB; ensemble finds ~11k grade A/B lenses.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Euclid is expected to discover ~$10^5$ galaxy-galaxy strong gravitational lenses across ~14,000deg$^2$, requiring automated searches with high completeness and low false-positive rates. Despite advances in deep learning, the effects of training-set composition, image representation, and preprocessing on survey-scale detection remain poorly understood. We present an ablation study of the convolutional neural network used in the ECLIPSE strong-lens detection pipeline, evaluating different training configurations on a fixed test set. Using simulated and real Euclid data, we compare VIS-only and pseudo-RGB representations, FITS and JPEG formats, training-set composition, hard-negative selection, and multi-stage training. We also assess ensembling on ~67 million DR1 cutouts. Training-set composition and strategy strongly affect high-Recall performance. Omitting simulation based pre-training reduces high-Recall weighted average Precision by 83.7% for VIS-only and 74.5% for pseudo-RGB models. Negative-sample selection also substantially affects performance, while FITS yields modest gains over JPEG at high Recall (~2-5%). On DR1, the logit-mean ensemble improves average Precision by 28.0% and 39.9% over the individual VIS-only and pseudo-RGB models. Among ~70,000 candidates selected for visual inspection, it recovers 11,256 unique grade A (secure) and B (probable) lenses, compared with 9650 and 10,395 for the individual models. The branches show complementary ranking preferences, particularly for lenses benefiting from near-infrared information. Careful training-set design, realistic negatives, and multi-band ensembling improve survey-scale searches. Our final ensemble achieves ~80% Recall at the operational selection threshold, outperforming ablation models with higher global classification metrics and highlighting the need to optimise performance for realistic survey conditions.[Abridged]

</details>

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). AstroVink-TREX: Automated strong gravitational lens segmentation as input for mass modelling pipelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17894)

Euclid Collaboration, S. H. Vincken (Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, Portsmouth PO1 3FX et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17894)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 strong lenses: ViT+CNN hybrid automatically segments lens, source and contaminant light, feeding mass modelling for every DR1 system.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Strong gravitational lens modelling requires the lens light, lensed source light, and contaminants to be separated in each image before mass modelling codes can fit the system. Within Euclid this separation has so far been done by hand, which does not scale to the more than 15,000 candidates in the Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) catalogue of Data Release 1 (DR1). We present AstroVink-TREX , a neural network that performs this separation automatically and predicts three flux maps, in a standard format directly usable by mass-modelling pipelines. The network uses a hybrid architecture, pairing a vision transformer encoder reused from the lens detection network with a parallel convolutional branch. A custom loss function penalises both flux and structural error, while allowing independent weighting per component. From the network's predictions we also derive the modelling centre, fitting region, conjugate points, and binary maps, providing all the inputs that a modelling pipeline needs to run without manual setup. We evaluate the efficiency of the network on DR1 systems against existing fits and through expert validation. Across approximately 3000 inspected systems, taking 75% agreement between inspectors, just 39 lens models fail because of a segmentation error. Following this validation, the network provides the component separation and modelling setup for every system modelled in DR1. With automated segmentation, lens modelling is no longer limited by manual preprocessing and can now operate at the same scale as lens discovery.

</details>

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1): The MERge processing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17832)

Euclid Collaboration, M. K\"ummel (Universit\"ats-Sternwarte M\"unchen, Fakult\"at f\"ur Physik, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit\"at M\"unchen et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17832)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 MER pipeline: detection, PSF-matched and Sersic photometry, multi-band catalogue merging of VIS/NISP and ground imaging.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The ESA satellite mission \Euclid was launched in July 2023 and started its regular observing mode in February 2024. The data from the \Euclid instruments Visible Camera (VIS) and the Near IR Spectrometer and Photometer (NISP) are complemented by ground-based imaging data in the $u$, $g$ $r$, $i$, and $z$ bands to enable the determination of photometric redshifts and other galaxy properties. This paper describes the MERge processing function (MER PF) that has been developed and run for the Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). The aim of the MER PF is to detect objects in the \Euclid imaging data, measure their properties, and combine them into a single multi-wavelength catalogue. In this paper we illustrate the design of the MER PF and introduce the different photometric methods applied to the data, which are Kron total flux, aperture photometry on Point Spread Functions (PSF) -matched images, template fitting photometry, and S\'ersic fitting photometry. We also present the various morphological measurements and quality flags that improve the scientific content of the output catalogues and better characterize the detected sources. We illustrate the processing and measurements that help to validate the MER PF results, which is an essential step to qualify the output for the public DR1 release. We discuss the global assessment of the photometric homogeneity and provide an internal comparison of our processing results. In addition, we give an extensive overview of the DR1 output data and the various processing scenarios

### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1) : Performance of the redshift measurement and spectroscopic analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17960)

Euclid Collaboration, V. Le Brun (Aix-Marseille Universit\'e, CNRS, CNES et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17960)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 NISP spectroscopic pipeline: redshift and line-flux measurement, 98.5% agreement with DESI redshifts in EDF-N.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The European Space Agency's Euclid mission has issued its first major data release(Data Release 1, DR1), providing a transformative view of the extragalactic sky through deep photometry and near-infrared slitless spectroscopy. This paper presents the Euclid DR1 spectroscopic data set and details the evolution of the spectroscopic processing function (SPE PF) pipeline, which is responsible for the automated analysis of 1-dimensional spectra acquired by the Near-Infrared Spectro-Photometer (NISP). The SPE PF pipeline workflow comprises spectroscopic classification, redshift determination, and the systematic measurement of spectral features. Emission line fluxes are derived using two independent methodologies: direct integration (DI) and Gaussian fitting (GF). For sources exhibiting broad H$\alpha$ profiles (velocity dispersion larger than $1500$ km s$^{-1}$), a dedicated `QSO mode' is implemented to robustly deconvolve nuclear and host galaxy components. The data set was validated by cross-matching Euclid results with external spectroscopic catalogues, specifically Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) DR1, and through an extensive visual inspection campaign of approximately 4000 sources within the Euclid Deep Field North (EDF-N). For high-quality samples selected in the EDF-N, we find a 98.5\% agreement between Euclid and DESI spectroscopic redshifts. This success rate increases to 98.9\% when incorporating pipeline probability and reliability estimators. (abridged)

### [Precise Deep-Learning Reconstruction of Galaxy Cluster Mass Maps and Substructures from Optical Observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18487)

Atulit Srivastava, Weiguang Cui, Daniel de Andres, Guillaume Bonnet et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18487)

**Why:** Deep learning on HST optical imaging reconstructs cluster mass maps and substructure, matching lensing for CLASH; scalable to wide surveys.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Accurately mapping the total mass distribution within galaxy clusters, including dark matter, is fundamental to understanding structure formation and constraining cosmological parameters. Conventional mass-mapping techniques rely predominantly on gravitational lensing and often produce smooth mass distributions based on analytical profiles, potentially missing important internal substructures. We introduce a complementary, simulation-based inference approach to reconstruct detailed galaxy cluster mass maps and resolve their internal substructures directly from optical imaging. We trained deep learning models on realistic hydrodynamical simulations of galaxy clusters to infer their mass distributions from optical observations. We subsequently applied this framework to observations of real galaxy clusters obtained with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) as part of the Cluster Lensing And Supernova survey with Hubble (CLASH) programme. The reconstructed mass maps successfully reproduce the overall shapes and radial mass profiles of the CLASH clusters, showing strong agreement with independent gravitational lensing measurements. Furthermore, the subhalo mass functions derived from the reconstructed maps exhibit power-law behaviour broadly consistent with predictions from numerical simulations. Our results demonstrate that combining optical imaging with deep learning provides a scalable and physically meaningful approach to mapping galaxy cluster mass distributions. This framework opens a new avenue for exploiting upcoming wide-field imaging surveys to systematically investigate dark matter substructures across large cluster samples, complementing traditional gravitational lensing techniques. Moreover, the model is inherently flexible and can incorporate multi-wavelength observations, including X-ray and Sunyaev--Zel'dovich data, to further improve total mass predictions.

</details>

### [Multipoles in the IllustrisTNG simulation galaxies and implications for Strong Gravitational Lens modelling in Time-delay Cosmography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17538)

Abdullah Al Zaif, Suhail Dhawan, Vasily Belokurov, Erin E. Hayes · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17538)

**Why:** Strong lensing: IllustrisTNG shows angular multipoles are common and bias time-delay H0; motivates multipole priors for Euclid/LSST lens modelling.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Time-delay cosmography is a powerful independent probe of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), yet reaching the sub-2$\%$ precision required to resolve the Hubble tension demands a rigorous treatment of mass model systematics. We use realistic massive elliptical galaxies from the IllustrisTNG hydrodynamic simulation suite to characterise higher-order angular mass complexity in the form of multipoles ($m=1,3,4$) and quantify their impact on $H_0$ inference. We find that these multipole perturbations are common and emergent features of galaxy assembly; in 51$\%$ of our total samples ($54\%$ TNG100-1 galaxies and $85\%$ for TNG50-1 galaxies), the addition of multipole terms to a standard elliptical power-law model is preferred by the Bayesian Information Criterion. Ignoring these structures in lens modelling introduces systematic biases in $H_0$: for perturbations of $\sim2\%$, the biases are of the order of 1--2$\%$ for $m=1$, 4--5$\%$ for $m=3$, and 3--6$\%$ for $m=4$ multipole. While the $m=4$ (boxy/discyness) mode induces a predictable directional bias, odd-order modes ($m=1,3$) introduce significant stochastic scatter. We demonstrate a degeneracy where unmodelled internal multipoles can be effectively absorbed by the external shear component. This combined with low signal-to-noise observations can create ``hidden biases'' where formally perfect fits to imaging data ($\chi_\nu^2 \approx 1$) could harbour significant cosmological errors. Our results provide an astrophysical basis for multipole priors and highlight the necessity of moving beyond rigid parametric models to achieve high-accuracy cosmography in the era of \textit{Euclid} and LSST.

</details>

### [Unsupervised learning reveals a small number of galaxy formation pathways in IllustrisTNG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17559)

C. Alvarez-Fernandez, H. Dominguez Sanchez, J. Vega-Ferrero, E. Angeloudi et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17559)

**Why:** UMAP and representation learning: UMAP plus k-means on TNG star formation histories reveals six distinct galaxy formation pathways.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Understanding the main drivers of galaxy formation and the connection between observable properties and evolutionary pathways is a central goal of extragalactic astronomy. Observations provide single-epoch snapshots of galaxies at different stages of their development, whereas cosmological simulations allow us to trace the full formation histories of individual systems within a theoretical framework. Extracting information from these datasets remains challenging. Our aim is to use machine-learning techniques to unveil physical properties from the simulation datasets. We apply two unsupervised ML techniques, dimensionality reduction and clustering, to identify galaxy evolution key parameters. We use UMAP as our dimensionality reduction tool and k-means as our clustering method. We perform dimensionality reduction on the star formation histories (SFHs) of a sample of aproximately 10000 galaxies drawn from the TNG50 and TNG100 simulations. The resulting representation of the SFHs in a two-dimensional UMAP space reveals a clear ordering of galaxy mass assembly histories, separating quenched and star-forming systems, as well as galaxies with significant ex situ stellar mass contributions. Galaxy properties from simulation catalogs that are not used in the dimensionality reduction show correlations across the UMAP plane. Furthermore, using a k-means clustering analysis, we identify six groups of galaxies with distinct evolutionary histories. These groups show systematic differences in stellar mass, morphology, angular momentum, star formation activity, and merger history, and occupy different regions of the SFR-mass and mass-size planes. Our results indicate that the diversity of galaxy formation histories in IllustrisTNG can be organized into a small number of characteristic evolutionary pathways linking galaxy accretion histories and present-day structural and star-forming properties.

</details>
