### [Spectro-Spatial Survey Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21798)

Georgios N. Vassilakis, Miles Cranmer, Vasily Belokurov · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21798)

**Why:** Image fusion/mosaicking: Gaussian-splatting field jointly fit to SDSS images and spectra, predicting withheld HSC images and DESI spectra.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Imaging and spectroscopy constrain complementary information about the same sky but are usually processed separately. We introduce Spectro-Spatial Survey Fusion, a framework that unifies these measurements by fitting a continuous spectral surface-brightness field jointly through each instrument's response. Adapting from Gaussian splatting in computer vision, we represent the field as a sum of adaptive spatial Gaussians, each carrying a spectrum built from a shared, learned dictionary of templates. We demonstrate this on 400 Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) $ugriz$ exposures and 800 fiber spectra across an $84^{\prime}\times84^{\prime}$ region of COSMOS. The joint fit closely reproduces the spatial structure and broad spectral diversity of galaxies, stars, and quasars. For fitted SDSS observations, the summed predicted flux in source-selected pixels agrees with the data to within 2 percent in every band, and 96 percent of fitted spectra agree to within 10 percent in integrated flux. Sparse fiber spectra constrain a dictionary shared by every Gaussian component, so a model spectrum can be queried anywhere in the imaged area, including where no spectrum was fitted. Without refitting, the field predicts withheld SDSS images and spectra, and selected Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) images and Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) spectra, which never enter the fit. The field predicts broad spiral-arm patterns in HSC images of two selected galaxies and DESI continuum shapes for galaxies without a fitted fiber. This framework provides a route to combining surveys so that spatial and spectral information from different observations and instruments can constrain a common representation of the sky.

</details>

### [Cross-correlating the SPHEREx extragalactic background light with CMB lensing I: theoretical predictions and forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21779)

Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah, Zucheng Gao, Tom Chiesura · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21779)

**Why:** SPHEREx science: forecasts for cross-correlating SPHEREx extragalactic background light maps with CMB lensing, including line-detection prospects.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The near-infrared extragalactic background light (EBL), which SPHEREx maps in 102 spectral channels, holds the emission lines of galaxies on top of a much brighter continuum from the integrated galaxy light (IGL) and the intrahalo light (IHL). Cross-correlating these maps with the lensing of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) isolates the emission that traces the matter, unbiased by uncorrelated foregrounds. The lines face two obstacles. Filtering along the line of sight removes the long radial modes that carry the cross-correlation, and the extragalactic continuum, a signal in its own right, is a correlated foreground for the lines. We forecast the detectability of all EBL components, in a common halo-model framework, for the SPHEREx deep fields and all-sky survey with lensing maps at the sensitivity level of Planck and the Simons Observatory (SO). Without filtering, and with all amplitudes fitted jointly, the cross-correlation with Planck lensing detects the IGL at $9\sigma$ in the deep fields, but not the lines. In the all-sky survey, it measures the IGL and IHL amplitudes to $1.0\%$ and $3\%$ and detects the five lines, fitted as one template, at $4.6\sigma$. With SO lensing, these become $0.6\%$, $1.6\%$ and $7.4\sigma$. In these joint fits, a filter removing radial modes longer than about $600$ Mpc costs every component $90\%$ or more of its signal-to-noise ratio, so the lines are reached by fitting the continuum, not by filtering it. These values assume a known spectral shape of the continuum. Calibrating it with the continuum's auto-spectra keeps the lines at $7.3$--$7.4\sigma$; fitting it from the cross-spectrum alone leaves $1.4$--$2.3\sigma$. The line detections vary by a factor of seven to eleven across line models. The cross-correlation is thus a robust probe of the IGL and a viable route to the emission lines, provided the continuum is modeled across the channels.

</details>

### [Cross-correlating the SPHEREx extragalactic background light with CMB lensing II: theoretical predictions confronted with lightcone simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21780)

Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah, Zucheng Gao · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21780)

**Why:** SPHEREx science: simulated SPHEREx intensity lightcones validating halo-model predictions for EBL x CMB lensing cross-spectra.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Cross-correlating intensity maps with the lensing of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) rejects the foregrounds and noise that do not trace the dark matter. In the near infrared, the remaining signal combines the integrated galaxy light (IGL), the intrahalo light (IHL) and several emission lines. Accurate modeling of all three is essential to forecast detectability and interpret measurements. We present simulated SPHEREx intensity maps of all three on a lightcone, painted on halos from the Hidden Valley $N$-body simulation, and the CMB-lensing convergence of the same lightcone, over $100\,{\rm deg}^2$ to $z \approx 6$. We assign each halo its lines and continuum by stellar-population synthesis, in two models whose metallicities are fixed or follow its stellar mass. Halo-model predictions from the simulation's luminosity-halo mass relations and measured clustering, without free parameters, match the auto-spectra of the IGL and lines, and their cross-spectra with lensing, across the band: at $2.2\,\mu$m, to within $5\%$ above $\ell \approx 400$ and $7\%$ above $\ell = 200$, respectively. The agreement requires nonlinear clustering: linear theory describes the cross-spectrum of a line up to $\ell \approx 1000$-$1600$ at $2.2\,\mu$m, but fails for the IGL and the convergence at multipoles of a few hundred. With the noise of a SPHEREx deep field and of Simons Observatory lensing, the unfiltered cross-spectra, each component taken alone, reach signal-to-noise ratios of $9$-$21$ for the lines, $20$ for the IGL and $15$ for the IHL. A filter that removes radial modes longer than about $600$ Mpc keeps $62\%$ of the IGL's signal-to-noise and at most $20\%$ of the lines', with fitted amplitudes within about $1\sigma$ of the predictions before and after filtering. The emission model changes a channel's intensity by up to a factor of three, but the line detectability only by tens of percent.

</details>

### [Improving Early Detection of Scientifically Interesting Radio Sources Using Enhanced Isolation Forest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21674)

Pfunzo Thavhana, Clement Nyirenda, Michelle Lochner · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21674)

**Why:** Anomaly detection: enhanced Isolation Forest variants on BYOL self-supervised embeddings rank interesting MeerKAT radio sources.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The rapid growth of radio astronomical surveys has created a major challenge in identifying scientifically interesting sources from large populations of observed objects. This study investigates whether enhanced Isolation Forest (iForest) methods can improve the early detection and ranking of scientifically interesting radio sources. Four iForest variants, Standard Enhanced, Pooled Gain, Kurtosis-based, and Hypervolume, are evaluated using 1,031 extended radio sources from the MeerKAT Galaxy Cluster Legacy Survey (MGCLS). Each source is represented by a 52-dimensional feature set derived using Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) self-supervised learning and principal component analysis (PCA), while expert scientific-interest ratings are used to define the ground-truth labels. The methods are evaluated using Average Precision (AP), receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (ROC AUC), Precision@10, Precision@50, and cumulative anomaly detection. Pooled Gain achieves the highest Average Precision (AP = 0.201) and the strongest early-stage ranking performance (P@10 = 0.600), identifying six expert-validated anomalies among the ten highest-ranked sources. Standard Enhanced and Kurtosis-based achieve the highest ROC AUC (0.677), while the AUC values of the four variants range from 0.653 to 0.677. These results indicate that feature-aware isolation strategies can improve the prioritization of scientifically interesting sources at the top of the ranked list, although overall discrimination remains comparable across the evaluated variants. The findings support the use of enhanced Isolation Forest methods as an initial ranking mechanism within human-in-the-loop systems for large-scale radio astronomical surveys.

</details>

### [Efficient and Optimal Analysis of Galaxy Surveys on Large Scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21718)

Julius Adolff, Uro\v{s} Seljak · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21718)

**Why:** Large-scale structure: fast field-level likelihood beating power-spectrum analysis on largest scales, with non-parametric angular systematics deprojection for DESI.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

On sufficiently large scales the galaxy overdensity field is approximately Gaussian, so its information can be losslessly compressed into the power spectrum. Standard power spectrum estimators are nevertheless suboptimal on the largest scales, where the survey window induces significant coupling between different modes. Optimal power spectrum analysis typically requires an inverse of the covariance matrix, which can be overly expensive. Here we develop an alternative field level approach, which is significantly faster. We assess its impact on constraining primordial non-Gaussianity of the local type, which is particularly sensitive to these largest scales. Relative to the standard power spectrum analysis, our field level likelihood forecasts a $14\%$ improvement in constraining power. For the DESI DR2 LRG and QSO samples, our approach forecasts $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})=4.1$, compared to $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})=4.8$ from the power spectrum based analysis. The field level approach also enables efficient implementations of additional non-cosmological contributions to the signal. As an example, we present a novel non-parametric method to mitigate angular systematics, enabling efficient deprojection of contaminated modes.

</details>

### [Baryonification V: Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich maps from dark-matter-only lightcones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21733)

Michael Kova\v{c}, Andrina Nicola, Jozef Bucko, Siyu Bian et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21733)

**Why:** Weak lensing: baryonified tSZ maps from N-body lightcones for joint WL+tSZ simulation-based inference targeting Euclid and LSST.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Baryonic feedback is a major theoretical uncertainty for Stage-IV weak lensing (WL) surveys. The thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect traces the hot gas redistributed by feedback, so combining it with WL can calibrate feedback and improve cosmological constraints. Doing so with simulation-based inference requires correlated lensing and Compton-$y$ maps across many cosmologies and feedback scenarios. We present two methods to generate Compton-$y$ maps from dark-matter-only $N$-body light cones using the component-wise baryonification (BFC) model, which produces separate gas and stellar fields and computes the gas pressure from hydrostatic equilibrium. The two methods, painting pressure profiles onto halos or redistributing the signal along the baryonified gas, agree to within a few per cent. We calibrate the model to the fiducial and strongest AGN feedback FLAMINGO simulations by jointly fitting gas mass fractions and thermal pressure profiles, measuring the non-thermal pressure from the simulated gas velocities rather than treating it as a free parameter. The predicted Compton-$y$ profiles match FLAMINGO within halo-to-halo scatter, and when painted onto the FLAMINGO halos we reproduce the tSZ power spectrum of both feedback models to $\sim5\%$ for $500\lesssim\ell\lesssim3000$. Applied to a \emph{CosmoGridV1-like} $N$-body simulation, our maps agree with FLAMINGO within $\sim10\%$ for $300\lesssim\ell\lesssim8000$; the remaining differences come from the few hundred most massive clusters, and removing them from both halo catalogs brings the two into agreement. Our method thus enables accurate and efficient tSZ forward models from $N$-body simulation suites such as CosmoGridV1, an important step towards joint WL and tSZ simulation-based inference for Euclid, LSST, and the Simons Observatory.

</details>

### [Euclid: Quick Data Release (Q1) -- The evolution of the radio-loud AGN fraction-stellar mass relation to $z\sim3$ in the Euclid Deep Field North](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22887)

S. Visser (Leiden Observatory, Leiden University, Einsteinweg 55, 2333 CC Leiden et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22887)

**Why:** Euclid Q1 science: radio-loud AGN incidence versus stellar mass to z~3 combining Euclid Deep Field North with LOFAR.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The incidence of radio-loud active galactic nuclei (RLAGN) and their energetic output via AGN feedback are crucial in understanding the evolution of galaxies and the large-scale structure of the Universe. In the local Universe, the incidence of RLAGN has been shown to correlate strongly with host galaxy properties, particularly stellar mass. Using the large-area survey capabilities provided by Euclid and the LOw Frequency ARray (LOFAR), we present the first study of the RLAGN incidence--stellar mass relation to $z=3.0$. We perform a stringent analysis accounting for cross-matching reliability and stellar-mass incompleteness. In addition, we test the robustness of our results against AGN and host galaxy classification. This yields a sample of $3\,154$ RLAGN in the Euclid Deep Field North, comprising $2\,803$ low-excitation radio galaxies (LERGs). We find that the LERG incidence as a function of stellar mass and radio luminosity follows a tight correlation over more than two orders of magnitude in stellar mass to $z=3.0$. The slope of the power-law relation decreases from $\gamma\approx2.5$ in the local Universe to $\gamma\approx1.0$ at $z=3.0$, indicating a relative increase in low-mass LERGs at earlier epochs. Separating the LERG population into actively star-forming and quiescent galaxies reveals that quiescent LERGs show a much steeper relation between incidence and stellar mass, with $\gamma\approx2.5$ compared to star-forming LERGs that show $\gamma\approx1.5$. However, the relation seems to be independent of whether galaxies are star-forming or quiescent at $z\geq2$, where both populations show relatively flat relations with $\gamma\approx1.0$. Our results suggest an evolutionary picture in which the LERG population transitions from cold-mode, merger-dominated accretion at earlier epochs to a scenario in which systems require a massive hot halo to fuel the AGN in the local Universe.

</details>

### [Testing general relativity with galaxy cluster clustering from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23104)

Eleni Tsaprazi, Federico Marulli, Pier-Stefano Corasaniti, Amandine M. C. Le Brun et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23104)

**Why:** Clustering: redshift-space clustering of 86k DESI Legacy Imaging clusters constrains f sigma_8, with new random-catalogue methods.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Deviations from general relativity on cosmological scales have been proposed as possible explanations for dark energy and dark matter. Galaxy cluster clustering offers an independent probe of gravitational growth, benefiting from theoretically motivated halo-bias constraints and complementing cluster-abundance measurements. We analyse a subsample of the largest publicly available spectroscopic galaxy cluster catalogue to date, constructed from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. Following our selection cuts, the sample comprises 85 818 clusters at $0.1\leq z \leq0.4$ with $M_{\rm 500c}\gtrsim 6\times10^{13}\,M_\odot$. To enable a robust clustering analysis of this sample, we develop and validate methods for constructing random catalogues and calibrating the effective halo bias. We model the redshift-space two-point correlation function monopole and quadrupole over separations of $10$--$150\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ to jointly constrain gravitational growth and Alcock--Paczynski (AP) distortions, fixing the matter power-spectrum shape to that of a fiducial cosmology. We infer the product $f\sigma_8$ of the linear growth rate $f$ and the matter power spectrum normalisation $\sigma_8$, obtaining $f\sigma_8=0.45\pm0.08$ at an effective redshift of $z_{\rm eff}=0.28$, changing to $0.52\pm0.03$ when excluding baryon acoustic oscillation scales with tight AP priors. Both results are consistent with general relativity within the adopted fiducial cosmology.

</details>
