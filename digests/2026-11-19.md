### [Towards modelling redshift uncertainties in the photometric void size function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24642)

Pierre Boccard, Giovanni Verza, Giulia Degni, Nico Schuster et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24642)

**Why:** Cosmology/photo-z: models photometric-redshift scatter in the void size function, tested on AbacusSummit lightcones as groundwork for Euclid and LSST.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present an extension of the theoretical formalism for the Void Size Function (VSF) to incorporate photometric redshift uncertainties, combining the excursion set with the theory of Lagrangian density peaks. Photometric redshift scatter induces anisotropic line-of-sight smoothing of the density field, which modifies the variance of the field and suppresses the abundance of large voids. We model this effect by damping the variance of the smoothed density field in the theoretical model, and we test it on AbacusSummit halo lightcones with true redshifts and with mock photometric redshifts generated with a Gaussian scatter. The predicted damping of the variance agrees with the one measured in the halo distribution to 1% and the suppression of the VSF due to photometric redshift scatter is well reproduced by the modified model, with recalibrated barrier parameters, for sigma_z <= 0.015 and for voids larger than 40 Mpc/h (about 2.5 mean tracer separations). This work is a first step towards modelling the VSF in modern photometric surveys such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time and the Euclid mission.

</details>

### [Searching the Pareto Front: Multi-Objective Retrieval for Heterogeneous Scientific Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23639)

Song Young Oh, Nesar Ramachandra, Bin Xia, Ian Foster et al. · `cs.AI` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23639)

**Why:** Multimodal astronomy embeddings: compares a foundation-model baseline with Pareto-fused per-modality embeddings for galaxy retrieval across spectra and imaging.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In cosmology, surveys span different sky regions and wavelength ranges, resulting in uneven modality coverage across observational datasets. Such heterogeneity can be addressed through either multimodal foundation models (FMs), which learn shared embedding spaces, or independently learned modality embeddings combined via late fusion. We investigate whether the latter provides an adaptive alternative to FM-based scientific retrieval. We evaluate this approach on a galaxy retrieval task spanning diverse modalities--history of star formation, spectra of light emitted, and multi-wavelength observations from two telescopes--comparing an FM baseline with separately trained embeddings fused through multi-objective optimization based on Pareto optimality. We find that the FM baseline achieves strong embedding-space proximity, but our method preserves modality-specific distance structure that allows scientists to inspect which physical signals drive similarity. Our method also achieves improved fidelity in raw-data space: across six measured properties, its nearest neighbor is 8% closer to the query on average than that retrieved by kNN in the FM embedding space (spread 0.128 vs. 0.139). These results suggest that multi-objective retrieval can surface astrophysically coherent candidates that single-objective search alone may overlook.

</details>

### [JEPA for astronomy: multi-survey spectral alignment puts stellar parameters on the leading principal axes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23492)

Xiaosheng Zhao, Rosemary F. G. Wyse, Yuan-Sen Ting, Alexander S. Szalay · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23492)

**Why:** Foundation-model representation learning: shows a JEPA alignment objective beats contrastive loss for multi-survey embeddings (stellar spectra, not imaging).

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Spectra from different surveys carry different systematics and are not calibrated against one another. Multi-survey foundation models for stellar spectra are then built by aligning embeddings of the same star observed across surveys. Most in astronomy follow the recipes established for language and vision, aligning with a contrastive loss that separates every star from the others. However, this does not ensure that the stellar physics is encoded in a form that a simple read-out can recover. Holding the cross-survey architecture fixed and varying only the alignment loss, we measure what an objective encodes from the principal-component geometry of the Gaia BP/RP (XP) spectra and from a linear probe of the stellar parameters. In a properly aligned embedding, the leading two principal components form a plane that recovers an analog of the Kiel diagram, the third component tracks [Fe/H], and [alpha/Fe] varies coherently across the plane, such that the chemically defined Galactic disks and accreted populations occupy distinct loci. We show that legibility, the ease of reading the information out, is highest for a training objective that never pushes stars apart, and instead constrains only the overall distribution of each survey's embeddings (JEPA). JEPA also exceeds the contrastive alternative at the same architecture, and the performance improves further as more surveys (Gaia XP+LAMOST LRS+DESI+APOGEE) are included. Predicted abundances for 8.0 million XP giants reproduce the main [alpha/Fe]-[Fe/H] structure of the Milky Way. These results indicate that the choice of alignment objective, not alignment alone, determines whether stellar labels are linearly recoverable.

</details>
