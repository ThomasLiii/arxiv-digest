## High priority

### [EuclidFM: Foundation Model for Compact Photometric Survey Representation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25080)

Reyhaneh Vojoudi Salmani, Mahsa Berahman, Saeed Ansarifard, Michael M. Fausnaugh et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25080)

**Why:** Euclid Q1 foundation model: masked-autoencoder Transformer learning 128-d source embeddings from heterogeneous photometry, with neighbour and quasar-candidate tests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present EuclidFM, a compact self-supervised model that learns object-level representations from heterogeneous catalogue photometry in Euclid Quick Data Release 1. Using 90,258 sources across three Euclid Deep Fields, a masked-autoencoder Transformer reconstructs withheld fluxes and uncertainties from available measurements, producing a 128-dimensional embedding per source. The embeddings encode morphological and photometric information: sources with high point-like probability have, on average, 12.4 similarly classified neighbours among their 15 nearest neighbours, compared with 4.3 under field- and magnitude-stratified randomization. Linear models trained on frozen embeddings predict VIS magnitudes with correlations of 0.954-0.980 across three held-out fields. For sources with VIS aperture-flux uncertainties up to 1 microJy, incorporating embeddings reduces the root-mean-square error in predicted log uncertainty by at least 24% relative to a magnitude-only model. A classifier trained on spectroscopically confirmed sources identifies 1,150 quasar candidates, but only 21 of the 156 candidates with accepted external counterparts show AGN evidence, including 12 spectroscopic quasars. The classifier's transferability to the wider photometric catalogue therefore remains uncertain, and its purity and completeness require further evaluation. Independently of classification, EuclidFM provides a common representation for downstream survey analyses when photometric coverage differs between sources. Code and data are available at https://github.com/AI-Driven-HEP/EuclidFM.

</details>

### [Star-forming disc galaxies align tangentially with the cosmic tidal field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26593)

Calum Murray, Roman Paviot, Martin Kilbinger, Cail Daley et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26593)

**Why:** SPHEREx spectrophotometry plus Legacy Surveys build a 71M-galaxy catalogue, detecting tidal alignments of blue discs; key weak-lensing systematic.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The alignments of galaxy shapes, with one another and with the large-scale structure of the Universe, have been a topic of considerable investigation within cosmology and extragalactic astrophysics for the last 60 years. Galaxy alignments are a major systematic for weak gravitational lensing and help us learn about the processes of galaxy formation. Red early-type galaxies are known to show strong radial alignments around other galaxies; however, blue star-forming galaxies have shown little evidence of alignment. We combine SPHEREx spectrophotometry with DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys to build a full-sky catalogue of 71 million galaxies with precise redshifts. With this catalogue we are able to measure alignment signals by splitting galaxies into colour and morphological samples. We detect tangential alignment of blue disc galaxies at $22.5\sigma$ and radial alignment of red spheroidal galaxies at $65\sigma$. By measuring the alignment signal separately around overdensities and underdensities, we show that each of these alignments has the scale and environmental dependence of a linear response to the cosmic tidal field; a response purely quadratic in the Gaussian tidal field, as in classic tidal torque theory is ruled out.

</details>

### [Constraints and biases: impact of light-cone effects on power spectrum and bispectrum forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26488)

Chris Addis, Chris Clarkson, Dionysios Karagiannis · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26488)

**Why:** Large-scale clustering forecasts for Euclid-like surveys showing light-cone/lensing effects bias f_NL by ~2 sigma if neglected.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Next-generation galaxy surveys will map large-scale structure over unprecedented volumes, bringing two goals within reach: constraining primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG) and detecting relativistic effects on the largest scales. On these scales, relativistic and wide-separation (light-cone) effects share the scale dependence of local-type PNG, so they must be modelled to avoid biased inferences and to recover the true uncertainties. We present joint power spectrum and bispectrum forecasts that include these effects, with a multi-tracer bispectrum covariance including the odd-parity multipoles and leading non-Gaussian terms. Neglecting light-cone effects biases local-type $f_{\rm NL}$ by $\sim2\sigma$ for a Euclid-like H$\alpha$ survey and $>10\sigma$ for a MegaMapper-like LBG survey, mostly through lensing; wide-separation and local relativistic effects contribute $<1\sigma$. With these corrections included, marginalising over the evolution and magnification biases without informative priors increases the uncertainty on local-type $f_{\rm NL}$ by $\sim60\%$ for a Euclid-like power spectrum and by $\sim50\%$ for a joint power spectrum and bispectrum analysis of the even multipoles. However, the odd-parity multipoles of the single-tracer bispectrum and the multi-tracer power spectrum, which are sensitive to leading-order relativistic corrections, break most of this degeneracy. Non-local shapes are less affected; for these, a bright-faint split of the MegaMapper-like survey improves the joint constraints on the equilateral and orthogonal shapes by a factor of $\sim2$. Finally, local relativistic effects should be detectable at SNR $\sim13$, with a bright-faint split of a DESI-like BGS survey, provided the magnification bias is known to a few per cent, but only marginally, SNR $\lesssim2.5$, for the higher-redshift surveys, and not with the single-tracer bispectrum dipole.

</details>

### [Revealing galaxy assembly bias with luminosity-weighted marked correlations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25093)

Joaquin Armijo, Roberta Vasallo, L. Raul Abramo, Antonio D. Montero-Dorta · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25093)

**Why:** Galaxy clustering: luminosity-weighted marked correlations as a null test for assembly bias on DESI-BGS mocks.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We investigate whether luminosity-weighted marked correlation functions can reveal secondary halo information beyond that encoded by halo mass alone, as expected in scenarios of galaxy assembly bias. We propose a controlled null test in which galaxy luminosities follow the same mean luminosity--halo mass relation and scatter, while their residuals are either (A) uncorrelated with secondary halo properties or (B) correlated with a standardized secondary halo property, $s$. Both models have identical halo occupation, galaxy positions, luminosity function, and unweighted projected two-point correlation function, $w_p(r_p)$, so their distinction is encoded entirely in the luminosity marks. At leading order, the projected marked correlation function difference, $\Delta\mathcal{M}_p(r_p)$, scales as $\rho^2\sigma_{\log L}^2\xi_{ss}(r_p)$, where $\rho$ controls the coupling between the luminosity and secondary-property residuals, $\sigma_{\log L}$ is the scatter in log luminosity at fixed halo mass, and $\xi_{ss}$ describes the spatial correlation of the secondary property. We test this framework using a DESI-BGS mock catalogue over $0.1<z<0.4$. For the injected dependence, we detect a marked-clustering deviation over $0.11<r_p/(h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc})<1.04$, obtaining $\chi^2=30.14$ relative to the null covariance. A one-parameter test rejects the mass-only hypothesis at greater than $5\sigma$ significance ($p=2.13\times10^{-7}$) for $|\rho|>0.63$. At fixed $\sigma_{\log L}=0.23$, correlations as weak as $|\rho|\simeq0.52$ remain detectable, with the results stable under variations in the HOD prescription and modelled galaxy selection effects. These results establish luminosity-weighted marked clustering as a sensitive null test for property-based galaxy assembly bias and secondary halo information hidden from unweighted two-point clustering.

</details>
