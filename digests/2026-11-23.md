### [Information-Criterion-Driven, Non-linear Deprojection of Angular Imaging Systematics for the SPHEREx Galaxy Clustering Sample](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26864)

James R. Cheshire, Sean T. Bruton, Charuhas W. Shiveshwarkar · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26864)

**Why:** SPHEREx methods: automated information-criterion template selection and non-linear deprojection of imaging systematics for the SPHEREx clustering sample.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Imaging systematics that inject false, or suppress true, galaxy clustering signals are of primary concern for current- and next-generation galaxy surveys, such as the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx) in its search for local primordial non-Gaussianity $f_{\rm NL}$. As statistical errors shrink with growing galaxy samples, systematic rather than statistical uncertainties come to dominate the cosmological error budget. For effects whose spatial patterns are known or well-modelled \emph{a priori}, a common mitigation approach regresses (deprojects) the density variation associated with a known angular template. In this work we present and demonstrate novel aspects of this procedure as will be applied to the SPHEREx galaxy clustering sample. These include flexible -- but, crucially, constrained and interpretable -- non-linear basis regression with polynomial/spline bases, and aggressive information criterion-driven automated template selection with an associated unbiased two-stage regression, using agreement between two independent selectors as a consistency diagnostic. The method automatically selects a template set of minimal-but-sufficient complexity while limiting the number of templates actually regressed, minimising cosmological information loss and making modern additive-plus-multiplicative approaches tractable. We demonstrate these features on SPHEREx-like lognormal mock catalogues with injected contamination of various spatial profiles, amplitudes, and levels of complexity. Principled, signal-preserving deprojection of the kind demonstrated here is a crucial step towards achieving SPHEREx's goal of $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}) < 0.5$.

</details>

### [SciPIC-lite: A flexible Halo Occupation Distribution pipeline aimed at clustering observables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26785)

Gabriele Parimbelli, Martin Crocce, Giosu\`e Gambardella, Alexander Eggemeier et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26785)

**Why:** Clustering/LSS: public HOD library validated by fitting Euclid Flagship-2 H-alpha galaxy clustering and building AbacusSummit mocks.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) framework provides a flexible approach to modelling the galaxy--halo connection in cosmological simulations, encoding the statistical relationship between galaxies and their host dark matter haloes. We present SciPIC-lite, a new public Python library for fast, flexible, and user-friendly HOD modelling, designed to populate dark matter haloes extracted from cosmological simulations with galaxies and to provide a comprehensive suite of tools for their subsequent analysis. The library is built around a modular and extensible codebase that incorporates the full breadth of the HOD framework, from the core occupation functions to a wide range of physically motivated extensions, making it suitable for a broad spectrum of galaxy clustering applications. We validate and characterise the performance of SciPIC-lite by developing a pipeline to fit the two-point correlation functions of H$\alpha$-selected galaxies from the Flagship~2 simulation across multiple redshift bins. To this end, we populate AbacusSummit dark matter haloes with galaxies according to a variety of HOD parametrisations. We assess the robustness of the fits with respect to the choice of HOD parametrisation and the inclusion of physically motivated extensions, including assembly bias, velocity bias, conformity, and halo profile rescaling. The best-fit models are found to be remarkably robust across different HOD parametrisations and fitting configurations, with reduced $\chi^2$ values clustering tightly around a common value regardless of the model adopted. We exploit these results to generate new AbacusSummit-based galaxy mocks that accurately reproduce the clustering properties of the Flagship~2 H$\alpha$ galaxy population.

</details>

### [Kinetic Sunyaev Zel'dovich Velocity Reconstruction Using Vision Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26822)

Sophie Coulombe, Matthew C. Johnson · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26822)

**Why:** Vision Transformers applied to CMB and LSS maps for kSZ velocity reconstruction, capturing non-Gaussian information beyond quadratic estimators.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Kinetic Sunyaev Zel'dovich (kSZ) velocity reconstruction uses correlations between maps of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and a tracer of large scale structure (LSS) to infer the large-scale peculiar velocity field. Standard approaches to kSZ velocity reconstruction use quadratic estimators that are Fisher optimal for purely Gaussian fields. Here, we develop a pipeline for kSZ velocity reconstruction based on Vision Transformers (ViT) and show that this approach can improve the fidelity of velocity reconstruction. This is consistent with the network utilizing non-Gaussian information in the inputs that the quadratic estimators do not, though with the single simulation realization we have access to, we cannot exclude overfitting. Our approach can be applied to full-resolution maps from existing and near-term CMB and LSS datasets.

</details>
