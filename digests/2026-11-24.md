### [Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Learning machine learning: Investigating the complementarity and biases of strong gravitational lens finding networks on Euclid data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31231)

Euclid Collaboration, S. H. Vincken (Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, Portsmouth PO1 3FX et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31231)

**Why:** Euclid DR1 ECLIPSE lens search: compares biases and complementarity of five CNN/ViT strong-lens finders over 68 million galaxies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1) strong lens search presents ECLIPSE, the largest catalogue of strong gravitational lens candidates to date, with >15 000 systems identified across 1900 deg2 of sky. It was constructed using five neural networks applied to approximately 68 million galaxies, followed by citizen science and expert visual inspection. In this work, we compare how the independently developed networks use image information to identify their selection of strong lens candidates. The search combines convolutional neural networks and a vision transformer, using the same pool of data but with different image processing and training choices. We investigate why these networks find different systems and what that means for the completeness of our catalogue. We evaluate all networks on a 50 deg2 calibration set and their application to the full DR1 sample. We find that the networks score candidates very differently, and that combining them recovers over 500 lens candidates within our calibration set at an inspection budget of 2000 objects. On the full DR1 sample, we show that individual networks have distinct biases: some networks favour systems with a larger Einstein radius while others are more sensitive to compact configurations. We also investigate network preferences in terms of lens light profile, mass distribution geometry, photometric redshift, and magnitudes. These findings provide a detailed analysis of how neural network selection biases have shaped the Euclid DR1 ECLIPSE catalogue. The results inform how networks should be combined in future Euclid data releases and upcoming wide-field surveys such as LSST or Roman to maximise both completeness and diversity of the recovered lens population.

</details>

### [JAISP: A Joint Rubin-Euclid Foundation Model for Cross-Instrument Source Detection and Astrometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31204)

Shoubaneh Hemmati, George Helou, Yu-Heng Lin, Rachel Akeson et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31204)

**Why:** Self-supervised foundation model jointly encoding Rubin and Euclid ten-band pixels for detection and astrometry; directly hits foundation models and Euclid methods.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Joint pixel-level processing of Rubin and Euclid imaging promises deeper source detection and the cross-survey consistency that precision cosmology requires. We introduce JAISP (Joint AI Survey Processing), a foundation model that learns a single representation of ten-band imaging: Rubin ugrizy and Euclid VIS, Y, J, and H, from the first public releases over $\approx 0.2$ deg$^2$ of the ECDFS deep field. Trained without labels by predicting each band from the other nine, its frozen encoder drives small task-specific networks for source detection and astrometry. The detection network recovers nearly all VIS-selected Euclid Q1 catalog sources on held-out sky, and source injection shows that combining all ten bands extends the point-source detection limit by $\approx 0.3$ mag beyond VIS alone. The astrometry network transfers the sharp VIS localization to the other nine bands, reducing median cross-band positional offsets from $\approx 50$ to $6$--$10$ mas while retaining the full precision of its VIS centroid anchor even for faint sources. Separating this per-source scatter exposes the cross-survey concordance field: a coherent $9$--$10$ mas offset between the two independently Gaia-registered astrometric solutions, dominated by a component common to all bands. Applied without retraining to a second Euclid Deep Field, both networks perform without measurable loss. These gains serve the joint-survey science case: a deeper census recovers faint neighbors whose blending biases weak-lensing shapes and photometric redshifts, and VIS-grade positions in every band supply the common astrometric reference that joint photometry and faint-source proper motions require. A single representation learned once from survey pixels thus serves several measurements across the sky.

</details>

### [Probabilistic Inversion of Strong Gravitational Lenses with Flow Matching Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28983)

Tianao Li, Jimena Gonz\'alez, Xinfeng Xu, Aggelos K. Katsaggelos et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28983)

**Why:** Flow-matching priors for blind posterior inversion of strong lenses on simulated Euclid data, faster and better than diffusion approaches.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Strong gravitational lensing reveals important information about the universe, but reconstructing the source image and lens properties requires solving an ill-posed inverse imaging problem. Generative image models serve as powerful data-driven priors in Bayesian inverse problems. Diffusion models have been used widely, but they are computationally costly. Flow matching has emerged as a promising alternative to diffusion models, with easier training, efficient sampling, and better-quality data generation. In this work, we present Blind-FlowSGS, a principled blind posterior sampling method using flow matching priors, enabling efficient posterior sampling for non-blind and blind inversion of strong gravitational lenses. On simulated Euclid data, we demonstrate our method's improvement over diffusion-based methods in both efficacy and efficiency. Our method shows promising capabilities to accurately process strong lensing data from modern sky surveys at scale. Our code and data are available on our project page.

</details>

### [Curvature Mismatch and Local Errors in Galaxy Property Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29224)

Austin Lutterbach, Angus Hoy, Jasper Chong, Aman Kumar et al. · `cs.LG` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29224)

**Why:** Representation learning on galaxy embeddings: shows linear-probe errors on five pretrained encoders stem from manifold curvature, and corrects them locally.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Linear probes are widely used to evaluate learned representations and infer physical quantities from unlabelled data. The probe's predictions are from an affine function in ambient space restricted to the underlying representation, and consequently, even a representation that has complete information needed to predict a target can produce systematic probe errors when that information is encoded in a form inaccessible to the linear probe. Here we share that some of this probing error is due to the innate geometry of the manifold. Linear probes, when evaluated on a curved manifold, generally have a nonzero intrinsic Hessian (what we colloquially call "probe-facing curvature") given by $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, the manifold's second fundamental form dotted with the probe's normal component. We fit linear probes to galaxy embeddings from five pretrained encoders and intervene on this curvature term to evaluate its contribution to the probe performance. We find that keeping this inherited curvature improves the local fit where it matches the label's curvature. Removing the curvature component of the probe where it does not match the label curvature also provides improvements, and correcting it in every neighbourhood raises held-out local $R^2$ by 0.006 to 0.010 on galaxies, gaining more where the two curvatures disagree. We isolated these gains to be from the underlying geometry and not an automatic guarantee from least squares training.

</details>

### [Redshift-space 3$\times$2pt statistics of galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments at one-loop order](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30596)

Toshiki Kurita, Atsushi Taruya, Teppei Okumura · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30596)

**Why:** One-loop redshift-space models for galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignment 3x2pt spectra, directly relevant to weak lensing and clustering cosmology.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Intrinsic alignments of galaxies, long regarded as a systematic in weak lensing, are now recognized as a cosmological probe complementary to galaxy clustering. Exploiting them with spectroscopic surveys requires accurate models in redshift space beyond the linear regime. In this work, we derive the galaxy density--shape cross-power spectrum and shape auto-power spectra at one-loop order in redshift space. We consider two models, one based on the effective field theory (EFT) and the other on the Taruya--Nishimichi--Saito (TNS) prescription. They share the bias expansion but differ in how they treat small-scale velocities along the line of sight. The EFT model expands the effects of these velocities perturbatively and absorbs their sensitivity to small scales into counterterms. We show that, for the sky-projected shape field, this model requires six renormalized bias parameters and two counterterms. Up to order $k^2$, the stochastic terms require two parameters for the cross spectrum and four parameters for the auto spectra. The TNS-based model keeps the one-point velocity dispersion in an unexpanded damping factor and treats it as a single free parameter. We extend our previous linear-bias TNS-based model of the cross spectrum to nonlinear bias and the shape auto spectra. We find that all loop integrals reduce to those already used for shape spectra in real space and galaxy clustering in redshift space, and we evaluate them with FFTLog. We compare both models with power spectra measured from halos in $N$-body simulations and find good agreement on mildly nonlinear scales. These models provide a basis for joint cosmological analyses of galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments with imaging and spectroscopic galaxy surveys, in which intrinsic alignments are utilized as a cosmological probe.

</details>

### [Revisiting RadioGalaxyNET: modern detectors for radio galaxy detection and host identification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29798)

Argo Saakyan · `cs.CV` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29798)

**Why:** Modern transformer and CNN detectors (D-FINE, RF-DETR, YOLO26) set new state of the art for radio galaxy detection in EMU survey images.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

RadioGalaxyNET is the public benchmark for finding extended radio galaxies in EMU pilot-survey images, classifying them and locating their infrared hosts. Its best published results date from 2023: 54.5 box AP (Gal-YOLOv8) and 48.1 host keypoint AP (Gal-DINO). We train three current detectors, D-FINE-seg, YOLO26-pose and RF-DETR, on the same data and score them with the benchmark's own evaluation code, which reproduces the published Gal-DINO numbers exactly. All exceed the published results. D-FINE-seg M reaches 67.1 box AP and 72.4 host AP, 12.6 and 24.3 points above the best published results, and the highest host AP of the detectors we tested; on boxes it is level with YOLO26. Its host lead over YOLO26 comes from host-centered images, which make up most of the benchmark. End to end, D-FINE-seg S is the fastest model at 1.9 ms per image; engine against engine, YOLO26 is faster at the same size. We also report what matters for anyone using the benchmark: its scoring protocol adds 5 to 10 AP, its host labels sit at the image center, its box labels are offset from the image by 1.5 pixels, and its images can be rebuilt from the public survey data, which recovers sky positions and doubles as extra training data. We suggest that future work report host identification F1, as a measure closer to catalog use: D-FINE-seg M 71.8, YOLO26-pose M 70.5, RF-DETR 63.8, Gal-DINO 59.2.

</details>
