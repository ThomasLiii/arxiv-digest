### [Hesperos & Eosphoros: A Coupled Differentiable Forced-Photometry Operator and Forward Model for SPHEREx Spectrophotometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33092)

James Cheshire (Gemma), Spencer Everett (Gemma), Richard Feder (Gemma), James Bock (Gemma) et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33092)

**Why:** SPHEREx methods: differentiable GPU forced-photometry operator and forward model, ~700x faster than Tractor, enabling systematics response templates.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present Hesperos & Eosphoros, a forced-photometry operator and a forward model that, respectively, perform and model the spectrophotometric measurement of the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx). Implemented as GPU-accelerated, differentiable JAX programs, the two may be directly composed to measure the response of a downstream product (e.g., the galaxy density field) with respect to a modeled systematic or perturbation through the full measurement pipeline. We describe their design and demonstrate the composition on the response of an astrometric perturbation, and demonstrate how these may be used to generate direct measurement/pipeline response templates $\partial({\rm reconstructed\ density})/\partial({\rm systematic\ amplitude})$ for known image-level effects. Hesperos achieves a factor of $\sim700\times$ increased throughput per node-hour at production scale compared to the prior CPU-based Tractor measurement, previously the limiting step in the experiment's data processing, and propagates automatic derivatives through the full production measurement operator. We validate the measurement operator Hesperos against the public SPHEREx fork of The Tractor, and the forward model Eosphoros against the SPHEREx Sky Simulator and the renders themselves independently against GalSim. Taken together, this represents a powerful increase in capability for scaling, iterating, and optimizing galaxy clustering measurement pipelines.

</details>

### [VENUS: A Census of Strong Lensing in a 60-Cluster JWST Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33142)

Joseph F. V. Allingham, Lukas J. Furtak, Miriam Golubchik, Ashish K. Meena et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33142)

**Why:** Strong lensing: largest homogeneous JWST cluster strong-lens model census (57 clusters, 1733 multiple-image systems); catalog-level, not ML detection.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present strong-lensing models for the 57 galaxy clusters observed in Cycle 4 out of the 60 targeted by the JWST Vast Exploration for Nascent, Unexplored Sources (VENUS) program. Models for the remaining three clusters will be incorporated after their observations. The dataset comprises 5013 individual images, corresponding to 1733 multiply-imaged systems, of which 912 have spectroscopic redshift measurements and 227 are newly identified. Using this new wealth of observations, we construct parametric lens models with our $\mathtt{AstroLensPy}$ software, reproducing the observed image positions with an average image-plane precision $\Delta_{\rm RMS}$ of $0.54''$. With a total imaging area of $576$ arcmin$^2$, VENUS offers the largest homogeneous set of cluster strong-lensing models to date. The clusters yield a combined area of $191$ arcmin$^{2}$ magnified by $|\mu|\geq3$ for a source $z_s=10$, for example, establishing the VENUS fields as a premier survey for high-redshift studies. Among our sample, the clusters MACS J0717.5+3745, RXC J0600.1-2007 and PLCK G287.0+32.9 display the largest Einstein radii ($\geq 60''$) and magnified areas ($\mathcal{A}_{z_s=10}^{|\mu|\geq3} \geq 9$ arcmin$^2$) for a source $z_s=10$, and therefore constitute the most potent gravitational telescopes known to date. Finally, leveraging the statistical power of the sample, we derive scaling relations between various lensing observables (Einstein radius, magnified area, projected mass) and the SZ/X-ray-inferred cluster mass, $M_{500,c}$.

</details>

### [Euclid: Discovery of a quasar at z = 8.13 hosting a 360-million-solar-mass black hole](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31385)

J. Wolf, S. Belladitta, D. Yang, F. Guarneri et al. · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31385)

**Why:** Euclid science: Euclid Wide Survey imaging selection yields the first z>8 quasar, a rare-object discovery from survey data.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Quasars powered by rapid accretion onto supermassive black holes (SMBHs) have been uncovered deep into the first gigayear of cosmic history. We present the discovery of EUCL J150452.291+625224.39 (J1504+6252), selected from Euclid Wide Survey imaging and spectroscopically confirmed as the first quasar at z > 8 through observations with Keck, HST, LBT, and JWST. J1504+6252 exhibits the defining spectral signatures of archetypal quasars at lower redshift, including a luminous blue power-law continuum and broad permitted emission lines, indicating a classical broad-line region. JWST/NIRSpec G395M spectroscopy yields a systemic redshift of z = 8.129 +/- 0.005, corresponding to 630 Myr after the Big Bang and shifting the quasar redshift frontier by about 39 Myr (6% of the age of the Universe at that epoch). From H-beta and Mg II, we measured intrinsic broad-line-region FWHM of 3160 +/- 270 and 3740 (+160/-170) km/s, respectively. A widely used single-epoch H-beta calibration gives a reference black hole mass of log10(MBH/Msun) = 8.56 +/- 0.43. Applying a range of calibrations to both Mg II and H-beta yields log10(MBH/Msun) = 8.33-8.89 and Eddington ratios of 0.12-0.44. Assembling a black hole of several hundred million solar masses within the first few hundred million years, despite its currently sub-Eddington accretion rate, requires either a massive seed or sustained, highly efficient early growth. The discovery of a mature, sub-Eddington quasar at z > 8 suggests that substantially more luminous quasars hosting similarly massive black holes may remain to be discovered. Conversely, previously known reionisation-era quasars may represent the rapidly accreting end of a broader population of already massive black holes spanning a wider range of accretion states.

</details>

### [Lensing ratios as a geometric probe of dark energy in the LSST and Simons Observatory era](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32861)

Judit Prat, Carles S\'anchez · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32861)

**Why:** Weak lensing cosmology: galaxy-CMB/galaxy-galaxy lensing ratios as geometric dark-energy probe, forecast for LSST Y10 and Simons Observatory.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Ratios of galaxy-CMB and galaxy-galaxy lensing around the same foreground galaxies probe relative distances extending to recombination without a standard candle or ruler. For sufficiently narrow foreground redshift distributions, and with intrinsic alignments and lens magnification controlled, these lensing ratios (LRs) become geometrical. Here we characterize the geometrical limit and develop an end-to-end optimized framework for the probe with LSST Y10 and Simons Observatory uncertainties. In our forecast for LSST Y10, the individual ratios are measured to a median precision of 1.0%, with a total signal-to-noise ratio of 360. We then study the complementarity of LRs as an additional geometrical probe to constrain dark energy and curvature. We find that LRs improve the $w_0$-$w_a$ figure of merit of the combined CMB, Type Ia supernova, and baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data by 15%, after marginalizing over systematic uncertainties. As an independent geometric probe, they also enable competitive leave-one-out tests, in which one of the probes is dropped, and we find they are most effective at replacing the BAO: adding LRs to CMB+supernova raises the figure of merit from 33% to 66% of that of the full CMB+supernova+BAO combination. We also find LRs are especially valuable in extended model spaces constrained with minimal assumptions: in a seven-bin reconstruction of the dark-energy density, they reduce per-bin uncertainties by up to 16% at $z>1$, and by up to 50% when freeing curvature and dropping the non-geometrical part of the CMB likelihood (i.e. keeping the acoustic angular scale part), where they are able to break the degeneracy between curvature and dark energy.

</details>
