### [Toward Automated Strong-Lens Modeling with Agentic Workflows: An Application to Euclid Q1 Lenses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33363)

Xiaoyue Cao, Ran Li, Yunlong Yin, Rui Li et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33363)

**Why:** Strong lensing plus Euclid: multimodal LLM agents automatically model 185 Euclid Q1 lenses at survey scale.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Ongoing and forthcoming wide-field surveys will discover $O(10^5)$ galaxy-galaxy strong lenses, shifting the main bottleneck from detection to reliable physical modeling. Conventional forward-modeling pipelines still rely on case-by-case expert judgment and manual tuning to mask contaminants, initialize priors, diagnose unphysical fits, and reconfigure failed runs. We present Lens-Orchestra, an automated workflow coupling a team of multimodal large language model agents to multi-stage physical modeling with elliptical power-law mass profiles, external shear, and pixelized source reconstructions. Launched via natural-language instructions, it modeled 185 Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1) grade-A lens candidates with published mass models without human intervention, completing 149 fits. Independent human evaluation confirmed 140 usable models, including 126 grade-I fits with noise-level residuals (with only 6.0% of completed fits unacceptable), and endorsed all 36 halted runs. Across the 140 usable systems, effective Einstein radii match released Q1 models (+0.12% median offset; 2.73% typical scatter), while hierarchical inference on the 126 grade-I imaging-only fits yields a mean mass-density slope of $2.002 \pm 0.009$ (intrinsic scatter $0.074^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$). A dedicated residual-screening agent also guided the selection of two candidate low-mass perturbers for parametric and free-form substructure modeling; although neither was confirmed, this end-to-end search illustrates how automated screening focuses computationally intensive substructure analyses on a few promising systems. Coupling multimodal agents to explicit forward modeling thus enables accurate, survey-scale lens analysis from macro mass profiles to small-scale dark matter structure.

### [Probing Dynamical Dark Energy with Hyper Suprime-Cam Year 3 Cosmic Shear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34650)

Shreya Mukherjee, Surhud More · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34650)

**Why:** Weak lensing: HSC-Y3 cosmic shear with DESI BAO and SNe constrains w0wa; S8 stays robust to the dark-energy model.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recent measurements of baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) and Type Ia Supernovae (SNe) have revealed a preference for a time-evolving dark energy equation of state. Because such dynamical behavior alters both the cosmic expansion history and the late-time growth of structure, weak gravitational lensing measurements can be used to test these extended models. We present an extended analysis of the real-space cosmic shear two-point correlation functions (2PCFs) from the Hyper Suprime-Cam Year 3 (HSC-Y3) survey. Using over 25 million galaxies across 416 deg$^2$, divided into four tomographic redshift bins ($0.3 < z \le 1.5$), we constrain both a constant ($w$CDM) and a time-varying ($w_0w_a$CDM) dark energy equation of state. To break the geometric degeneracies inherent in cosmic shear, we combine the HSC-Y3 data with a CMB-independent suite of background distance probes: DESI DR2 BAO, the recalibrated DES five-year SN sample (DOVEKIE), and Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) priors. In the $w$CDM paradigm, our joint analysis yields $w =-0.936^{+0.074}_{-0.067}$, consistent with a cosmological constant. Within the $w_0w_a$CDM framework, we find $w_0 =-0.832^{+0.071}_{-0.059}$ and $w_a = -1.23^{+0.66}_{-0.44}$, representing a $2.4\sigma$ preference for dynamical dark energy. Although the unconstrained dark energy parameters reduce the cosmic shear-only $S_8$ constraints to smaller values, our joint multi-probe analysis agrees with the baseline HSC-Y3 $\Lambda$CDM structure growth amplitude. We obtain $S_8 = 0.774^{+0.027}_{-0.034}$ for $w$CDM and $S_8 = 0.772^{+0.031}_{-0.030}$ for $w_0w_a$CDM. Our results demonstrate that the weak lensing amplitude is robust to extended dark energy parameterizations, and that the dynamical dark energy signal is currently driven primarily by the geometric distance ladders, a critical consistency test for next-generation Stage-IV cosmological surveys.

</details>

### [UNIONS and Euclid: Calibrating point source-galaxy separation for ground-based surveys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34047)

G. Thomas, S. E. T. Smith, S. Gwyn, A. W. McConnachie et al. · `astro-ph.IM` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34047)

**Why:** Euclid methods: Euclid imaging labels train a neural star-galaxy classifier for ground-based UNIONS survey data.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Efficient separation of unresolved point sources from extended objects (star-galaxy separation) is essential for deep wide-field imaging surveys, particularly at faint magnitudes where morphological differences become subtle. We assess two morphology-based classification methods applied to single-band, ground-based imaging from the Ultraviolet Near-Infrared Optical Northern Survey (UNIONS), using Euclid observations as a high-fidelity reference to evaluate their performance and to train one of the methods. We analysed 8 deg$^2$ of overlapping UNIONS $r$-band and Euclid $I_E$ data, comprising more than $7\times10^{5}$ sources. The Euclid data enable robust morphological labelling down to $I_E\simeq 26$, which we used to train and validate two classification approaches applied to UNIONS alone: a curve-of-growth method and a supervised neural-network classifier, both based on aperture photometry and image-quality information. The curve-of-growth method yields an almost perfectly complete point-source sample ($>95\%$), but its purity decreases rapidly at $r \simeq 22.5$. The neural-network approach significantly improves the purity at the faint end, maintaining $\geq 80\%$ purity down to $r \simeq 24$, while the completeness decreases to almost $50\%$ at similar depths. In both cases, performance strongly depends on the image quality of the ground-based data. This work shows how Euclid data can be used to develop, calibrate, and validate star-galaxy separation methods for ground-based surveys, useful in regions not covered by Euclid. Both methods have already been applied to UNIONS data and are readily applicable to future surveys such as LSST, offering flexible solutions depending on whether completeness or purity is prioritised.

</details>

### [Curvature Mismatch and Local Errors in Galaxy Property Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29224)

Austin Lutterbach, Angus Hoy, Jasper Chong, Aman Kumar et al. · `cs.LG` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29224)

**Why:** Representation learning: manifold curvature explains linear-probe errors on galaxy embeddings from five pretrained encoders.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Linear probes are widely used to evaluate learned representations and infer physical quantities from unlabelled data. The probe's predictions are from an affine function in ambient space restricted to the underlying representation, and consequently, even a representation that has complete information needed to predict a target can produce systematic probe errors when that information is encoded in a form inaccessible to the linear probe. Here we share that some of this probing error is due to the innate geometry of the manifold. Linear probes, when evaluated on a curved manifold, generally have a nonzero intrinsic Hessian (what we colloquially call "probe-facing curvature") given by $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, the manifold's second fundamental form dotted with the probe's normal component. We fit linear probes to galaxy embeddings from five pretrained encoders and intervene on this curvature term to evaluate its contribution to the probe performance. We find that keeping this inherited curvature improves the local fit where it matches the label's curvature. Removing the curvature component of the probe where it does not match the label curvature also provides improvements, and correcting it in every neighbourhood raises held-out local $R^2$ by 0.006 to 0.010 on galaxies, gaining more where the two curvatures disagree. We isolated these gains to be from the underlying geometry and not an automatic guarantee from least squares training.

</details>

### [No Cluster Left Behind I: Joint Mass Calibration for Dark Energy Survey and South Pole Telescope Clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33522)

C. Zhou, A. N. Salcedo, S. Grandis, T. Jeltema et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33522)

**Why:** Cluster cosmology and weak lensing: joint DES richness+lensing and SPT SZ mass calibration that models optical selection bias.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multiwavelength observations of galaxy clusters are among the most promising probes of the growth of structure and dark energy. Optically identified clusters have a high number density and can thus probe the halo mass function well into the group regime, but their weak-lensing mass calibration suffers from a selection bias induced by line-of-sight projection effects. Clusters detected via their intracluster medium (ICM) -- through X-ray emission or the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect (SZe) -- are less affected by such selection biases, but current survey sensitivity restricts ICM-selected samples to high-mass systems. Previous multiwavelength analyses tend to limit to clusters with millimeter detections and thus to the high-mass population. In this paper, we propose a flexible modeling framework in which all clusters from multiwavelength surveys can be combined and modeled, even if they are detected in only one survey. As a pilot study, we jointly model richness and lensing of clusters from the Dark Energy Survey and the SZe signal from the South Pole Telescope. This approach yields precise cluster mass calibration while self-consistently constraining optical selection biases. We have found a $\sim$20% bias in the large-scale lensing signal of clusters selected by richness, and a bias ranging from $\sim-20$% at low richness to zero at high richness for clusters selected by both richness and SZe. Compared to current approaches, this framework is highly scalable and readily extensible, paving the way for the full utilization of multiwavelength cluster data in next-generation surveys.

</details>

### [A Variability Metric Pipeline for AGN studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33435)

Diego Mart\'inez Collipal (UdeC), Swayamtrupta Panda (NOIRLab) · `astro-ph.GA` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33435)

**Why:** Anomaly detection with UMAP: a variability-metric UMAP embedding of 14k ZTF AGN isolates extreme variables and changing-look candidates.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Variability in active galactic nuclei (AGNs) is remarkably diverse, spanning multiple timescales, wavelengths, and physical processes. Variability probes accretion physics and the interplay between supermassive black holes and their surroundings. Building on early case studies, the field has shifted toward population-level analyses of increasingly large datasets, making scalable techniques crucial for upcoming time-domain surveys. We developed a Python-based pipeline to analyze AGN light curves using variability metrics and applied it to 14,061 nearby AGNs ($z\lesssim 0.09$) observed with ZTF. We examined metric distributions and correlations. Amplitude-based metrics (e.g., $F_{\rm var}$) help identify major flares but are sensitive to single deviant epochs. Temporal metrics such as $\eta$ provide a complementary check. Comparison with WISE infrared photometry suggests that $\eta$ traces the strength of AGN activity. Most Type~I and Type~II AGNs in our sample exhibit similar variability patterns based on these metrics. This overlap may partly reflect misclassifications that targeted spectroscopic follow-up could help resolve. To identify subsets of AGNs with distinctive variability, we used UMAP to group sources with similar metric values. The resulting embedding shows a region enriched in high-amplitude variables, including a subset with long-term flux trends. We find evidence that extremely variable quasars and changing-look AGNs may populate this region, which can help prioritize sources for follow-up. Our pipeline offers a scalable, survey-agnostic framework suitable for application to Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST and other ongoing and upcoming photometric surveys.

</details>

### [Small-scale priors for large-scale cosmology: the case of $b_{\phi}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34212)

Mathilde Pinon, Raul E. Angulo, Sergio Contreras, Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34212)

**Why:** Galaxy clustering: priors on the PNG bias b_phi from small-scale clustering using a SHAMe empirical model.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Perturbative descriptions of galaxy clustering introduce free bias parameters that capture the complex physics of galaxy formation. Local primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG), parametrized by $f_{NL}$, induces a scale-dependent contribution to the galaxy bias that is proportional to $f_{NL} b_\phi$, where $b_\phi$ quantifies how galaxy formation responds to PNG. Accurately determining $b_{\phi}$ is therefore essential for robust constraints on $f_{NL}$. Current analyses usually adopt a modified universal relation, $b_\phi = 2\delta_c(b_1-p)$, with $p$ calibrated to simulations. This relies on the accuracy of the simulated galaxy-formation model and ignores the uncertainty on $b_\phi$, potentially biasing $f_{NL}$ constraints. We investigate whether $b_\phi$ can be constrained from small-scale galaxy clustering using a flexible empirical galaxy-formation model. We fit an extended Subhalo Abundance Matching (SHAMe) model to small-scale clustering measurements and combine it with dark-matter-only separate-universe simulations to infer a posterior on $b_\phi$. We validate the method using IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical separate-universe simulations. Without calibration on hydrodynamical simulations, SHAMe predicts $b_\phi$ with a median error of 10%, compared with 38% for a fixed halo occupation distribution (HOD) that neglects the occupation response to $\phi$, and 23% for the universality relation. The residual error is dominated by central galaxies near the selection threshold and persists when using the true stellar-mass assignment conditioned on the same subhalo properties. Small-scale clustering combined with a subhalo-based empirical model predicts most of galaxy-abundance response to local PNG. The remainder depends on galaxy-formation physics and should enter $b_\phi$ priors as a theoretical uncertainty. This provides a route to data-informed priors on bias parameters.

</details>

### [Signals from the early Universe: primordial features from the full-shape analysis of BOSS galaxies and eBOSS quasars in combination with Planck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34672)

Mario Ballardini, Nicola Barbieri, Antonio Raffaelli, Chiara Moretti et al. · `astro-ph.CO` · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34672)

**Why:** LSS: EFT full-shape analysis of BOSS/eBOSS with Planck searching for primordial oscillatory features; null result.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We search for primordial oscillatory features using cosmic microwave background (CMB) and large-scale structure (LSS) data, combining \textit{Planck} Public Release 4 (PR4) with galaxy clustering from the Baryon Oscillation Spectroscopic Survey (BOSS) and quasars from the extended Baryon Oscillation Spectroscopic Survey (eBOSS). For the LSS data we perform a full-shape analysis of the power spectrum multipoles with the one-loop effective field theory of large-scale structure, applying infrared resummation separately to each oscillatory component. We consider four templates: linearly and logarithmically oscillating features in Fourier space, with constant, localised, or power-law-decaying amplitudes, and fit them to the CMB and LSS data separately and jointly. We find no statistically significant evidence for primordial features in either dataset, individually or in combination. The best-fitting LSS features improve the fit by $\Delta\chi^2\simeq-6$ to $-10$, which corresponds to global significances of at most $1.6\,\sigma$ once the look-elsewhere effect is accounted for. In the CMB alone, the most significant feature is reduced to $\sim2.6\,\sigma$. Over the full frequency range, the LSS bounds on the feature amplitudes are weaker than those from \textit{Planck} by factors of $1.4$--$3$. Combining \textit{Planck} with \textit{BOSS+eBOSS} leaves the CMB constraints essentially unchanged, because the LSS data do not favour the frequencies preferred by the CMB.

</details>
